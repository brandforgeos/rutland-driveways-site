Garden lighting in Great Bowden: what is involved

We design and install low-voltage garden lighting: step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around seating, with the ducts laid as the patio is built and the electrics certified.

Ground and conservation in Great Bowden

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. We design for the water first. Garden lighting in Great Bowden: questions

How much does a garden lighting scheme cost in Great Bowden?
A path, step and border lighting scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. Your fixed price comes in writing after the site visit.
Do I need planning permission for a garden lighting scheme in Great Bowden?
Garden lighting does not need planning permission, but a new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, so it is done and certified by a registered electrician. Keep lights pointing down and away from neighbours' windows. Listed buildings and the Great Bowden conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
Does the ground in Great Bowden make a difference?
Cables run in ducts laid below the paving and planting, so we bring the lighting in as the patio or garden is built in Great Bowden wherever we can.
Can you light an existing garden?
Yes. Walls, beds and steps can be lit without lifting paving; lights set into the paving itself usually mean lifting slabs.
Is low voltage bright enough?
Yes. Modern LED fittings give plenty of light, and a few well-placed lights look better than floodlighting.
