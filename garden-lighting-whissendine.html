Garden lighting in Whissendine

Garden lighting in Whissendine

Whissendine has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes, and bigger gardens and longer drives are common. A long garden can feel like it ends at the patio once the sun goes down. Good lighting pulls the far end back into use: a lit path, a lit tree or hedge at the boundary, and soft light around a second seating area. We design low-voltage schemes with step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and gentle seating light. Prices on this page are guides.

Farm conversions often have courtyards, barns and old walls that look superb lit from the ground, while newer homes on the edge of the village usually have open gardens where lighting can be laid out alongside a new patio. Longer drives can be marked with low lights at the edges so they are easy to follow at night. Timers, sensors or app control mean each zone comes on only when it is needed.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine sits on Vale clay, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among those with a few properties at river flood risk. For lighting, we design the water route before anything else: transformers and controls mount high near the house, buried fittings sit on free-draining stone, and in the wettest spots we use wall or spike lights instead. Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction.

A typical Whissendine scheme is for a house on the main street or a farm conversion with a long garden or courtyard. Lights mark the patio steps, low lights run along a path to the far end, a barn or wall is uplit, and the drive has a few low edge lights. The transformer sits on the house wall, well above the ground.

Garden lighting in Whissendine: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Whissendine?
A path, step and border scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. Longer gardens tend towards the multi-zone figure. These are guides; a fixed price comes in writing after we visit.
Does garden lighting need permission in Whissendine?
No. Garden lighting does not need planning permission, and Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, so it is done and certified by a registered electrician. Keep lights pointing down and away from neighbours' windows.
How do you light the far end of a long garden?
We give it something to look at, usually a lit tree, hedge or seating area, and connect it to the house with low path lights. The far zone can sit on its own switch or timer. Cable runs are planned for the distance so the end of the garden is not dimmer than the patio.
Main Street floods sometimes. Where should the controls go?
High and close to the house, above any level you have seen water reach, with no connections left in low ground. Low-voltage fittings cope with wet gardens, but transformers and timers should never sit where floodwater can reach them.
