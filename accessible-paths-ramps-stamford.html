Accessible paths & ramps in Stamford: what is involved

We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, level landings and widened paths so wheelchairs, walkers and pushchairs can get to the door safely, including adaptations funded by a Disabled Facilities Grant.

Stamford's centre is Georgian and older, built of local limestone under Collyweston slate, with more than 600 listed buildings. Victorian streets run out along Rutland Terrace, Empingham Road, Tinwell Road, Ryhall Road and Casterton Road, there is 1920s housing around Melbourne Road and New Cross Road, and new estates are growing on the north side of town.

We cover Stamford (PE9) and the villages around it, including Ryhall, Belmesthorpe, Great Casterton, Little Casterton, Tinwell, Ketton, Barnack, Uffington, Collyweston and Easton on the Hill.

Ground and conservation in Stamford

Stamford sits on Lincolnshire Limestone with well-drained loamy soils, so many sites take a soakaway well. Old cellars, rubble fill and the Welland flood plain change that locally, so we check every site rather than assume. We dig the base below the topsoil in Stamford and lay it on a compacted sub-base, so it stays level.

Stamford was the first conservation area in Britain, designated in 1967, and since December 2018 an Article 4 direction across the whole conservation area removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates and fences, porches and extensions where they face a road, footpath or open space. In practice, most front driveway and boundary work in the conservation area needs planning permission, and the council's appraisal favours York stone, granite setts and natural materials over concrete paving and tarmac.

Sources: Stamford Article 4 direction · Stamford conservation area appraisal.

Accessible paths & ramps in Stamford: questions

How much does a access ramp cost in Stamford?
A permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface typically costs up to about £2,500. Longer ramps, landings and widened paths are priced on the visit. Disabled Facilities Grant work is priced to the council's schedule. Your fixed price comes in writing after the site visit.
Do I need planning permission for a access ramp in Stamford?
A ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Stamford's Article 4 direction brings some work facing a road, footpath or open space under planning control, so we check it against your address first.
Does the ground in Stamford make a difference?
We dig the base below the topsoil in Stamford and lay it on a compacted sub-base, so it stays level.
Can I get a grant for a ramp?
Possibly. A Disabled Facilities Grant from your council can pay for adaptations such as ramps and level access, up to £30,000 in England, after an occupational therapist's assessment. Some councils top that up.
How steep can a ramp be?
As a guide, no steeper than 1:12 for a short ramp and gentler for a longer one, with level landings. We set it out to suit the person using it.
