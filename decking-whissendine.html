Composite & timber decking in Whissendine

Composite & timber decking in Whissendine

Whissendine, on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border, has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes, with its working windmill as the landmark everyone knows. Bigger gardens are common, and on a large plot the cost per square metre matters more, which is why softwood decking is still a popular choice here alongside composite. Many of these plots have space for a deck that would not fit in a town garden.

Every deck we build has posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the joist spacing the boards need, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath to keep the frame dry. Farm conversions often suit a broad, simple timber deck that weathers to silver, while newer homes lean towards composite with a picture-frame edge. Farmhouse kitchens that open onto a yard or garden often make the obvious starting point, and we plan the deck around how you step out of the house and where the sun falls through the day, before worrying about the size.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine sits on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Clay that stays wet softens near the surface, so the supports go down to firm ground, and water from the boards and from the ground beneath needs somewhere to go. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, so on low plots we keep the frame raised and ventilated. With no conservation area, the planning question is mostly about height and area.

A typical Whissendine deck is a broad softwood or composite platform off the back of a farm conversion or newer house on a large plot, often bigger than in town, with a step down to grass. It involves a test dig, supports set on firm ground, membrane and drainage, building the frame and fitting the boards.

Questions

Composite & timber decking in Whissendine: questions

What does a deck cost in Whissendine?
Softwood timber is £110–£140 per m² supplied and fitted and composite £200–£250 per m². On the larger decks common in Whissendine that difference adds up, so we often price both. A 15–20m² composite deck is usually £3,000–£5,000, and raised designs with steps and balustrades come out higher. The fixed figure follows in writing after our visit.
Do I need permission for a deck in Whissendine?
Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction. A rear deck is normally permitted development if no part is more than 30cm high and, with other outbuildings and platforms, it covers no more than half the garden. A raised deck over that height needs planning permission, and a listed farmhouse or cottage needs consent.
Is softwood a good choice for a large deck?
It keeps the cost down on a big area, and pressure-treated softwood suits a farm conversion well. It does need treating every year or two to stay looking good, whereas composite costs more at the start but needs no staining.
Will clay ground move my deck?
Supports sitting in soft, wet clay can settle. We dig to firm ground on the visit and set the posts or pads there, so the frame stays level through wet winters.
