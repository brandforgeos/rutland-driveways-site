Fire pits & sunken seating in Ryhall

Fire pits & sunken seating in Ryhall

Ryhall sits just north of Stamford, but it is in Rutland, and that matters for anyone who has read about Stamford's Article 4 rules. Planning here is handled by Rutland County Council, and Ryhall has no Article 4 direction. For a rear-garden fire pit or sunken seating area, planning is rarely the hurdle; the ground is. We build lined pits set into the patio and sunken areas with retaining walls, benches, steps and drainage. These are guide prices, fixed in writing after the visit.

Stone cottages around the green and the church have the oldest gardens in the village, often established and enclosed. Much of the later estate housing was built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders as Stamford's, with regular rear gardens, a lawn and a basic patio, which suit a fire pit with built-in seating very well. We also cover Belmesthorpe, Essendine, Great Casterton, Little Casterton and Uffington. Each design starts with a test hole.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Ryhall is on the limestone, which generally drains well, and on many plots a soakaway under a sunken seating area is enough. The River Gwash changes that close to its banks, where the ground is wetter, and Rutland's flood strategy names Ryhall among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. We check each site before deciding between a soakaway and channel drainage, and on the wettest plots we may suggest keeping the seating at ground level.

A typical Ryhall job is an estate house with a square rear garden. We extend the patio, sink a seating area slightly below it with a step down, build benches on two sides faced in brick or a stone-coloured material, and set a lined fire pit in the middle. A soakaway or channel drain at the low point keeps the floor clear of standing water.

You can check any address on the Environment Agency flood map.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Ryhall: questions

What does a fire pit cost in Ryhall?
As a guide in Ryhall, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500. A Ryhall sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build. You get a fixed price in writing after we have seen the garden.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction apply in Ryhall?
No. Ryhall is in Rutland, and planning is handled by Rutland County Council, not South Kesteven. Ryhall has a conservation area covering the historic village and no Article 4 direction. A fire pit or sunken seating area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission either way.
Our garden backs towards the Gwash. What changes?
The drainage. Ground near the river is wetter than the limestone elsewhere in the village, so a soakaway may not cope. We test on the visit and may use channel drainage instead, or keep the seating level rather than sunken. We explain the reasons before you commit to anything.
Do you build fire pits in Belmesthorpe too?
Yes. Belmesthorpe sits with Ryhall on the Gwash, and the same approach applies: we check the ground, design the drain or soakaway for what we find, and build the retaining walls on proper footings. Stone finishes suit the older cottages, and brick or render can match newer houses.
