Louvred pergolas in Great Bowden

Louvred pergolas in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes. It has a conservation area and its own neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, with policies on design and character. A pergola in a rear garden is usually out of public view, but choosing a frame that sits comfortably with the house still matters, and the latte and light stone colours often suit older properties better than black.

On planning, a freestanding pergola in the rear garden is normally permitted development within the height, platform and garden coverage limits. In the conservation area it cannot go between the side of the house and the boundary, and a listed house needs permission from Harborough District Council. On the later housing at the edges of the village, the choice of frame colour is more open, and the gardens often have room for a larger frame with closed sides for privacy.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped and affected part of Great Bowden Lane. For a pergola that means designing for water first: the closed roof sends rain down the posts, and on this clay a soakaway needs testing before we rely on it. Thin patios over clay often mean concrete pads.

A typical Great Bowden job is a 3m × 4m latte or light stone pergola over a patio behind an ironstone or brick house near the greens, with LED lighting and open sides, or a larger anthracite frame on later housing at the village edge, with the post water piped to a tested soakaway or drain.

Louvred pergolas in Great Bowden: questions

How much does a pergola cost in Great Bowden?
As a guide, a Great Bowden pergola starts from £4,500 supplied and fitted, for a 3m × 4m louvred aluminium frame on a suitable existing base. Pads, drainage, bigger frames, motorised louvres and side screens are priced after the site visit, and you receive a fixed price in writing.
Does the neighbourhood plan affect a pergola?
Great Bowden's neighbourhood plan has policies on design and character, and road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. A freestanding rear-garden pergola is normally permitted development, but in the conservation area it cannot sit beside the house, and listed houses need permission from Harborough District Council.
Which colour suits a thatched or ironstone cottage?
Latte and light stone tend to sit more quietly beside ironstone, brick and thatch than black or anthracite. We bring samples of all four colours to the visit so you can judge them against your house.
Is my garden near Great Bowden Lane at flood risk?
Harborough's records note the 1998 flood affected part of Great Bowden Lane. We do not assess flood risk, but we do plan the pergola's drainage carefully there, testing the clay before choosing a soakaway, channel or existing drain.
