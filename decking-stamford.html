Composite & timber decking in Stamford

Composite & timber decking in Stamford

Stamford gardens range from walled plots behind Georgian limestone houses in the centre to the Victorian streets along Rutland Terrace, Empingham Road and Tinwell Road, the 1920s houses around Melbourne Road and New Cross Road, and the new estates on the north side of town. A deck can suit all of them, but what you can build without asking first varies more here than anywhere else we work. So before anything else, we check whether your house is listed and how the garden relates to the road.

We build composite and timber decks on a braced frame, with posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the right joist spacing, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath. For a period house a low softwood deck often sits more quietly against old stone than a bright composite, while on the newer estates owners usually choose composite so they never have to stain it. Every board colour is chosen with the surrounding stone in mind.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

Stamford was Britain's first conservation area, and since December 2018 its Article 4 direction removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates, fences, porches and extensions that face a road, footpath or open space. A deck tucked behind the house is judged on the normal rules, but on a corner plot or a garden backing onto a path we check carefully. Underneath, the Lincolnshire Limestone usually drains well, though old cellars, rubble fill and Welland flood plain ground near the river mean we dig before setting any posts.

A typical Stamford deck is a low platform off the back of a Victorian or 1920s house, often replacing worn paving or a shady lawn, kept under 30cm so it stays permitted development. It involves clearing the area, finding firm limestone for the pads, building the frame, and fitting boards in a colour that sits well beside local stone.

Composite & timber decking in Stamford: questions

How much is decking in Stamford?
Our guide figures for Stamford are £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted for composite and £110–£140 per m² for softwood timber decking. Most low rear decks here are modest in size, and anything raised with steps and balustrades costs more. After the site visit we give you a fixed price in writing.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction affect a garden deck?
The direction covers hard surfaces, walls, gates, fences, porches and extensions facing a road, footpath or open space. A rear deck out of sight is normally assessed on the usual rules: under 30cm high and within half the garden with other outbuildings. A listed house needs consent whatever the deck's height.
Could old cellars or fill affect where a deck goes?
They can. Much of Stamford drains well on limestone, but old cellars and rubble fill turn up in older gardens, and they are not firm ground for a post. We dig on the visit and move or extend supports so each one bears on solid material.
I live near the Welland. Is a deck still sensible?
Often it is, but we take more care. The Environment Agency runs a flood warning area for properties near the river, so we keep the frame and membrane clear for water to pass under, and avoid boxing in the space beneath the deck.
