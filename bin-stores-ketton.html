Bin stores in Ketton

Bin stores in Ketton

Ketton gave its name to Ketton stone, and the cream oolitic limestone of the older streets sets the tone for anything new you put in front of a house. A grey concrete pad and a dark plastic bin box look out of place against it. We fit composite slatted stores with aluminium doors for one, two or three bins, and we choose paving for the pad in buff and natural tones that sit comfortably beside the stone.

Around the historic core, the later 20th-century houses and bungalows usually have more room, and a side store with a path to the kerb is often the neatest answer. Wherever it goes, we measure your bins, check the lids open fully inside the store and look at the ground before designing the pad, whether you are in the village itself or nearby in Tinwell, Collyweston or Barrowden. A guide price is given on the visit, and a fixed price follows in writing.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Ketton is on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which usually drains well, so a pad on high ground in the village rarely needs much more than a good sub-base and a fall. Near the River Chater it is a different matter. Ketton has the only Environment Agency flood warning area in Rutland, and ground close to the river can stay wet. We test before building, so a pad near the Chater gets the base and run-off route it needs, and one higher up is not over-engineered.

On one of Ketton's stone cottages, a typical job is a compact one or two bin store tucked to the side or rear, on a pad in buff paving. On the later bungalows and houses around the core, it is usually a two or three bin store beside the house with a short paved path to the pavement.

Sources: Rutland conservation areas and Article 4 list · Rutland flood risk strategy.

Questions

Bin stores in Ketton: questions

How much is a bin store in Ketton?
A single wheelie bin store fitted on a level paved base starts from about £1,000 as a guide. Two and three bin stores, and any new path to the kerb, are priced on the visit. Natural-toned paving and ground near the river can affect the work, so we confirm a fixed price in writing.
Does Ketton's conservation area affect a bin store?
Ketton has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings, with the usual conservation area limits. A store at the side or back is normally permitted development. In front of the house a free-standing store technically falls outside it, so we check with Rutland County Council first.
What colour should the pad be next to Ketton stone?
Buff and natural tones rather than grey. Ketton stone is a warm cream, and a grey concrete pad tends to look harsh beside it. We bring paving options on the visit so you can see them against your walls before choosing.
We live near the Chater. Is that a problem for a bin store?
Not usually, but the ground needs checking. Land close to the river can stay wet, and Ketton has Rutland's only Environment Agency flood warning area. We test the ground and build the pad base and fall to suit, so the store stays level through the winter.
