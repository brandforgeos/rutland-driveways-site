Accessible paths & ramps in Uppingham

Accessible paths & ramps in Uppingham

Uppingham is built on a hill, and plenty of its houses sit above or below the pavement. That makes level access harder here than in a flat town, because a gentle gradient needs length, and length is exactly what a tight frontage or a rear courtyard does not have. We build permanent concrete and paved ramps, level landings and widened paths, working out on the visit whether a straight run will fit or whether the ramp needs to turn back on itself with a landing in between.

Away from the centre, the Victorian and interwar houses on the roads out of town usually have more room, and the later estates to the north and east tend to have front drives that can carry a ramp alongside the parking. Wherever the house is, the gradient is set to suit the person using it. Where a Disabled Facilities Grant is paying, we follow the occupational therapist's recommendations and price to the council's schedule.

Uppingham specifically

What makes Uppingham different

Uppingham stands on Northampton Sand ironstone. It usually drains better than the clay around Oakham, but it varies from one end of a street to the other, and on a hill the water is always moving. A ramp that runs across a slope can act like a gutter, carrying rain straight to the door or into a neighbour's garden. We set falls so water leaves the ramp at the side, add edge drainage where the slope pushes water at the house, and dig to check the ground rather than trusting the house next door.

A typical Uppingham job is a terraced or older stone house where the door is a step or two above a narrow frontage or courtyard. The ramp often has to turn, with a level landing at the turn and another at the door, and handrails on both sides. Houses on the outer roads and estates more often get a straight ramp run beside the drive.

Permissions in Uppingham

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Uppingham conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by Rutland County Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Accessible paths & ramps in Uppingham: questions

What does a ramp cost in Uppingham?
Our guide figure is that a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface typically costs up to about £2,500. A turning ramp with extra landings, common on tight Uppingham frontages, is priced on the visit, as is Disabled Facilities Grant work against the council's schedule. Every price is then confirmed in writing.
Will a ramp need planning permission in Uppingham or nearby villages?
Ramps and paths at ground level in a garden do not normally need planning permission; a raised ramp or platform above 30cm can. Uppingham has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction. Lyddington, Seaton, Ayston, Preston, Ridlington and Belton-in-Rutland do have one, where front walls, gates and parking spaces can need permission, so we check first.
My frontage is tiny. Is a ramp still possible?
Usually. Rather than one long straight run, a ramp can double back with a level landing at the turn, keeping the gradient gentle in a smaller footprint. If the front genuinely cannot take one, a rear courtyard or side route sometimes can, and we look at all the doors on the visit.
My house is on a slope. Does that change the ramp?
Yes, in most cases. On Uppingham's hill a ramp can end up carrying water as well as people. We shape the ramp so rain runs off its side rather than down its length to the door, and add edge drainage where the slope sends water towards the house.
