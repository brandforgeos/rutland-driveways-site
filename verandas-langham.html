Glass verandas in Langham

Glass verandas in Langham

Langham sits just north west of Oakham, a large village long known as the home of Ruddles brewery, with ironstone and limestone houses in the centre and later housing around them. Plots are often generous, with room for a full rear garden, which makes a veranda a natural addition: a dry, light space outside the back doors that turns the garden into somewhere to eat and sit for more of the year.

What sets Langham apart from most villages we cover is planning. It has a conservation area with an Article 4 direction, and Rutland's Article 4 directions can cover front walls, gates and fences, parking spaces, porches and roofs. A rear veranda that does not face a road may well sit outside it, but we check the Langham direction against your house before designing, rather than finding out later. The design then follows the answer.

Langham specifically

What makes Langham different

Langham is on the edge of the Vale of Catmose clay, where water drains slowly, and Rutland's flood strategy notes that the Langham Brook has a history of exceeding its bank capacity. A veranda roof collects rain over its whole area and delivers it to one downpipe. On clay, a soakaway has to be sized and tested, not guessed, and on plots near the brook it may not be the right answer at all. Post footings may also need to go deeper to reach firm ground.

A typical Langham veranda is a 4m or 6m wide frame across the back of a later house or a stone house in the centre, over a patio leading to a long rear garden. It involves an Article 4 check, footings dug through the clay, a wall plate and flashing, a glass roof and a tested route for the downpipe.

Questions

Glass verandas in Langham: questions

How much does a veranda cost in Langham?
As a guide, £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda, or £15,000–£22,000 for the 6m × 3m size that larger Langham gardens can take. Screens, heaters and lighting cost extra. Drainage work for the clay is included in the written fixed price after the visit.
Does the Langham Article 4 direction mean I need planning permission?
Not necessarily. Rutland's Article 4 directions can cover porches and roofs as well as front walls, gates, fences and parking, so we check the Langham direction for your house. Beyond that, a rear veranda is usually permitted development within the rear extension limits if it does not face a road and the house is not listed.
Will a soakaway work in Langham clay?
Sometimes, but slow-draining clay needs a larger soakaway than free-draining ground, and it must be tested. If the test shows the ground will not take the water, we look at connecting to a suitable drain instead. Near the Langham Brook we are especially cautious, given its history.
How big a veranda suits a Langham garden?
Plots here are often generous, so both sizes can work. A 4m × 3m frame covers a table and chairs outside the back doors; a 6m × 3m gives room for dining and seating together. We measure the back wall, doors and windows on the visit and suggest a size that suits the house rather than the biggest that fits.
