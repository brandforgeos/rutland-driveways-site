Louvred pergolas in Ketton

Louvred pergolas in Ketton

Ketton's older streets are built from the cream oolitic limestone that carries the village's name, many under Collyweston slate, and that colour palette shapes how a pergola looks here. Four frame colours are offered: black, anthracite, latte and light stone. Against Ketton stone, the conservation area guidance points to buff and natural tones rather than grey, and latte or light stone usually suits that best. On later bungalows and 20th-century houses, darker frames are also an option.

The pergola is freestanding, built from powder-coated aluminium, with louvres you tilt open for sun and close against rain. The water runs into gutters inside the frame and down the posts. Warm-white LED lighting is built in, and the sides can be left open or closed with louvres, glass or blinds. Rear-garden pergolas are normally permitted development, though in the conservation area one cannot sit between the side of the house and the boundary.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Ketton sits on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which usually drains well, so water from the posts can often go to a soakaway. Near the River Chater, on its way to meet the Welland, it is a different matter, and Rutland's flood strategy names Ketton among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. We test the ground before designing the soakaway, and check the patio or ground at each post position before choosing between fixing down and casting pads.

A typical Ketton pergola is a 3m × 4m frame in latte or light stone over a stone or buff-toned patio behind a village house, with LED lighting for evenings. Bungalows on the later streets often have flatter, more open gardens where a larger frame across the back of the house works well.

Questions

Louvred pergolas in Ketton: questions

How much is a pergola in Ketton?
From £4,500 supplied and fitted for a 3m × 4m louvred aluminium frame on a suitable existing base. Larger sizes, motorised louvres, glass walls, blinds and any new base are priced after the visit, and we confirm a fixed price in writing.
Do I need planning permission in Ketton?
Ketton has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction. A freestanding rear-garden pergola within the permitted development limits normally needs no application, but in the conservation area it cannot go between the side of the house and the boundary. Any listed building in the village needs planning permission from Rutland County Council.
Which colour suits a Ketton stone house?
Latte or light stone usually sits best beside cream Ketton stone, and buff and natural tones suit the village better than grey. We bring samples of all four colours to the visit so you can judge them against your own walls.
My garden runs down towards the Chater. Is that a problem?
Not for the frame, but it matters for drainage. Ground near the river can stay wet, so a soakaway may not suit. We test before deciding where the water from the posts goes, and choose pads or fixings to suit the ground.
