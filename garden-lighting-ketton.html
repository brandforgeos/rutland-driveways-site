Garden lighting in Ketton

Garden lighting in Ketton

Ketton gave its name to Ketton stone, the cream oolitic limestone quarried beside the village, and the older streets are built of it, many under Collyweston slate. Lit well, that stone glows a soft cream; lit badly, with cold white light, it turns grey and flat. That is why warm-toned fittings and low, grazing light are where we start in Ketton. We design low-voltage schemes with step and riser lights, uplighters on walls and trees, and soft light around seating. Prices shown are guides.

Around the historic core are later 20th-century houses and bungalows, often with level, easy gardens where a lit patio, a lit path and one or two lit shrubs make the whole space usable on autumn evenings. Bungalows suit lighting particularly well, because the garden is seen from every room. Where you are rebuilding a patio, we lay the ducts at the same time so cables never sit under slabs and the lights can be extended later.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Most of Ketton is on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which drains well, so buried uplighters in the higher parts of the village usually sit happily in the ground. Near the River Chater, on its way to the Welland, it is a different story. The Welland flood warning area at Ketton is the only Environment Agency flood warning area in Rutland, so close to the river we keep the transformer and connections high and favour wall-mounted fittings. Ketton has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction.

A typical Ketton scheme is for a stone cottage with a walled rear garden or a later bungalow with a level lawn. Two or three warm uplighters graze a stone wall, path lights run to a gate or shed, and the patio gets soft light around the table. Ducts run under the edges of beds rather than across the lawn.

Questions

Garden lighting in Ketton: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Ketton?
A path, step and border scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. These are guide prices; the fixed price comes in writing after we have visited and agreed the layout.
Is permission needed for garden lighting in the Ketton conservation area?
Garden lighting does not need planning permission, and Ketton has no Article 4 direction. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, so it is done and certified by a registered electrician. Lights should point down and away from neighbours' windows, which is also what looks best against the stone.
What colour of light suits Ketton stone?
Warm white. Cream limestone looks its best under warm light, which picks out the buff tones that make the village distinctive. Cool white light tends to make the same wall look grey and harsh. We show you the difference on the visit if you are unsure.
Our garden runs down towards the Chater. Can it still be lit?
Yes, with the design adjusted. On low ground near the river we favour wall and spike lights over fittings set into the ground, keep the transformer and controls high near the house, and seal the ducts. The higher, better-drained part of the garden can take buried uplighters as normal.
