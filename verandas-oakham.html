Glass verandas in Oakham

Glass verandas in Oakham

A veranda puts a dry roof over the patio without darkening the room behind it. The frame is powder-coated aluminium, the roof is toughened glass or polycarbonate, and a gutter built into the front beam carries the rain to a downpipe. Fixed to the back wall of the house with a proper wall plate and flashing, it lets you sit out on a wet evening while the kitchen keeps its daylight. In Oakham we fit them to period terraces, interwar semis and newer estate houses alike.

Most Oakham enquiries come from two directions. On the newer estates at Barleythorpe and Oakham Heights, owners want real use from a garden that has had little more than a patio laid by the builder. Off Brooke Road, Braunston Road and Burley Road, older houses with narrower back gardens want cover close to the house so the space gets used in more than a few summer weeks. Both start with the same visit: we measure, test the ground, then price.

Oakham specifically

What makes Oakham different

Oakham sits in the Vale of Catmose, and the clay around much of the town holds water through the winter. That matters twice for a veranda. The posts stand on footings, so we dig to find firm ground rather than trusting the top layer. And the roof gathers every drop that lands on it into one downpipe. In a town that Rutland County Council's flood strategy lists as a surface water flood risk area of national significance, we decide on the visit whether that water goes to a soakaway or an existing drain, never into a puddle by the back door.

A typical Oakham veranda goes across the back of a semi or an estate house, around 4m wide by 3m deep, over an existing patio outside the kitchen or living room doors. It usually involves new post footings, a wall plate fixed and flashed into the brickwork, a glass roof, and a downpipe connected to a soakaway or drain. Some owners add a side screen on the more exposed end and LED lighting in the frame.

Questions

Glass verandas in Oakham: questions

How much is a veranda in Oakham?
Expect £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda, and £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m. Side screens, lighting and heaters are extra. If the downpipe needs a new soakaway because the clay will not take the water, that goes into the written fixed price we send after the visit.
Do I need planning permission for a veranda in Oakham?
Usually not. A veranda fixed to the house is treated like a single storey rear extension, so it is normally permitted development within the depth and height limits, away from a road and on a house that is not listed. In the conservation area around the castle and Market Place the rules are tighter, but Oakham has no Article 4 direction. We check your address first.
Where does the rain off the veranda roof go?
Into the gutter built into the front beam, then down a downpipe. On Oakham clay we do not let it run onto the patio or lawn, because the ground cannot take it away fast enough. We connect it to an existing surface water drain where that is suitable, or build a soakaway sized after testing the ground.
Can a veranda go over a builder-laid patio at Barleythorpe?
Often, yes, but our posts do not rely on the slabs. We lift the slabs where each post lands, dig a footing into the ground beneath, then relay around it. That way the roof stays true even if the patio itself moves over the years on the clay.
