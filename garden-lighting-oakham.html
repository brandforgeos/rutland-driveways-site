Garden lighting in Oakham

Garden lighting in Oakham

Good garden lighting lets you use an Oakham garden after dark and makes steps and paths safe on a winter evening. We design and install low-voltage schemes: step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around seating. Every scheme is drawn as one plan, so the patio, the borders and the back fence work together rather than as a scatter of separate lights. The prices on this page are guides; after we have walked the garden with you, the fixed price comes in writing.

Oakham gardens come in three broad shapes. Walled gardens behind the limestone and ironstone houses of the old town suit a handful of wall lights and one lit tree. Terraces and semis off Brooke Road, Braunston Road and Burley Road usually have long, narrow plots where path lights lead the eye to a seating area at the far end. On the newer estates at Barleythorpe and Oakham Heights, the best time to plan lighting is when the builder-grade patio is being replaced, because the ducts go in as the new patio is built.

Oakham specifically

What makes Oakham different

Clay is the deciding factor. The Vale of Catmose ground around much of Oakham stays wet through the winter, and Rutland County Council's flood strategy lists the town as a surface water flood risk area of national significance. An uplighter set into wet clay with nothing beneath it sits in a puddle, so we bed buried fittings on free-draining stone and seal the duct ends. The transformer and controls go somewhere dry and reachable, never in a low corner. The conservation area covers the historic centre, but there is no Article 4 direction.

A typical Oakham job is a Victorian or interwar semi with a long rear garden: a patio by the back door, steps down to a lawn, borders either side and a seating area or shed at the end. The scheme usually lights the steps, adds a few spike lights in the borders, washes one wall and puts soft light over the far seating, with cable run in a duct along the side path.

Questions

Garden lighting in Oakham: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Oakham?
A path, step and border lighting scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. Those are guide prices; we confirm a fixed price in writing after the visit, once we know the cable routes and where power can come from.
Do I need permission for garden lighting in Oakham's conservation area?
Garden lighting does not need planning permission, and Oakham has no Article 4 direction. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, so it is done and certified by a registered electrician. In the tightly packed old town it matters most to keep lights pointing down and away from neighbours' windows.
Will lights set into the ground fill with water in Oakham clay?
They will if they are simply pushed into the clay. We dig out beneath each buried fitting and bed it on free-draining stone so water can get away, and the cable runs in a sealed duct. Where the ground is very wet, wall and spike lights are often the better choice.
We are replacing our Barleythorpe patio. Should lighting be planned now?
Yes, this is the best moment. Ducts laid with the patio mean cables never sit under slabs, and lights in steps or walls can be built in rather than added later. Even if you only fit a few lights now, empty ducts leave room to extend the scheme.
