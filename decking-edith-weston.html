Composite & timber decking in Edith Weston

Composite & timber decking in Edith Weston

Edith Weston sits on the south shore of Rutland Water, a stone village with a green, a number of substantial houses, and St George's Barracks next door. Projects here are usually about the view: terraces, outdoor rooms and lighting arranged so you can sit out and look across the water. A deck is often the simplest way to lift a seating area to the level where the view opens up.

We build raised and ground-level decks on braced frames, with posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the joist spacing the boards need, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath. Raised decks get steps, balustrades and, where the view matters most, glass panels that guard the edge without blocking the outlook. LED step lights make the deck usable after dark. We plan the layout from where you will actually sit.

Edith Weston specifically

What makes Edith Weston different

Edith Weston is on the limestone plateau above the reservoir, and most plots drain reasonably well, though clay pockets turn up. A raised deck puts more load on fewer posts, so we test the ground at each support position on the visit. Rutland's flood strategy says Rutland Water itself carries negligible flood risk, and the village is not named for river flooding, so drainage is usually about the ground under the deck rather than flood water. There is no Article 4 direction in the conservation area.

A typical Edith Weston deck is a raised composite platform off the back of a substantial stone house, with glass balustrade panels, wide steps to the garden and lighting set into the treads. It involves testing the ground at each post, the planning application if it goes above 30cm, then the frame, boards and balustrade.

Composite & timber decking in Edith Weston: questions

How much is a raised deck in Edith Weston?
Start from £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted for composite, where a 15–20m² ground-level deck is usually £3,000–£5,000. A raised deck adds longer posts, steps and a balustrade, glass or otherwise, so it costs more than those figures. We give a fixed price in writing once the height and balustrade are agreed.
Will a raised deck for the view need planning permission?
Yes, if any part is more than 30cm above the ground. Lower rear decks are normally permitted development if they cover no more than half the garden with other outbuildings. For a listed house, consent is required at any height. Edith Weston's conservation area has no Article 4 direction.
Will a glass balustrade spoil the view?
Glass panels are chosen precisely so it does not. They guard the edge of a raised deck while letting you see straight through to the water from a seated position.
What about the clay pockets in the village?
Most plots drain reasonably well on the limestone, but clay pockets do appear. We test the ground at each post position, and where we hit clay we go deeper to firm material before setting the support.
