Bin stores in Ryhall

Ryhall's later estate housing was built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders who worked in Stamford, and the frontages share the same problem: a neat drive, a small lawn and nowhere obvious for two or three wheelie bins. We fit composite slatted stores with aluminium doors for one, two or three bins, on a level paved pad with a route to the kerb, so the front of the house looks tidy all week.

Around the green and the church, the stone cottages usually have less room at the front and more character to protect, so a side or rear store is often the better fit. Whichever kind of house you have in Ryhall or Belmesthorpe, we measure your bins, check that each lid opens fully inside the store and give you a guide price on the visit, with a fixed price in writing afterwards.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Ryhall sits on the limestone, which generally drains well, so a pad on most plots needs a good sub-base and a fall rather than special measures. The River Gwash runs through the village, though, and ground near the river is wetter. Rutland's flood strategy names Ryhall among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. We check each site before deciding whether a pad can shed water to a soakaway or needs a channel to take it away.

On a post-war or modern estate house in Ryhall, a typical job is a two or three bin store to the side of the drive, on a paved pad with a short path to the pavement. On a stone cottage near the green, it is usually a compact store at the side or rear.

Bin stores in Ryhall: questions

How much does a bin store cost in Ryhall?
A single wheelie bin store fitted on a level paved base starts from about £1,000 as a guide. Two and three bin stores, and any new path to the kerb, are priced on the visit. Ground near the Gwash can need more work on the base, so we confirm a fixed price in writing after the visit.
Do I need planning permission for a bin store in Ryhall?
Behind or beside the house, a bin store is normally permitted development. A free-standing store in front of the house technically falls outside householder permitted development, so we check with Rutland County Council. Ryhall has a conservation area covering the historic village and no Article 4 direction.
Our house is close to the Gwash. Does that change the pad?
It can. Ground near the river stays wetter than the limestone higher up, so we check it first and may build a deeper base or use a channel to take water away, rather than relying on it soaking in.
We are in Ryhall but planning is with which council?
Although Ryhall is right next to Stamford, it is in Rutland, so Rutland County Council handles planning here, not South Kesteven. Stamford's Article 4 direction does not apply in Ryhall.
