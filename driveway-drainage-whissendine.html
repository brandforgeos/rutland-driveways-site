Driveway drainage in Whissendine: what is involved

We fix puddling and flooding drives with channel drains across the slope, soakaways and crate systems sized for the ground, and corrected falls, so water leaves the drive without running to the road, the garage or the house.

Whissendine, on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border, is known for its working windmill, and has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes. Bigger gardens and longer drives are common.

We cover Whissendine (LE15) and the villages around it, including Langham, Teigh, Ashwell, Pickwell and Stapleford.

Whissendine specifically

Ground and conservation in Whissendine

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Ground drains slowly, so we design the base and the water route before anything else. Clay around Whissendine drains slowly, so a plain soakaway often fills and stays full. We run a percolation test first; where the ground will not take water, we use a lined system or crates with a controlled outlet.

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings.

Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street in Whissendine floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. You can check any address on the Environment Agency flood map.

Driveway drainage in Whissendine: questions

How much does a driveway drainage cost in Whissendine?
A channel drain across a typical driveway or garage entrance costs about £1,000–£1,800 installed, or £120–£200 per metre for a heavy-duty, vehicle-rated channel. Soakaways are sized and priced after a percolation test. Your fixed price comes in writing after the site visit.
Do I need planning permission for a driveway drainage in Whissendine?
Drainage work on your own driveway does not need planning permission, but surface water must not go into the foul sewer or onto the road, and any new connection to a public sewer needs the water company's approval.
Does the ground in Whissendine make a difference?
Clay around Whissendine drains slowly, so a plain soakaway often fills and stays full. We run a percolation test first; where the ground will not take water, we use a lined system or crates with a controlled outlet.
Why does my drive puddle?
Usually the falls are wrong, the base has sunk in places, or the water has nowhere to go at the bottom. We check which before suggesting a channel drain.
Can drive water go into the drains?
Not into the foul sewer. It has to go to a soakaway, a permeable area or, with approval, a surface water sewer.
