Louvred pergolas in Corby

Louvred pergolas in Corby

In a lot of Corby gardens the first question is not which colour, but what the pergola will stand on. Priors Hall Park is planned for more than 5,000 homes, and many of its gardens are handed over as bare, compacted plots. A louvred pergola needs a sound, level base to fix to, so here we often price concrete pads or a full new patio alongside the frame, and design the two together.

The pergola itself is a freestanding powder-coated aluminium frame with an adjustable louvred roof. Opened, it lets in sun and air; closed, it sends rain into gutters inside the frame and down the posts. LED lighting is built in. On estates like Kingswood, Lodge Park, Beanfield and Danesholme, where the patio is older, the job is more often about checking that existing slabs are firm enough to take the fixings.

Corby specifically

What makes Corby different

Corby sits on the Northampton Sand ironstone that fed the steelworks, and large areas around the town were opencast quarried and restored. Within one garden the ground can change from natural ironstone to clay or fill, which affects both the post pads and where the roof water can soak away. We dig before we design. Near the Willow Brook, drainage needs extra care. In the Old Village and Corby's other conservation areas, a pergola cannot sit between the side of the house and the boundary.

A typical Corby job at Priors Hall Park is a new porcelain or slab patio with a 3m × 4m anthracite pergola set on it, often with one side closed for privacy from close neighbours. On the older new town estates it is more usually a frame fixed over an existing patio behind a semi or terrace.

Questions

Louvred pergolas in Corby: questions

How much does a pergola cost in Corby?
From £4,500 supplied and fitted for a 3m × 4m louvred frame on a suitable existing base. If your garden is a bare plot, the pads or new patio are priced after the site visit, along with any bigger frame, motorised louvres or side screens. The fixed price comes in writing.
Do I need planning permission for a pergola in Corby?
Usually not, if it is freestanding in the rear garden, behind the front of the house, within the 2.5m height limits, not on a platform over 30cm, and with outbuildings under half the garden. Listed houses need permission from North Northamptonshire Council, and in Corby's conservation areas it cannot go between the side of the house and the boundary.
My Priors Hall Park garden is just compacted soil. Can a pergola go on it?
Not directly. The posts need a sound, level base, so we either cast concrete pads at each post or lay a new patio for the pergola to stand on. Doing both together means the levels, falls and drainage from the posts are designed as one job.
Does restored quarry ground affect the base?
It can. Restored ground and fill can sit next to natural ironstone or clay in the same garden, and they behave differently under load and in the wet. We dig at the post positions before deciding on pad size and before choosing where the roof water goes.
