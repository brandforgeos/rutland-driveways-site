Fencing in Stamford

Fencing in Stamford

Stamford is the one town we cover where the planning question often comes before the design question. The whole conservation area has been under an Article 4 direction since December 2018, and it covers fences and gates that face a road, footpath or open space. So the first thing we ask is where your boundary sits and what it faces. A rear fence between two back gardens is a very different matter from one alongside a lane.

Outside the conservation area, along the Victorian streets such as Empingham Road, Tinwell Road, Ryhall Road and Casterton Road, the 1920s housing around Melbourne Road and New Cross Road, and the newer estates on the north side, the normal rules apply. There we build closeboard timber on concrete posts with gravel boards, or composite and aluminium slatted fencing on aluminium posts, with matching gates and screens. Listed buildings anywhere in the town need consent first.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

Stamford sits on Lincolnshire Limestone with well-drained soils, which is kind to fence posts. Old cellars and rubble fill turn up behind older properties, though, and gardens near the Welland sit in an Environment Agency flood warning area, so we dig before we promise a post position. In the conservation area, the council's appraisal favours natural materials, so where a boundary faces a road we talk through what is likely to be acceptable before anything is designed.

A typical Stamford job is a Victorian or 1920s house with a long rear garden, where the old panels between neighbours have given up. It means removing the old fence, setting new concrete posts in the limestone, fitting gravel boards and closeboard, and sometimes a slatted screen near the house for a seating area.

Questions

Fencing in Stamford: questions

How much is a new fence in Stamford?
Closeboard runs at £80–£100 per metre supplied and fitted on concrete posts, and composite or aluminium slats from about £240 per metre fitted at 1.8m high. Removing your old fence is priced on the visit. If the Article 4 direction means a planning application is needed, we talk that through before fixing the price.
Does my Stamford fence need planning permission?
Inside the conservation area, a fence facing a road, footpath or open space has lost its permitted development rights under the Article 4 direction, so it generally needs permission. Elsewhere, fences up to 2m, or 1m next to a road or footpath used by vehicles, are normally permitted development. Listed buildings always need consent.
What about a fence between two back gardens?
A rear boundary that does not face a road, footpath or open space is not caught by the Article 4 direction in the same way. The normal permitted development height limits still apply, and listed building consent is still needed if the property is listed.
Will posts go in easily on Stamford limestone?
Usually the limestone takes a post well and drains freely. Older gardens can hide cellar walls or rubble fill, so we dig a trial hole where we are unsure and adjust the post spacing rather than forcing a post into the wrong place.
