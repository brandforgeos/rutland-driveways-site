Garden lighting in Market Harborough

Garden lighting in Market Harborough

We design and install low-voltage garden lighting across Market Harborough, from the historic streets around the Square and the Old Grammar School to the Victorian and Edwardian roads towards Little Bowden and Great Bowden. A scheme typically combines step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around a seating area, all running from one controlled circuit. The prices on this page are guides; once we have seen the garden and agreed the layout, you get a fixed price in writing.

Edwardian and Victorian gardens here are often long and narrow, with mature planting that rewards a few uplighters. The later estates around the edge of town, and the large Airfield Farm development near Lubenham, tend to have open, newer gardens where lighting can be planned from scratch, ideally while the patio and paths are being laid. In both, we aim for a few well-placed lights rather than floodlighting, keeping beams pointed down and away from the houses behind.

Market Harborough specifically

What makes Market Harborough different

Market Harborough sits where the River Welland meets the River Jordan, on Jurassic mudstones and clays that drain slowly. The town centre flooded from surface water in September 2024, when a high Welland stopped the drains discharging, and more than 50 properties flooded in July 2013. For lighting, that means the transformer, timer and any connections stay high on a wall near the house, buried fittings sit on free-draining stone, and ducts are sealed. Where a patio is being rebuilt on permeable lines, ducts go in alongside the drainage.

A typical scheme here is for an Edwardian house towards Little Bowden with a long garden: lights in the patio steps, a row of low path lights to a summerhouse or seating area, and two uplighters on mature trees. On an Airfield Farm plot, it is more often a new patio with lights built into a low wall and ducts laid out to the borders.

Garden lighting in Market Harborough: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Market Harborough?
A path, step and border scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. Any new outdoor circuit is priced separately. These are guide prices; the fixed price comes in writing after we have seen the garden.
Is planning permission needed for garden lighting in Market Harborough?
No, garden lighting does not need planning permission. The conservation area covers the historic core, and the 2020 Article 4 direction at Queen's Street, Little Bowden, covers gates, fences and walls, not lighting. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable and is done and certified by a registered electrician.
Where does the transformer go if our garden sometimes floods?
High and dry. We mount the transformer and controls on a wall near the house, above any level you have seen water reach, and keep connections out of low ground. Low-voltage fittings cope with wet gardens, but the parts that feed them should never sit in standing water.
We have a new house at Airfield Farm. When should lighting go in?
Before the turf and planting, ideally while any patio or path is being built. Ducts laid then can reach every border, and lights in steps or walls are built in rather than added later. If you are not ready for the full scheme, empty ducts keep the option open.
