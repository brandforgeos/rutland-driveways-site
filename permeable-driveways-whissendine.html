Permeable driveways in Whissendine

Permeable driveways in Whissendine

Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street in Whissendine floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. Against that background, a drive that sends its rain straight onto the road makes little sense. A permeable driveway lets the water soak through the surface and base instead, and on Whissendine's clay that usually means a lined system that releases it through a controlled outlet.

Bigger gardens and longer drives are common along the village's long main street, among stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes. More area means more rain, and more cost, so the choice of finish matters. Stabilised gravel is the lowest-priced permeable option, with permeable block paving and resin bound giving a more formal surface. Rutland Driveways & Landscaping designs the base and water route on the visit, then sends a fixed written price.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. The ground drains slowly, so we design the base and the water route before choosing a surface. On the planning side it is simpler than many Rutland villages: Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings. Known for its working windmill, the village sits on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border.

A typical Whissendine job is a long drive serving a farm conversion or a newer house set back from the main street. We strip the old surface, check how the clay holds water, and build an open-graded base that is lined and drained to a controlled outlet where the ground will not take it. Stabilised gravel or permeable block often suits the length and the rural setting.

Questions

Permeable driveways in Whissendine: questions

What does a permeable drive cost in Whissendine?
Going by guide prices, permeable resin bound is £150–£220 per m², permeable block paving £140–£210 per m² and stabilised gravel £60–£100 per m², all in. The Whissendine clay usually calls for a lined system with a controlled outlet, which adds to those figures. On a longer drive we set out the options in a fixed written price.
Do I need planning permission for a drive in Whissendine?
Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings. Under them, no planning permission is needed for a new or replacement front driveway that is permeable, or one whose water drains to a lawn or border on your own land.
What is the lowest-cost permeable option for a long drive?
Of the three finishes we build, stabilised gravel is the lowest-priced, at £60–£100 per m², all in. On Whissendine clay, a lined system with a controlled outlet adds to any of them. Permeable block paving or resin bound cost more but give a more formal finish, so we price the options side by side.
Main Street floods. Will my drive make it worse?
A permeable drive is designed not to add to the problem, because rain falling on it goes into the base rather than straight onto the road. On clay, a lined base with a controlled outlet holds the water and lets it out gradually rather than all at once. It cannot stop Main Street flooding, but it avoids your drive feeding it.
