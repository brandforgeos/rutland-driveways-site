Louvred pergolas in Stamford

Louvred pergolas in Stamford

Stamford is a town where the paperwork comes before the posts. Its Georgian and older centre is limestone under Collyweston slate, it was the first conservation area in Britain, and it has more than 600 listed buildings. If your house is listed, a pergola needs planning permission from South Kesteven District Council. If it is not, a freestanding pergola in the rear garden can normally be permitted development, though in the conservation area it cannot sit between the side of the house and the boundary.

Once that is settled, the pergola itself is straightforward. The frame is powder-coated aluminium, freestanding, with a louvred roof you open for sun and close for rain, which then drains through hidden gutters and down the posts. The colour range includes latte and light stone as well as black and anthracite, and the paler shades often sit more quietly against Stamford limestone. On the Victorian roads and the newer estates to the north, the planning picture is usually simpler.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

Stamford sits on Lincolnshire Limestone with well-drained soils, so water from the posts can often go to a soakaway. That is not guaranteed: old cellars, rubble fill and the Welland flood plain all change things locally, and the Environment Agency runs a flood warning area for properties near the river. We dig where the posts will stand and where the soakaway would go. Rubble fill under an old garden is a common reason to set the frame on concrete pads rather than an existing patio.

On the Victorian streets along Rutland Terrace, Tinwell Road or Casterton Road, a typical job is a 3m × 4m pergola over a stone or slab patio at the back of the house, often in latte or light stone. On the new estates on the north side it is more often a larger anthracite frame over a fresh patio.

Questions

Louvred pergolas in Stamford: questions

How much is a louvred pergola in Stamford?
It starts from £4,500 supplied and fitted for a 3m × 4m frame on a suitable existing base. Bigger frames, motorised louvres, side screens, glass walls and any new base are priced after the site visit. Planning drawings for a listed house are a separate matter we discuss on the visit.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction stop me having a pergola?
Stamford's Article 4 direction removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates and fences, porches and extensions where they face a road, footpath or open space. A listed house needs planning permission for a pergola, and in the conservation area it cannot go between the side of the house and the boundary. We check your address on the visit.
Which frame colour suits a limestone house?
The frames come in black, anthracite, latte and light stone. Against Stamford limestone and Collyweston slate, latte or light stone often blends in, while black or anthracite gives a crisper contrast on newer brick homes. We bring colour samples to the visit so you can see them against your own walls.
My garden is near the Welland. Does that matter?
It affects drainage rather than the frame. Near the river, a soakaway may not work when ground water is high, so we test before choosing between a soakaway, a channel or an existing drain for the water that runs down the posts. The pergola itself is freestanding and fixed to a sound base.
