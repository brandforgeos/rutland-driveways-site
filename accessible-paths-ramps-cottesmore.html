Accessible paths & ramps in Cottesmore

Accessible paths & ramps in Cottesmore

Plenty of Cottesmore homes are already planning a wider drive or a lower-maintenance garden, and when someone in the house needs level access it makes sense to think about both together. A ramp built at the same time as a new drive can share its levels, base and drainage, rather than being squeezed in later. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, landings and widened paths across the village and out to Market Overton, Barrow, Burley and Greetham.

In the older centre, with its ironstone and limestone cottages, doors often open onto a small frontage, and natural-toned materials suit the conservation area. The later housing usually has more room for a straight ramp beside the parking. Either way we set out the gradient and landings around the person who will use the ramp, and where a Disabled Facilities Grant is funding it, the occupational therapist's recommendations and the council's schedule set the brief.

Cottesmore specifically

What makes Cottesmore different

Cottesmore sits on mixed ground on the edge of the limestone, and the North Brook rises nearby. One garden can drain well while the next holds water, which affects how deep a ramp's base goes and where its run-off is sent. We dig a test hole before deciding between a soakaway and a permeable build-up. Because Rutland's flood strategy traces the North Brook through the village, we take particular care on lower plots.

A typical Cottesmore job is a family home with a step at the front door and a drive that is due for widening. The ramp runs from the new drive level to a landing at the door, with handrails where needed, and the path is widened so a wheelchair can get past the cars.

Permissions in Cottesmore

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Cottesmore conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by Rutland County Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Accessible paths & ramps in Cottesmore: questions

What does an access ramp cost in Cottesmore?
Guide price: a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface typically costs up to about £2,500. If you are widening the drive at the same time, both are priced together on the visit, as are longer ramps and landings. Disabled Facilities Grant work is priced to the council's schedule, and you receive the fixed price in writing.
Do I need planning permission for a ramp in Cottesmore?
Generally not. Planning permission is not normally required for a garden ramp or path, but it can be for a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high. The historic village is a conservation area, with no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings. Any application goes to Rutland County Council.
Should we do the ramp and the new drive at the same time?
It usually works better. Building them together means the drive, landing and door levels are set once, the base and drainage are designed as one, and you avoid cutting into a new drive later to fit a ramp. Both are priced on the visit.
Why do you need a test hole for a ramp in Cottesmore?
Because the ground varies from plot to plot on the edge of the limestone. The test hole tells us whether the water will soak away or needs a permeable build-up, and how deep the base should be so the ramp stays level.
