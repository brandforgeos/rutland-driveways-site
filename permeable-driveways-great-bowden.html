Permeable driveways in Great Bowden

Permeable driveways in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes and later housing on its edges. It has its own neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, with policies on design and character, so road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. A permeable driveway can do that while dealing with the rain properly, letting it soak through the surface and an open-graded base rather than run onto the lane.

The difficulty is the ground. Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, so a permeable surface often needs a lined base that releases water through a controlled outlet. We build in resin bound, permeable block paving and stabilised gravel, and decide on the build-up only after testing the ground at your own property. Rutland Driveways & Landscaping sends a fixed written price once that is done.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

The River Welland and the Langton Brook lie to the south of the village, and Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped, affecting part of Great Bowden Lane. That is a good reason to keep drive water under control rather than send it to the road. The conservation area covers the historic village and its greens. Harborough District Council handles planning, with Leicestershire County Council as the highways authority.

A typical Great Bowden job is a brick or ironstone house near one of the greens where an old drive is being replaced with something that looks right in the conservation area. We test the clay, build a lined or unlined open-graded base as the ground dictates, and finish in a natural-toned resin bound, permeable block or stabilised gravel that sits with the houses around it.

Permeable driveways in Great Bowden: questions

What does a permeable drive cost in Great Bowden?
On our guide prices, stabilised gravel is £60–£100 per m², permeable block paving £140–£210 per m² and permeable resin bound £150–£220 per m², all in. Because Great Bowden is on slow-draining clay, a lined system with a controlled outlet is often needed and adds to that. The fixed price comes in writing after the visit.
Do I need planning permission for a permeable drive in Great Bowden?
A front driveway that is permeable, or that drains to a lawn or border on your own land, needs no planning permission. Great Bowden's conservation area and neighbourhood plan mean road-facing changes should respect the surrounding materials, and listed buildings need checking. Planning decisions sit with Harborough District Council.
How does the neighbourhood plan affect my choice of surface?
Its policies on design and character mean road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. Around the greens, with ironstone, brick and thatch nearby, that usually points to natural-toned finishes rather than grey concrete. We look at your house and its setting before suggesting a resin bound, block or gravel finish.
Why is clay a problem for a permeable drive?
Clay and mudstone hold water, so rain passing through a permeable surface can sit in the base instead of soaking away. In Great Bowden we test the ground first, and where it drains slowly we line the base and drain it to a controlled outlet, which manages the water rather than leaving it trapped.
