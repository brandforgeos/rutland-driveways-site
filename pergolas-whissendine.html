Louvred pergolas in Whissendine

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, which makes the planning side simpler than in many villages we cover. A freestanding pergola in the rear garden is normally permitted development if it stays behind the front of the house, within the height limits, not on a platform over 30cm, and with outbuildings covering no more than half the garden. A listed house still needs planning permission.

Bigger gardens and longer drives are common along the village's long main street, so there is usually space to choose the best spot. A louvred pergola can sit against the house over the patio, or further out where the sun lasts longest. The aluminium frame is freestanding, the louvres open for light or close for rain, and the sides can be open or closed with louvres, glass or blinds. Placing it away from the house usually means new pads, which we price on the visit.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Ground drains slowly, so we design the base and the water route before anything else. A louvred roof gathers rain and sends it down the posts, and on clay a soakaway often will not keep up, so we test first. Rutland's flood strategy records Main Street flooding and becoming impassable, and names the village among places with a few properties at river flood risk.

A typical Whissendine job is a 3m × 4m to 6m × 3m pergola over the rear patio of a stone or brick house, or on new pads beside a farm conversion, with the posts' water piped to a tested soakaway or channel and LED lighting for evenings. Closed sides are less common here, where gardens are less overlooked.

Questions

Louvred pergolas in Whissendine: questions

What is the cost of a pergola in Whissendine?
Whissendine pergolas start from £4,500 supplied and fitted, for a 3m × 4m louvred aluminium frame on a suitable existing base. Drainage for the roof water, concrete pads, larger frames, motorised louvres and side screens are priced after the visit, with a fixed price confirmed in writing.
Is planning permission needed in Whissendine?
Whissendine has no conservation area and no Article 4 direction, so a freestanding rear-garden pergola within permitted development limits normally needs no application. That means behind the front of the house, 2.5m at the eaves, 2.5m overall within 2m of a boundary, and no platform over 30cm. A listed Whissendine house needs planning permission from Rutland County Council.
Can a pergola go next to a barn or farm conversion?
It can, as a freestanding frame, provided it stays within permitted development limits and the property is not listed. Together with any outbuildings it must cover no more than half the garden. We check the base and plan the water route on the visit.
Will the clay cause the frame to move?
Clay can move a thinly laid patio, and the frame would follow it. Where the existing slabs are not sound, we cast concrete pads at each post so the pergola stands on its own footings and the louvres stay aligned.
