Questions

Things people ask us

How much does a garden transformation cost?
A full rear garden transformation typically costs £15,000–£35,000 for an average garden, and a refresh, such as a new patio and lawn, £6,000–£12,000. Large or steeply sloping gardens can cost £35,000 and up. Most take two to four weeks on site.
How much does a garden refresh cost compared with a full transformation?
A refresh, such as a new patio and lawn, typically costs £6,000–£12,000, while a full rear garden transformation is usually £15,000–£35,000 for an average garden. The difference is mostly structure: a refresh keeps the existing levels and boundaries, while a full transformation changes levels and adds retaining walls, steps, drainage, fencing, lighting and planting. Large or steeply sloping gardens can cost £35,000 and up.
How much does it cost to landscape a sloping garden?
Large or steeply sloping gardens typically cost £35,000 and up to transform, against £15,000–£35,000 for an average garden. Level changes are the biggest single variable in the price, because every retaining wall needs digging out, footings or concreted posts, drainage behind it and steps through it, and the soil cut out has to go somewhere. A gentle slope handled with one sleeper wall costs far less than a fully terraced hillside.
What is included in the price of a garden transformation?
Our guide prices are all in: clearing the site and taking spoil away by grab lorry, setting levels and drainage, building the patios, paths, walls and steps, then topsoil, turf, planting, lighting and finishing, with a five year workmanship guarantee in writing. The proposal sent after the site visit sets out the build-up, the programme and one whole price, so you can see what each part of the garden involves before agreeing to anything.
What is the cheapest way to transform a garden?
Keep the existing levels and boundaries and spend on the parts you use most, usually a new patio and lawn, which as a refresh typically costs £6,000–£12,000. Real turf is cheaper than artificial grass and softwood decking cheaper than composite. Phasing helps too: build the structure now and add lighting, fencing or planting in a later year, provided the whole garden is planned first so nothing has to be redone.
Does landscaping add value to a house?
A well-built, usable garden generally helps a house sell and supports the asking price, but landscaping rarely returns its full cost on resale, so it is best treated as money spent on enjoying the house. Buyers notice a good-sized patio, a level lawn they can use, secure boundaries and parking. Unfinished or badly drained work can count against a sale, so quality of build matters more than the size of the project.
How are garden transformations paid for?
Payment terms, including any deposit and when the balance falls due, are written into the quote, so you know what is payable and when before you commit. Nothing is due until you have accepted the quote in writing. Bank details are sent with the first invoice after acceptance rather than printed on the quote, and the guide prices on this page are there to help you set a budget, not a bill.
