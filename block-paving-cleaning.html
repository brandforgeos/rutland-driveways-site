Block paving cleaning

What the job involves

A rotary clean at a pressure that lifts dirt without blasting the sand out of the joints or scarring the blocks, then a weed and moss treatment, fresh kiln-dried jointing sand brushed in, and a sealer if you want one to lock the sand and resist stains.

When cleaning is not enough

If blocks have sunk, rocked or spread, the base or the edges have failed and cleaning will not fix it. We will tell you on the visit and price a repair instead.

Questions

Things people ask us

How much does block paving cleaning cost?
Block paving cleaning is priced after we have seen the drive, because the area, the amount of moss and staining, whether the joints need re-sanding and whether you want it sealed all change the job. You get one fixed figure before we start, and if the paving has sunk or the base has failed we will tell you, rather than clean a drive that really needs relaying.
Will pressure washing damage my block paving?
Not at the right pressure with a rotary head. Blasting with a narrow lance is what strips the joints and marks the blocks.
How often should block paving be cleaned and re-sanded?
Every three to five years suits most block paving, or sooner on a shaded drive that greens over quickly. Watch the joints: once the sand has washed out below the surface and weeds or moss take hold, the blocks can start to move, so it is worth re-sanding before that happens. A regular sweep and a spot weed treatment between cleans make a real difference.
Should block paving be sealed after cleaning?
Sealing block paving is optional but worth doing on most drives: a sealer binds the jointing sand so it is not washed or swept out, slows weeds and moss, and makes oil and drink spills easier to clean off. It needs a dry forecast and dry blocks, because sealer on damp paving can turn milky. Most sealers need renewing every few years, usually at the next clean.
What sand should be used to re-sand block paving joints?
Kiln-dried sand is the right sand for re-sanding block paving: it is fine, completely dry and flows deep into narrow joints when brushed in. Building sand or sharp sand is damp and coarse, so it bridges at the top and leaves the joint hollow underneath. The paving must be dry before sanding, and the joints are best topped up again after a few weeks once the sand has settled.
How do you stop weeds and moss growing in block paving?
Weeds and moss grow in the dirt and seed that collects in joints that are not full, so the lasting fix is a thorough clean, a weed and moss treatment, joints filled with kiln-dried sand and, ideally, a sealer to lock the sand in. Regular sweeping helps more than people expect. On a permanently shaded or damp drive some regrowth is normal, and a yearly treatment keeps it down.
Can permeable block paving be cleaned and re-sanded the same way?
Not quite: permeable block paving drains through wider joints filled with clean angular grit, commonly 2–6mm, so it is topped up with the same grit rather than kiln-dried sand, which would clog it. It should not be sealed with a product that closes the joints. A careful clean that lifts silt out of the joints actually helps it drain better.
Can oil stains be removed from block paving?
Fresh oil stains can usually be lifted from block paving with a degreaser and a scrub before the drive is washed, but old oil that has soaked deep into concrete blocks often leaves a shadow that cleaning only lightens. Covering a fresh spill quickly with an absorbent such as cat litter or sawdust helps a lot. A sealed drive resists oil far better, because the spill sits on the surface long enough to wipe away.
What are the white marks on new block paving?
White patches on newish block paving are usually efflorescence, natural salts in the concrete drawn to the surface as the blocks dry out. It is harmless and normally fades on its own over the first year or so as rain washes it away. Pressure washing does not cure it and can draw more salts up, so it is best left, or treated with a dedicated efflorescence cleaner once it has stopped appearing.
Can I clean block paving myself?
Yes, block paving can be cleaned at home with a pressure washer fitted with a rotary patio cleaner head rather than a bare lance, which strips the joints and scars the blocks. Let it dry fully, then brush kiln-dried sand into every joint and repeat until full. The steps people skip are the weed and moss treatment and the re-sanding, and those are what keep the drive looking right for years.
How long does block paving cleaning take?
Cleaning and re-sanding a typical block paved driveway takes one to two days. The wash and the weed and moss treatment come first; the paving then has to dry before kiln-dried sand will flow into the joints, and sealing, if you want it, needs a further dry spell. A wet week can stretch the job, so the work is planned around the forecast.
What affects the price of block paving cleaning?
The price of cleaning block paving depends mainly on the area, how dirty and weedy it is, whether you want it sealed, and how easy the access is for water and waste. Heavily moss-covered or oil-stained drives take longer, and any sunken or rocking areas that need relaying are priced separately. We give one fixed price in writing after seeing the drive.
