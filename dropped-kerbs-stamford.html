Getting a dropped kerb approved in Stamford

  1. Check the basicsLincolnshire County Council replies within 10 working days. When a contractor builds it, the start date has to be 21–90 days ahead. Trees, lamp posts or utilities within 1m can restrict the works, and A, B and C roads need planning permission first.
  2. Planning first if neededYou need planning permission before the kerb application if the house is on a classified (A, B or C) road or is listed, or if the frontage is in the Stamford conservation area under the Article 4 direction.
  3. Apply to the highway authority£300 if lincolnshire county council builds it (the council puts a typical crossing at £2,500–£3,500, tarmac only, 2.75m wide), or £470 if an accredited contractor builds it. Lincolnshire County Council: apply for a dropped kerb
  4. Build the crossingBuilt by the county council itself, or an accredited contractor with £10m public liability insurance and NRSWA-qualified operatives, which we can do for you. We hold the NRSWA street works qualification and can build the crossing for you, then build the driveway behind it so the levels line up.
  5. Build the drivewayThe drive behind the kerb is built to drain to your own ground, so no water runs onto the road.

Stamford specifically

Ground, drainage and conservation in Stamford

Stamford sits on Lincolnshire Limestone with well-drained loamy soils, so many sites take a soakaway well. Old cellars, rubble fill and the Welland flood plain change that locally, so we check every site rather than assume.

Stamford was the first conservation area in Britain, designated in 1967, and since December 2018 an Article 4 direction across the whole conservation area removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates and fences, porches and extensions where they face a road, footpath or open space. In practice, most front driveway and boundary work in the conservation area needs planning permission, and the council's appraisal favours York stone, granite setts and natural materials over concrete paving and tarmac.

Dropped kerbs in Stamford: questions

How much does a dropped kerb cost in Stamford?
Lincolnshire County Council charges £300 if Lincolnshire County Council builds it (the council puts a typical crossing at £2,500–£3,500, tarmac only, 2.75m wide), or £470 if an accredited contractor builds it. The crossing itself is priced after the site visit.
Who builds the dropped kerb in Stamford?
The county council itself, or an accredited contractor with £10m public liability insurance and NRSWA-qualified operatives, which we can do for you. We can build it under the permit.
How long does a dropped kerb take in Stamford?
Lincolnshire County Council replies within 10 working days, and when a contractor builds it the start date has to be 21–90 days ahead.
Do I need planning permission for a dropped kerb?
Only on a classified (A, B or C) road, for a listed building, or where an Article 4 direction applies. Otherwise the highway authority's consent is what you need.
