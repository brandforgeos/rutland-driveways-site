Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for a dropped kerb?
Only on a classified (A, B or C) road, for a listed building, or where an Article 4 direction applies, such as road-facing frontages in the Stamford conservation area. Otherwise the highway authority's consent is what you need.
Can you build the dropped kerb for my driveway?
Yes, in every area we cover. We hold the NRSWA street works qualification, apply under the right permit or licence for your council, and build the driveway behind it so the levels line up.
How much does a dropped kerb cost?
A dropped kerb costs the council fee plus the construction. Fees are £194 for Rutland's permit, £300 or £470 in Stamford, £300 to apply plus a £240 permit in Market Harborough, and £333 in Corby, Kettering and Weldon. Lincolnshire County Council puts a typical crossing it builds at £2,500–£3,500. We price construction after the visit, as length, kerbs and utilities vary.
How long does it take to get a dropped kerb approved?
It depends on the highway authority. Lincolnshire County Council replies within 10 working days and a contractor's start must be 21–90 days ahead; North Northamptonshire usually decides in 28 days, with work 2–3 weeks after the licence; Leicestershire assesses within 30 days and decides within two weeks. Classified roads need planning permission first, which adds weeks.
Can I drive over the kerb without a dropped kerb?
No. Driving across a footway that has no vehicle crossing is an offence, and it damages kerbs, paving and the services underneath. The highway authority can recover the cost of repairs and can require a proper crossing to be built at the owner's expense. Approval and a properly built dropped kerb are the only legal way onto a new drive.
Why would a dropped kerb application be refused?
The usual reasons are a parking space too small, poor visibility, or obstructions. North Northamptonshire wants at least 5m by 3m and 10m from a junction; Leicestershire wants 5.5m deep and rarely accepts slopes steeper than 1 in 20 over the first 5m. Trees, lamp posts or utilities within about 1m can also restrict it. We check these at the site visit.
How wide does a dropped kerb need to be?
Each highway authority sets its own width, usually counted in kerbs. Lincolnshire County Council's standard crossing is 2.75m, while others size it to the drive and how vehicles turn in. Parking space rules sit alongside: North Northamptonshire wants each space at least 3m wide. Ask for the width you need on the application; widening later means a fresh one.
Can I widen an existing dropped kerb?
Yes, by applying to the highway authority in the same way as a new crossing, with the same fee and checks on space, visibility and obstructions. The extra kerbs are lowered to match, and the footway relaid to take vehicle loads. Our Ryhall job replaced an old crossing with a wider dropped kerb alongside a new three-car block drive.
Who applies for a dropped kerb, the homeowner or the contractor?
The property owner applies to the highway authority and pays the fee; tenants need the landlord's agreement first. Where a contractor builds the crossing, the contractor holds the street works permit or licence and must meet the council's insurance and qualification rules. We hold the NRSWA street works qualification and can help you through the application in every area we cover.
How long does it take to build a dropped kerb?
Once approved, most domestic dropped kerbs take one to two days on site: breaking out the footway, lowering the kerbs, building a vehicle-rated base and resurfacing. Concrete-bedded kerbs need a short time before vehicles cross. Longer crossings, or utilities that need protecting, can take longer. We build the drive behind it at the same time so the levels match.
Do I need a dropped kerb for a new driveway?
Yes, if your car will cross a public footway or grass verge to reach the drive. That needs the highway authority's approval and a crossing built to its specification, even when the driveway itself needs no planning permission. If your drive opens onto a private, unadopted road, the highway authority may not be involved; check with the council first.
