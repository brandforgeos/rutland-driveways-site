Glass verandas in Corby: what is involved

A veranda is an aluminium-framed roof fixed to the back wall of the house, with clear glass or polycarbonate panels and a built-in gutter, so you can sit out in the rain and the kitchen still gets its light.

Corby grew as a steel town: a New Town designated in 1950, with estates such as Kingswood, Lodge Park, Beanfield and Danesholme built for steelworkers, around the older villages of Corby Village, Great Oakley and Weldon. Priors Hall Park in the north east is planned for more than 5,000 homes, and many of its gardens are handed over as bare, compacted plots.

Ground and conservation in Corby

Corby sits on the Northampton Sand ironstone that fed the steelworks, and large areas around the town were opencast quarried and restored. Ground can change from natural ironstone to clay or fill within a garden, so we dig before we design the base and drainage. Ground in Corby can change within a single garden, so we dig the first post hole as a trial and set the depth of every footing from what we find.

Corby has nine conservation areas, including the Old Village, Cottingham and Middleton, Gretton, Rockingham, Stanion, Great Oakley and Weldon. Its only Article 4 direction (Kingswood) controls houses in multiple occupation and does not affect driveways, porches or extensions.

How much does a veranda cost in Corby?

Do I need planning permission for a veranda in Corby?
A veranda fixed to the house is treated like a single storey rear extension: usually permitted development if it stays within the extension depth and height limits, does not face a road and is not on a listed house. Open-sided verandas under 30m² are usually exempt from building regulations. Listed buildings and the Corby conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
Does the ground in Corby make a difference?

Do I need planning permission for a veranda?
Usually not if it is at the back of the house and within the rear extension limits. In a conservation area or on a listed house, the rules are tighter and we check first.
Glass or polycarbonate?
Glass looks better, stays clearer and is quieter in rain; polycarbonate is lighter and cheaper. Both keep you dry.
