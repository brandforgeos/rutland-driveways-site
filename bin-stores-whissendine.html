Bin stores in Whissendine

Bin stores in Whissendine

Whissendine's long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes comes with bigger gardens and longer drives than most villages. That gives you room for a bin store, but it also means a longer trip to the kerb on collection day. We fit composite slatted stores with aluminium doors for one, two or three bins on a level paved pad, and we plan a firm, even route out to the road.

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so for most homes the planning side is straightforward. The ground is the harder part, and it shapes most of what we do here. We measure your bins, check every lid opens fully inside the store, dig a test hole to see what the pad will sit on and give a guide price on the visit. A fixed price follows in writing once the base and route are agreed.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Clay drains slowly and moves with the seasons, so a pad laid on it without a proper base will settle and tilt. We dig through to firm ground, build a sub-base and give the pad a fall, then decide where the water goes. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, so on low plots we keep run-off away from the house.

On a farm conversion or a house with a long drive, a typical job is a two or three bin store near the house with a paved path down to the road. On the older stone and brick houses along Main Street, it is often a one or two bin store at the side, on a compact pad.

Questions

Bin stores in Whissendine: questions

What does a bin store cost in Whissendine?
As a guide, a single wheelie bin store fitted on a level paved base starts from about £1,000. Two and three bin stores, and any new path to the kerb, are priced on the visit. With longer drives common in Whissendine, the path often matters as much as the store, and we confirm a fixed price in writing.
Is planning needed for a bin store in Whissendine?
Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings. Beside or behind the house, a store normally counts as permitted development. Out front, a free-standing store technically falls outside householder permitted development, so we raise it with Rutland County Council before building.
Why does clay matter for a bin store pad?
Clay holds water and moves as it wets and dries. A pad laid straight on it tends to settle unevenly, twisting the store so the doors stick. We dig to firm ground and build a proper sub-base, so the pad stays level.
Main Street floods sometimes. Will that affect the store?
We plan for it on low-lying plots. The pad is built up on a firm base with a fall away from the house, and we look at where its run-off goes so it does not add to standing water near the property.
