Composite & timber decking in Ryhall

Composite & timber decking in Ryhall

Ryhall, with Belmesthorpe, sits on the River Gwash just north of Stamford. Around the green and the church are stone cottages, and beyond them later estate housing, much of it built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders as Stamford's estates. Those estate gardens tend to share a pattern: a small slab patio by the back door and a lawn, which a deck can replace with a larger, level sitting area.

We build each deck on a braced frame, with posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the right joist spacing for the boards, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath. Because Ryhall is in Rutland rather than Lincolnshire, planning sits with Rutland County Council, and the conservation area here has no Article 4 direction, unlike Stamford just down the road. Each plot is still checked on its own merits.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Most of Ryhall is on limestone, which usually drains well and gives firm ground for deck supports close to the surface. The Gwash changes that near the river, where ground is wetter. For a garden that runs down towards the water we test before setting any posts, raise the frame clear of damp ground, and leave the space under the deck open so water can pass. On higher estate plots the base is usually more straightforward.

A typical Ryhall deck replaces the small builder's patio behind an estate house with a composite deck sized for outdoor dining, with a step to the lawn. Behind the stone cottages it is more often a smaller softwood deck. The work covers checking the ground, setting supports, building the frame, and laying membrane and boards.

Composite & timber decking in Ryhall: questions

How much does decking cost in Ryhall?
Replacing a small estate patio with a 15–20m² composite deck usually costs £3,000–£5,000. Per square metre, that is £200–£250 supplied and fitted for composite, or £110–£140 for softwood timber. Raised versions with steps and balustrades cost more. After seeing the plot, we confirm the price in writing.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction apply in Ryhall?
No. Ryhall is in Rutland, and its conservation area has no Article 4 direction. Decking behind the house is normally permitted development when it is no higher than 30cm and covers no more than half the garden with other outbuildings. Taller decks go to Rutland County Council for permission, and listed homes need consent.
My garden backs onto the Gwash. Can I have a deck?
Usually, yes. Ground near the river is wetter, so we test it, set the supports on firm material, and keep the frame raised and the space beneath open. We talk through the position on the visit.
Do you cover Belmesthorpe and the nearby villages?
We do. We build decks in Belmesthorpe, Essendine, Great Casterton, Little Casterton and Uffington as well as Ryhall itself, and check the ground and planning position for each address, since the river runs close to some of those villages too.
