Hot tub & swim spa bases

Built to the supplier's requirements

Every hot tub and swim spa maker publishes what its base must carry and how level it has to be, and an out-of-spec base can void the warranty. We build to those requirements and can give the supplier a written method statement.

We plan delivery day too: the crane or wheel route, any fence panels or gates to lift, and where the drain-down water goes.

Questions

Things people ask us

How much does a hot tub or swim spa base cost?
A hot tub base starts from about £2,000 and a swim spa slab typically costs £4,000–£6,500; part-sunk installations start from about £17,500 and fully sunk from about £25,000. A filled swim spa can weigh as much as several cars on a small footprint, so it needs a reinforced concrete slab built to the supplier's published requirements.
Do I need planning permission for a hot tub or swim spa?
Not normally for a hot tub or swim spa on a base in the rear garden. An enclosure or roof over it counts as an outbuilding and has to meet the outbuilding size and height limits.
How thick should a concrete base for a hot tub or swim spa be?
Most hot tub makers ask for a level reinforced concrete slab, commonly around 100–150mm thick, while swim spas usually need a thicker slab, often 150mm or more with steel mesh, on a compacted sub-base. The exact thickness, reinforcement and size come from the supplier's published requirements, because loads vary so much between models. We build to those and can provide a written method statement.
How much does a filled swim spa weigh?
A filled swim spa commonly weighs somewhere between 8 and 15 tonnes once water and bathers are included, and a large hot tub around 1.5 to 3 tonnes, all on a small footprint. That is why paving slabs, gravel and ordinary decking are not suitable, and why the slab has to be reinforced and built on firm, compacted ground. The supplier's dry weight and water capacity give the exact figure.
Can a hot tub go on an existing patio, decking or gravel?
A hot tub can sometimes go on an existing patio if it was built on a concrete slab and is level, but most patios are laid on a sub-base and mortar bed that can crack or settle under the load. Decking has to be structurally designed for the weight, and most manufacturers do not accept gravel. Check the supplier's base requirements first, because the wrong base can void the warranty.
How level does a hot tub base need to be?
A hot tub or swim spa base needs to be flat and level within the tolerance the manufacturer sets, typically just a few millimetres across the footprint, so the shell is supported evenly and the warranty holds. An uneven slab stresses the shell and cabinet and leaves the waterline visibly off. We power float the slab level and check it before the spa arrives.
How long does a hot tub or swim spa base take to build?
A concrete slab for a hot tub or swim spa is usually two to five days on site, then the concrete needs time to cure before the spa is lifted on, commonly at least a week and longer if the supplier asks for it. Part-sunk and fully sunk installations, with excavation and retaining walls, take considerably longer. Book the delivery around the curing time, not the pour date.
Does a hot tub need a dedicated electrical supply?
Many hot tubs, and almost all swim spas, need a dedicated supply, usually a 32 amp or larger circuit with RCD protection from the consumer unit, while some smaller plug-and-play tubs run from a 13 amp outdoor socket. A new circuit is notifiable under Part P, so a registered electrician should install and certify it. We lay ducting in the slab so the cable can be run neatly.
Should a swim spa be sunk into the ground?
Sinking a swim spa, fully or partly, makes it easier to get in and out and helps it sit into the garden, but it is a much bigger job: excavation, retaining walls, drainage at the bottom and access to the equipment bay for servicing. As a guide, part-sunk installations start from about £17,500 and fully sunk from about £25,000, against £4,000–£6,500 for a slab at ground level.
Where should hot tub drain-down water go?
Hot tub and swim spa water contains sanitiser, so it is best drained to the foul sewer, for example a gully that takes household waste water, rather than into surface water drains, a soakaway or a watercourse. Letting chlorine or bromine levels fall before draining helps. We plan the drain-down route when we design the base, along with where rainwater around the slab will go.
Do I need a crane to deliver a swim spa?
Often, yes: swim spas are too long and heavy to wheel through most side passages, so many are lifted over the house or a fence by crane or lorry-mounted crane, while hot tubs usually go through on a trolley if the route is wide enough. Measure gates, paths and corners against the tub on its side. We plan delivery day, including any fence panels or gates that need lifting.
