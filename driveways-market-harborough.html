Harborough homes, Harborough drives

The town built around the old grammar school and The Square has aged well, and so have its houses. The Victorian villas and bay-fronted terraces around the centre and toward Little Bowden are classic frontage conversions: original walls and gate piers to keep, levels to manage, and a finish that has to respect period brickwork. Tumbled block with contrasting borders and warm resin blends both earn their place. Great Bowden's village character calls for softer gravel-and-stone treatments.

Further out, the family homes of the Ridgeway area and the newer developments around town bring the standard upgrade brief: widen for two or three cars, replace builder-grade surfacing, and keep a front garden that still looks like a garden. Behind the houses, Harborough gardens tend to be generous — we build porcelain terraces, lawns and full transformations here alongside the driveways.

Market Harborough specifically

Ground, drainage and conservation in Market Harborough

Market Harborough is on Jurassic mudstones and clays, which drain slowly, and the town sits where the River Welland meets the River Jordan. Water management decides most designs here, so we test the ground before choosing a soakaway or a permeable build-up.

The Market Harborough conservation area covers the historic town core and was designated in 1969, with separate conservation areas at Great Bowden and Lubenham. An Article 4 direction served in 2020 on land at Queen's Street, Little Bowden, covers gates, fences and walls.

The town centre flooded from surface water in September 2024, when a high River Welland stopped the drains discharging, and more than 50 properties flooded in July 2013. Permeable surfaces and attenuation are worth taking seriously here.

Do I need planning permission for a driveway in Market Harborough?
A permeable drive, or one that drains to your own lawn or border, needs no planning permission; more than 5m² of impermeable surface draining to the road does.
