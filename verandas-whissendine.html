Glass verandas in Whissendine

Glass verandas in Whissendine

Whissendine sits on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border, known for its working windmill and a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes. Gardens are often bigger here and the houses more varied than in a town, so each veranda is sized to the building rather than picked from a range. The frame is aluminium, made to measure and powder-coated, with a glass or polycarbonate roof.

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so a rear veranda on a house that is not listed is usually permitted development within the rear extension depth and height limits. Open-sided verandas under 30m² are usually exempt from building regulations too. That leaves more of the conversation for the things that make a difference day to day: size, roof material, side screens, lighting and heaters.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

The village is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through it. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, and names Whissendine among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. So we design the water route before anything else: where the downpipe discharges, whether a soakaway can work in the clay, and how far the footings must go to reach firm ground. A veranda should never add to a wet garden.

A typical Whissendine veranda runs across the back of a stone or brick house or a farm conversion, sometimes at 6m × 3m where the garden is larger. The work covers footings dug through the clay, a wall plate and flashing, a glass roof and gutter, and a downpipe to a tested soakaway or drain, with side screens if the garden is open.

Questions

Glass verandas in Whissendine: questions

What is the price of a veranda in Whissendine?
Guide prices are £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda and £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m. Any side screens, lighting or heaters are added to that. On Whissendine clay the drainage can be a bigger part of the job, and it is set out in the fixed written price after the visit.
Is planning permission needed for a veranda in Whissendine?
Usually not. Whissendine has no conservation area and no Article 4 direction, so a veranda fixed to the back of a house that is not listed is normally permitted development within the rear extension depth and height limits, as long as it does not face a road. Planning is with Rutland County Council if it is needed.
Can a veranda be fitted to a farm conversion?
Usually, yes. Conversions can have long rear elevations and wide openings, and because our frames are made to measure, the posts and beam can be set to suit them. We check the wall construction on the visit, since the wall plate must fix into something sound. If the building is listed, permission comes first.
Our garden gets waterlogged in winter. Should we still have a veranda?
Yes, but the drainage has to be right. On Whissendine clay, the roof water must go somewhere that can take it, so we test the ground and may connect to a drain rather than build a soakaway. The footings go down to firm ground so the posts stay put through wet winters.
