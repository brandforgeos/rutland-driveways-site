Composite & timber decking in Market Harborough

Composite & timber decking in Market Harborough

Market Harborough has seen what water does when it has nowhere to go: the town centre flooded from surface water in September 2024, and more than 50 properties flooded in July 2013. A deck will not solve a flood, but it should not make drainage in your garden any worse, and it should survive years on wet ground. That shapes how we build here, from the depth of the supports to what goes under the frame.

Our decks use posts or pads set on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame spaced to suit the boards, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath. Around the Square and the historic core gardens are often small and enclosed. Out towards Little Bowden and Great Bowden the Victorian and Edwardian houses have longer plots, and on the newer estates, including Airfield Farm near Lubenham, owners often want a deck to replace a builder's patio.

Market Harborough specifically

What makes Market Harborough different

The Jurassic mudstones and clays under Market Harborough drain slowly, and that affects a deck in two ways. Supports set in soft, wet clay can settle, so we dig to firm ground before placing posts or pads. And a deck covers ground that would otherwise take some rain, so we keep the space underneath open and lined with membrane rather than sealed, with a planned route for water off and around the deck. On a low or wet plot we may suggest a permeable area alongside it.

A typical Market Harborough deck sits off the back of a Victorian or newer estate house, often 15 to 20 square metres, low to the ground and laid to replace a lawn that stays wet much of the winter. The work involves testing the ground, setting supports deep enough, building the frame, and fitting composite boards with a step to the garden.

Composite & timber decking in Market Harborough: questions

What does decking cost in Market Harborough?
Guide prices are £200–£250 per m² for composite supplied and fitted, and £110–£140 per m² for softwood. In pounds, a 15–20m² composite deck usually comes to £3,000–£5,000. If your garden needs drainage work alongside the deck, we price that separately so you can see it. The fixed figure is confirmed in writing after our visit.
Do I need permission for a deck in Market Harborough?
A rear deck of 30cm or lower that, together with other outbuildings, takes up no more than half the garden is normally permitted development. Go higher and Harborough District Council must grant permission; a listed property needs consent. The town core is a conservation area, and the Queen's Street, Little Bowden Article 4 direction covers gates, fences and walls.
Will a deck make my garden flood worse?
Built properly it should not. We leave the ground under the deck open beneath a weed membrane rather than covering it with concrete, and plan where rain falling on the boards goes. If your garden already struggles to drain, we look at that as part of the job.
Is composite or timber better on wet Harborough clay?
Composite boards do not rot, which helps where the air under a deck stays damp. The frame matters just as much, so we keep it clear of the ground and ventilated. Pressure-treated softwood costs less but has to be re-treated regularly on damp ground to keep its looks.
