Composite & timber decking in Great Bowden

Composite & timber decking in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens just north east of Market Harborough, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes and later housing on its edges. It has its own neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, with policies on design and character. Most decks are in rear gardens out of public view, but where a garden is seen from a green or lane we choose boards and finishes that sit comfortably with the materials around them.

Every deck we build has posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the joist spacing the boards need, and a weed membrane and drainage beneath to keep the frame dry. Behind older brick and ironstone houses, softwood or warm composite tones look at home, and the later houses on the village edges usually suit a clean composite deck with a picture-frame edge. Steps and step lights are added where the garden needs them.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped and part of Great Bowden Lane was affected. On this ground we set the supports on firm material below the soft surface layer, keep the frame clear, and make sure rain falling on the deck has a route away rather than collecting underneath.

A typical Great Bowden deck sits behind a brick or ironstone house, modest in size and finished in warm tones, or off the back of a newer edge-of-village home as a larger composite deck. It involves a test dig in the clay, supports on firm ground, membrane and drainage, the frame, and boards finished with a matching edge.

Composite & timber decking in Great Bowden: questions

How much does decking cost in Great Bowden?
Composite decking is £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted and softwood timber £110–£140 per m², so a modest deck behind an older house often costs less than the £3,000–£5,000 typical of a 15–20m² composite deck. Raised decks with balustrades and steps cost more. We give you a fixed written price after the visit.
Does Great Bowden's neighbourhood plan affect a deck?
The plan has design and character policies, and the village has a conservation area, so road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. Out of sight at the rear, a deck no taller than 30cm and within the half-garden limit is normally permitted development. Higher decks need Harborough District Council's permission, and listed houses need consent.
My garden is visible from the green. Does that change the design?
It makes material choice more important. We suggest natural or warm board tones and a simple edge so the deck reads as part of the garden rather than standing out against older houses.
Is clay a problem for decking near the Langton Brook?
Not if it is handled properly. Slow-draining clay softens when wet, so we take the supports down to firm ground, keep the frame ventilated, and plan where water goes before the boards are fitted.
