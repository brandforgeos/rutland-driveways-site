Driveway gates in Uppingham

Driveway gates in Uppingham

Uppingham's slopes decide more gate designs than anything else. A drive that climbs away from the road leaves no room for swing gates to open inwards, and the rule that gates must not open outwards over the footway or road rules out the other direction. Sliding gates get round this because they run along the boundary rather than through the drive. On the Victorian and interwar roads leading out of town, and on the later estates to the north and east, we often find exactly this situation.

In the centre, many properties have a rear courtyard or a tight entrance off a lane rather than a front garden. That changes the job again: there may be no straight run of wall for a sliding gate to park against, so a carefully sized pair of swing leaves, opening into the courtyard, can be the better fit. We fit both types in aluminium and composite, automate them with keypads, intercoms or fobs, and force-test every automated gate to the standard.

Uppingham specifically

What makes Uppingham different

Uppingham stands on Northampton Sand ironstone, which generally drains better than the clay around Oakham but varies from one house to the next, so we dig before we design post foundations. Its slopes also push water downhill towards gate openings, and a sliding gate's track or a motor at the low point needs that water given somewhere to go. The historic centre is a conservation area, where road-facing gates can need permission. Several nearby villages, including Lyddington, Seaton, Ayston, Preston, Ridlington and Belton-in-Rutland, have Article 4 directions under which gates can need planning permission.

A typical Uppingham job is an interwar or later house on one of the roads out of town, where the drive rises from the road and the owners want security for cars parked at the top. That usually means a single automated sliding gate running behind an existing wall or fence line, a concrete foundation along its run, a cable from the house, and photocells and safety edges.

Questions

Driveway gates in Uppingham: questions

What do automated gates cost in Uppingham?
For a sloping Uppingham drive, a new automated sliding gate typically costs £5,000–£10,000 supplied and fitted. Automated swing gates are typically £4,000–£8,000, and putting motors on gates you already own is usually £1,500–£3,000. Power supply is extra, and we write up a fixed price after the site visit.
I live in Lyddington. Do my gates need planning permission?
Possibly. Lyddington, Seaton, Ayston, Preston, Ridlington and Belton-in-Rutland have Article 4 directions under which front walls, gates and parking spaces can need planning permission from Rutland County Council. In Uppingham itself there is no Article 4 direction, but gates facing the road in the conservation area can still need permission.
Can I have swing gates on a drive that slopes up from the road?
Usually not well. A swing leaf opening inwards hits the rising drive, and gates must not open outwards over the footway or road. Sliding gates suit sloping and short drives because they need no swing space, which makes them a natural fit on Uppingham's hillier roads.
We only have a narrow courtyard entrance. What will fit?
It depends on the walls either side. A sliding gate needs a clear run of boundary to park against, which tight centre properties often lack. Where it is missing, a pair of swing leaves sized to the opening and set to open into the courtyard is usually the answer. We measure the swing arc on the visit.
