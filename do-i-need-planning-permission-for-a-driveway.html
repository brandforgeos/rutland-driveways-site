The rule

The 5m² front garden rule

Since 1 October 2008, paving more than 5m² of front garden with an impermeable surface that drains to the road needs planning permission. The rule is about where the rainwater goes, not the material you choose.

So you do not need planning permission if the surface is permeable (resin bound on a permeable base, permeable block paving, gravel), or if an impermeable surface is laid to fall onto a lawn, border or soakaway on your own land. Planning Portal: paving your front garden

Back gardens are different: a driveway or hardstanding behind the house is normally permitted development whatever it is made of.

When you do need it

When a driveway needs planning permission

  • More than 5m² of impermeable surface in the front garden draining to the road
  • A listed building, where the setting is protected too
  • An Article 4 direction: in the Stamford conservation area, a new or altered hard surface facing a road needs permission whatever the material
  • Flats and maisonettes, which do not have householder permitted development rights
  • A new access onto a classified (A, B or C) road, which needs planning permission before the dropped kerb application
  • Planning conditions on newer estates that keep front gardens open-plan
Dropped kerbs

A dropped kerb is a separate approval

Even when the drive needs no planning permission, crossing the footway to reach it always needs the highway authority's approval for a dropped kerb. In Rutland that is Rutland County Council; around Stamford, Lincolnshire County Council; in Corby, Kettering and Market Harborough, North Northamptonshire or Leicestershire County Council.

See dropped kerbs for the fees and who builds the crossing in each area.

Our approach

How we keep you on the right side of it

We design every front driveway to be permeable or to drain to your own ground, so the front garden rule is met without an application. Where an Article 4 direction, a listed building or a classified road means permission is needed, we tell you at the site visit and draw it up with you.

If the ground will not take water, a permeable driveway can be lined and drained to a controlled outlet, and driveway drainage such as channel drains and soakaways can take water off an impermeable surface before it reaches the road.

Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for a new driveway?
Usually not. A new or replacement front driveway needs no planning permission if it is permeable, or if the rainwater drains to a lawn or border on your own land. You need permission if more than 5m² of impermeable surface drains onto the road, if the house is listed, if an Article 4 direction covers it (as in the Stamford conservation area), or if the dropped kerb is onto a classified road.
Is a resin bound driveway permeable?
Resin bound is permeable when it is laid on a permeable, open-graded base. Laid over solid concrete or tarmac it is not, and then the water has to drain to your own ground.
Do I need planning for a driveway in a conservation area?
Not usually for a permeable drive. The exception is where an Article 4 direction applies, such as the Stamford conservation area, where a new hard surface facing a road needs permission whatever the material.
Does a dropped kerb need planning permission?
Only on a classified (A, B or C) road, for a listed building, or where an Article 4 direction applies. It always needs the highway authority's approval.
What happens if I build a driveway without permission?
The council can require an application after the event or ask for the surface to be changed. Designing it to be permeable from the start avoids that.
Do I need planning permission to widen my driveway?
The same front garden rule applies to the extra area: if it is permeable or drains to your own lawn or border, no planning permission is needed; if more than 5m² of new impermeable surface drains to the road, it is. A wider entrance also needs the highway authority's approval to extend the dropped kerb, which is a separate application.
Do I need planning permission for a driveway at the back or side of my house?
A driveway or hardstanding behind the house is normally permitted development, whatever the material. The 5m² rule applies to land between the front of the house and the road, which can include a side garden on a corner plot. Raising ground levels significantly, a listed building or an Article 4 direction can still bring it under planning control.
Does block paving count as permeable for planning permission?
Standard block paving does not; it is treated as impermeable, like concrete and tarmac. It can still be used without planning permission if it is laid to fall onto a lawn, border or soakaway on your own land. Permeable block paving, with gritted joints on an open-graded base, counts as permeable and can drain straight through.
Do I need planning permission for a driveway on a new-build estate?
Check your planning conditions and deeds before anything else. Many newer estates have conditions that keep front gardens open-plan or remove permitted development rights, and covenants in the deeds can restrict hard surfaces, walls and gates. If neither applies, the normal front garden rule does. The council's planning register shows the conditions for your estate.
Does an Article 4 direction affect my driveway in Rutland or Stamford?
It might. The Stamford conservation area has had an Article 4 direction since December 2018, so road-facing hard surfaces need planning permission whatever the material. 17 Rutland villages, including Lyddington, Seaton, Braunston, Langham, Preston and Tinwell, also have Article 4 directions. Each covers different works, so check the wording for your village with Rutland County Council.
How much does planning permission for a driveway cost?
A householder planning application in England costs £548 from April 2026. If you only want proof that a drive is permitted development, a lawful development certificate costs £274. Pre-application advice is extra and varies by council: £210 with Rutland, £90 with South Kesteven for Stamford, £101 with Harborough and £187 with North Northamptonshire.
Should I get a lawful development certificate for a new driveway?
It is optional, but worth it where there is any doubt, such as a corner plot, a new-build estate with conditions, or a drive near an Article 4 area. A lawful development certificate is the council confirming in writing that the work is permitted development, which also helps when you come to sell. It costs £274 from April 2026.
Does a new driveway need building regulations approval?
No, building regulations do not normally apply to a driveway. What does need approval is any new connection to a public sewer, which goes through the water company, and any work on the footway, which needs the highway authority's dropped kerb consent. Surface water must never go into the foul sewer.
