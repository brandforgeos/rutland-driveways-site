Bin stores in Oakham

Bin stores in Oakham

Most Oakham households now juggle two or three wheelie bins, and they tend to end up wherever there is space: against the front wall, down the side passage or on a patch of lawn that turns to mud by February. We fit composite slatted stores with aluminium doors for one, two or three bins, built on a level paved pad with a sensible route out to the kerb. The bins disappear from view, and collection day stops involving a wrestle across wet grass.

We start by measuring your actual bins, not a catalogue size, and checking that each lid can lift fully once the bin is inside. Then we look at where the store should sit, how far it is to the kerb and what the ground is like. A store is only as good as the pad beneath it, and in Oakham the pad is where the real work is. You get a guide price on the day and a fixed price in writing after the visit.

Oakham specifically

What makes Oakham different

Oakham sits in the Vale of Catmose, and the heavy clay around much of the town holds water through the winter. A paving pad laid straight onto that clay will rock and sink unevenly, which is exactly when store doors start to bind. We dig down to firm ground, build a proper sub-base and give the pad a fall away from the house. With Oakham named as a surface water flood risk area of national significance, we also make sure the run-off from a new pad has a planned route rather than adding to a puddle by the back door.

On a Barleythorpe or Oakham Heights estate home, a typical job is a two or three bin store at the side of the house, replacing a strip of builder-grade gravel or turf with a level paved pad. On the older terraces off Brooke Road or Burley Road, it is more often a compact store in a small front garden, with a short paved path to the pavement.

Bin stores in Oakham: questions

How much does a bin store cost in Oakham?
A single wheelie bin store fitted on a level paved base starts from about £1,000 as a guide. Two and three bin stores, and any new path to the kerb, are priced on the visit, because the size of the pad and the clay underneath both affect the work. We confirm a fixed price in writing afterwards.
Do I need planning permission for a bin store in Oakham?
At the side or back of the house it is normally permitted development. In front of the house a free-standing store technically falls outside householder permitted development, so we check with Rutland County Council first. Oakham has no Article 4 direction, but homes in the historic centre sit in the conservation area, where the usual conservation area limits apply.
Why do bin stores on clay end up with doors that stick?
Usually because the pad was laid on soft clay without a proper base, so one corner settles and the frame twists. On Oakham clay we dig to firm ground, build a sub-base and lay the paving level with a fall, so the store stays square and the doors keep closing as they should.
Can the bin store go where the lawn keeps flooding?
It can, but we would not simply pave over a wet spot. Surface water is the commonest problem we deal with in Oakham, so we look at where the water is coming from and give the pad a fall and a route for run-off, which often improves the area around it as well.
