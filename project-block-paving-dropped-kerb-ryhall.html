The job

The brief

This house in Ryhall was being extended and remodelled, and its planning approval came with conditions for the frontage: three off-road parking spaces, a sealed surface rather than loose gravel, surface water dealt with on the property, and the existing dropped crossing extended under a Section 184 licence from Rutland County Council.

What we built

The old slabs and hardstanding came out first, as the before photo shows, and the drive was rebuilt across the whole frontage, around 90m² of new paving. The field is a brindle block framed by a charcoal double border, with a concrete edging kerb holding the edges where the paving meets the footway and the neighbouring drives.

A steel-grated channel drain runs the length of the side boundary, so water falling across the drive is collected on the property instead of running onto the next drive or the road. It is the kind of detail that is easy to leave out on a big frontage and hard to add later.

At the road, the crossing was extended to suit the wider drive and the footway reinstated in fresh tarmac, all under the council's licence. Dropped kerbs are part of most of our driveway jobs, and we hold the street works qualification needed to build them.
