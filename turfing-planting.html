Questions

Things people ask us

How much does turfing and planting cost?
Turfing and planting is priced after the site visit as one fixed figure, because preparing the ground usually costs more than the turf or plants. Breaking up compacted subsoil, bringing in topsoil and levelling are what move the price, especially on new-build plots. It is often best value done as part of a wider patio or garden project.
When can I walk on newly laid turf?
Lightly after about two weeks, once the roots have taken. Keep heavy use off it for four to six weeks.
Is turf or grass seed better for a new lawn?
Turf is better if you want a usable lawn within weeks and a clean finish straight away; seed is cheaper but takes a couple of months to thicken up and only works in the right weather. Seed is best sown in early autumn or mid to late spring, when the soil is warm and moist, and needs protecting from birds and feet. Either way, the ground preparation is the same and matters more than the choice.
How much topsoil do you need under new turf?
Aim for at least 100mm of good screened topsoil under new turf, and 150mm is better on poor or compacted ground. Less than that dries out quickly in summer and leaves roots sitting on subsoil or rubble, which is why so many new-build lawns struggle. On heavy clay, the layer beneath should be broken up first so water drains down through the soil rather than sitting under the turf.
How do you prepare ground for laying turf?
Clear the old grass and weeds, break up any compacted layer and pick out stones and rubble, then spread screened topsoil, level it and firm it by treading or rolling so it will not sink unevenly. Rake the surface to a fine tilth, add a pre-turf fertiliser and lay the turf the day it arrives, with joints staggered like brickwork and the edges butted tightly together. Water it straight after laying.
How often should I water new turf?
Daily for about the first three weeks, and twice a day in hot or windy weather, giving enough water to soak through the turf into the soil beneath. Lift a corner to check: the soil underneath should be damp. Gaps opening between the rolls, or turf turning straw-coloured, mean it is drying out. Once it has rooted, switch to a deep watering once or twice a week in dry spells.
When should I mow new turf for the first time?
Usually after two to three weeks, once the roots have taken and the turf no longer lifts at the corners. Set the mower to its highest setting and take off no more than a third of the grass height, with sharp blades so the turf is cut rather than pulled. Mow when the lawn is dry, then bring the height down gradually over the next few cuts.
Why is my new turf going yellow or brown?
Most often because it is drying out, either from too little water or because it was laid on thin soil over rubble or compacted subsoil that cannot hold moisture. Turf left rolled up for more than a day also yellows. Patches that stay brown after watering usually point to stones or debris underneath. Most turf recovers if watering is put right quickly; patches over rubble need lifting and the soil fixing.
Can turf be laid in summer or winter?
Yes, turf can be laid for most of the year, but autumn and spring are best because the soil is warm and moist and less watering is needed. In summer it needs heavy daily watering to stop it drying out before it roots. In winter it roots slowly, and it should not be laid on frozen or waterlogged ground or walked on while frosted.
What plants grow well in Rutland soil?
It depends which side of the county you are on. The free-draining limestone in the east, towards Ketton and Stamford, suits lavender, rosemary, salvias, euphorbias, pinks and other Mediterranean plants, but it is alkaline, so rhododendrons, camellias and azaleas struggle outside pots. The heavier clay in the west, around Oakham, suits roses, hardy geraniums, astrantias, viburnums, dogwoods and asters, which like the moisture it holds.
When is the best time to plant shrubs, hedges and perennials?
Autumn is the best time for most shrubs, hedges and perennials, because the soil is still warm and winter rain does the watering while roots establish. Bare-root hedging and roses are planted from November to March while they are dormant. Container-grown plants can go in at any time of year if the ground is not frozen or waterlogged, but anything planted in summer needs regular watering through dry spells.
What affects the cost of turfing a garden?
Ground preparation drives the cost more than the turf itself. Lifting an old lawn, breaking up compacted ground, removing rubble and importing topsoil can each cost more than the rolls, along with how far turf and soil have to be barrowed from the road. Lawn size and shape matter too. A quick lay on unprepared ground looks cheaper but is the version that fails, so compare quotes on preparation and topsoil depth.
