Driveway drainage in Whissendine

Driveway drainage in Whissendine

Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street in Whissendine floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. That makes it all the more important that drive water stays on your own plot rather than running into the lane. Surface water must not go onto the road anyway, and here the reason is obvious. We design drive drainage that collects water at the low point and gives it a sensible outlet within your boundary.

Whissendine sits on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border, known for its working windmill, with a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes. Bigger gardens and longer drives are common, and farm conversions often have large yards or shared access areas that were never designed to drain domestically. Those bigger surfaces collect more rain, so the outlet has to be sized for them.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the ground drains slowly, so we design the base and the water route before anything else. A soakaway may need to be larger than on limestone, or a crate system may store water better while the clay takes it in gradually; the percolation test decides. The Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, and drainage on your own drive needs no planning permission.

A typical Whissendine job is a house or farm conversion with a long drive or yard that falls towards the road or the building. We fit a vehicle-rated channel across the low point, take the water to a soakaway or crate system sized from a percolation test in the clay, and correct the falls so nothing runs out into the lane.

Questions

Driveway drainage in Whissendine: questions

How much does driveway drainage cost in Whissendine?
Whissendine prices start from the same guide: a channel drain across a typical driveway or garage entrance costs about £1,000–£1,800 installed, or £120–£200 per metre for a heavy-duty, vehicle-rated channel. A soakaway or crate system on the clay is sized and priced after a percolation test. You get a fixed price in writing after we visit.
Do I need permission for drainage work in Whissendine?
Your own driveway's drainage needs no planning permission, and Whissendine is neither a conservation area nor covered by an Article 4 direction. The limits are on where the water goes: not the foul sewer, not the road, and a new public sewer connection only with the water company's approval.
Our drive runs straight out onto Main Street. Is that a problem?
It is. Surface water must not go onto the road, and Main Street already floods. We set a channel across the bottom of the drive, before the boundary, and take the water to a soakaway or crate system on your own land.
How do you drain a big farm conversion yard on clay?
Larger surfaces collect more rain, so the outlet has to be sized for the area as well as the ground. We work out where the yard falls, place channels at the collecting points, and size a crate system or soakaway from a percolation test.
