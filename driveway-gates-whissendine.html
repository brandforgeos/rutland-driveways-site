Driveway gates in Whissendine

Driveway gates in Whissendine

Whissendine is one of the Rutland villages without a conservation area, and it has no Article 4 direction, so outside listed buildings, gates follow normal permitted development: up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles, and never opening outwards over the footway or road. That makes the planning side simpler than in many neighbouring villages, and lets the design focus on the ground and the layout.

The village has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes, and bigger gardens and longer drives are common. A long drive often means the gate is some distance from the house, so the power run and the way visitors get in, usually an intercom or keypad, need planning. We fit sliding and swing gates in aluminium and composite and force-test every automated gate to the standard.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, which drains slowly and moves with the seasons, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among those with a few properties at river flood risk. For gates, that means footings deep enough for wet clay, a sliding gate's foundation designed so water does not sit along it, and control boxes mounted high. We design the water route before anything else.

A typical Whissendine job is a farm conversion or larger house with a long drive off the main street, where the owners want automated swing gates with an intercom so they can let callers in from the house. It usually involves posts on deep footings in clay, a long cable run, the gates and motors, and photocells and safety edges, all tested before handover.

Driveway gates in Whissendine: questions

What does an electric gate cost in Whissendine?
Whissendine guide prices are typically £4,000–£8,000 for automated swing gates and £5,000–£10,000 for a new automated sliding gate, supplied and fitted. Fitting motors to gates you already own is usually £1,500–£3,000. The power supply is priced on top and can be a larger part of the job on a long drive. We confirm a fixed price in writing after visiting.
Do I need planning permission for gates in Whissendine?
In most cases, no. Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction, so gates are normally permitted development up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles, as long as they do not open outwards over the footway or road. Listed buildings are the exception and need checking with Rutland County Council.
Main Street floods. Where should the gate motor go?
Away from the lowest point, with the control box and connections mounted as high as practical. On a sliding gate we also look at whether the foundation would hold water. Where water regularly crosses the entrance, swing gates with raised motors can be the safer choice.
Can a farm conversion's old gateway take an automated gate?
Often, but we check the posts or piers first. Old gateposts were not built for a motor's load, so we either confirm they are sound or set new posts on proper footings in the clay. Wide farm openings can suit a sliding gate if there is a clear run alongside.
