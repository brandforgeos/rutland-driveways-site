Bin stores in Langham

Bin stores in Langham

Langham, just north west of Oakham and long known as the home of Ruddles brewery, is one of the Rutland villages where an Article 4 direction applies. That makes the front of the house the part to be careful with. We fit composite slatted stores with aluminium doors for one, two or three bins on a level paved pad, and in Langham we decide the position with the planning rules in mind from the start.

Plots here are often generous enough that a side or rear store, with a paved path out to the kerb, is perfectly practical. Where the front is the only sensible spot, we check with Rutland County Council before building anything. Either way, we measure your bins, make sure the lids open fully once they are inside and give a guide price on the visit, with a fixed price confirmed in writing afterwards.

Langham specifically

What makes Langham different

Langham sits on the edge of the Vale of Catmose clay, where water drains slowly, and the Langham Brook has a history of exceeding its bank capacity. For a bin store pad, that means digging to firm ground, a proper sub-base and a fall that sends water away from the house rather than leaving it to pool around the store. Rutland's Article 4 directions can cover front walls, gates and fences, and parking spaces, so a new paved area facing the road needs checking.

On one of Langham's ironstone or limestone houses in the centre, a typical job is a one or two bin store at the side or rear, with a paved path out. On the larger plots around the village, it is often a two or three bin store set beside a long drive, on its own pad with a firm route to the kerb.

Bin stores in Langham: questions

How much is a bin store in Langham?
A single wheelie bin store fitted on a level paved base starts from about £1,000 as a guide. Two and three bin stores, and any new path to the kerb, are priced on the visit. On a generous Langham plot the path can be a large part of the job, and we confirm a fixed price in writing.
Does Langham's Article 4 direction affect a bin store?
It can, at the front. Langham has a conservation area with an Article 4 direction, and Rutland's directions can cover front walls, gates and fences, and parking spaces. A free-standing store in front of the house is already outside householder permitted development, so we check with Rutland County Council first. Side and rear stores are normally permitted development.
Will a pad on Langham clay stay level?
It will if the base is right. Clay moves and holds water, so we dig through soft ground to a firm layer, build a proper sub-base and lay the pad with a fall. That keeps the store square and the doors working through wet winters.
We are near the brook. Is that a concern?
The Langham Brook has a history of exceeding its bank capacity, so on plots near it we look carefully at levels and where the pad's run-off will go. The aim is a pad that sheds water away from the house and the store, not one that adds to the problem.
