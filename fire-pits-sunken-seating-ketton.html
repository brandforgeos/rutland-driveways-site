Fire pits & sunken seating in Ketton

Ketton gave its name to Ketton stone, and the cream oolitic limestone of its older streets sets the tone for any garden work here. A fire pit or a ring of sunken seating faced in grey concrete would look out of place behind a Ketton stone cottage. We build pits lined and set into the patio, and sunken seating with retaining walls, benches, steps and drainage, finished in materials chosen to suit the house. Prices given here are guides, confirmed in writing after the visit.

The historic core has stone cottages under Collyweston slate, often with modest rear gardens. Around it, later 20th-century houses and bungalows usually have more open, regular plots, which suit a full sunken seating area with room for benches on several sides. The quarry and cement works sit on the edge of the village, a reminder of where the stone comes from. We also cover nearby Tinwell, Collyweston, Barrowden, Tixover, Aldgate and Geeston.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Most of Ketton stands on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which usually drains well, so a soakaway beneath a sunken seating area often works. The exception is land near the River Chater, which runs through the village to meet the Welland. The Welland flood warning area at Ketton is the only Environment Agency flood warning area in Rutland, and Rutland's flood strategy names Ketton among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. We test before designing any soakaway.

A typical Ketton job is a bungalow or later house with a level rear lawn, where the owners want somewhere sheltered to sit by a fire. We dig a shallow sunken area, build retaining walls on footings with drainage behind, face the benches in a buff stone to suit the village, and set a lined fire pit in the centre over a soakaway sized from a test hole.

You can check any address on the Environment Agency flood map.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Ketton: questions

How much is a built-in fire pit in Ketton?
For Ketton, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500. A Ketton sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build. Your fixed price comes in writing after we have visited and tested the ground.
Does Ketton's conservation area mean I need permission?
In Ketton, a fire pit or sunken seating area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission. Ketton has a conservation area covering the historic village but no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings. Wherever it goes, keep the fire a safe distance from fences, trees and stone buildings.
Can you match the benches to Ketton stone?
We face benches and walls in stone, brick or render chosen to suit the house, and in Ketton that usually means buff and natural tones rather than grey. We agree the facing material with you on the visit, looking at it against your own walls, before anything is ordered.
We live near the Chater. Is sunken seating sensible?
It depends on the ground. Away from the river, Ketton's limestone usually drains freely; close to it, the ground can hold water. We dig a test hole first. If a sunken area would sit in wet ground, we will tell you, and a level seating area with a built-in pit may be the better choice.
