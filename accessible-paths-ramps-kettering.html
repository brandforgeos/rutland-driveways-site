Accessible paths & ramps in Kettering

Accessible paths & ramps in Kettering

Kettering's housing covers a lot of ground, and so do its access problems. A Victorian or Edwardian terrace near the centre or along the Headlands might have a short front garden with steps up to the door, while an interwar or post-war semi usually has a drive and a path that is too narrow for a wheelchair. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, level landings and widened paths to suit each one.

Because the town has 26 conservation areas, including the town centre, Barton Seagrave, Burton Latimer, Geddington and Rothwell, and Article 4 directions that apply to individual streets or buildings, we check your address before designing anything that faces the road. The gradients and landings are set to Approved Document M and to the person using the ramp, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations and the council's schedule.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

Ground around Kettering ranges from ironstone to clay, and on newer plots, including those at Hanwood Park, it is often compacted subsoil under a thin layer of topsoil. That compacted layer can look firm but drains poorly, so water running off a ramp has nowhere to go. We check what is under each garden before choosing the base and drainage. Near the River Ise on the east of town, we take extra care over where the run-off ends up.

On a typical Kettering terrace, a ramp has to fit a short front garden between the pavement and the door, often with a landing and handrails on both sides. On post-war estates, the job is more often a ramp beside the drive plus a path widened enough for a wheelchair to pass the car.

Sources: North Northamptonshire Article 4 directions · Hanwood Park.

Permissions in Kettering

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Kettering conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by North Northamptonshire Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Accessible paths & ramps in Kettering: questions

What does a ramp cost in Kettering?
As a starting point, a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface typically costs up to about £2,500. Kettering terraces with short front gardens and estate homes needing a wider path are both priced on the visit. Disabled Facilities Grant jobs follow the council's schedule, and we confirm a fixed price in writing.
Will a ramp need planning permission in Kettering?
It depends on the address. A ground-level garden ramp or path does not normally need permission, but a raised ramp or platform over 30cm can. Kettering has 26 conservation areas and site-specific Article 4 directions covering individual streets or buildings, so we check yours before designing anything facing the road. North Northamptonshire Council is the planning authority.
My path is too narrow for a wheelchair past the car. Can it be widened?
Yes, and it often helps. On Kettering's post-war estates the path beside the drive is often the narrowest part of the route. Widening it is priced on the visit, and we lay it to the same falls and finish as the ramp so the whole route reads as one.
We have just moved into Hanwood Park. Is the ground ready for a ramp?
Usually not as handed over. New-build plots here are often compacted subsoil under thin topsoil, which drains poorly. We dig it out, build a proper base and plan the run-off so the ramp stays level and dry.
