Built to carry the weight

A sliding gate runs on a track and needs a level foundation beam; a swing gate hangs off posts that have to stay plumb for years. We build both properly, because that is what keeps an automated gate working.

Questions

Things people ask us

How much does a driveway gate cost?
A new automated sliding gate typically costs £5,000–£10,000 supplied and fitted, and automated swing gates £4,000–£8,000. Automating gates you already have is usually £1,500–£3,000. The power supply is priced on top.
Should I choose sliding or swing driveway gates?
Sliding gates suit drives that slope up from the road or are short, because they do not need space to swing. Swing gates are simpler where there is room for them to open inwards.
Are electric driveway gates safe?
They are when they are fitted with safety edges or photocells and force-tested after installation, which we do on every automated gate.
Do I need planning permission for driveway gates?
Usually not: driveway gates are normally permitted development up to 1m high next to a road used by vehicles, or its footway, and up to 2m elsewhere. Permission is needed above those heights, on a listed house, and where an Article 4 direction removes the right, which covers road-facing gates in Stamford's conservation area and 17 Rutland villages including Langham and Lyddington. We check your address before anything is ordered.
What happens to electric gates in a power cut?
Electric gates stop where they are in a power cut, and every motor has a manual release, usually a key, that unlocks it so the gates can be opened by hand. Many systems can also take a battery backup that runs a limited number of cycles until the power returns. We show you how the release works when the gate is handed over, so you are never stuck on the wrong side of it.
What safety standard do electric driveway gates have to meet?
Powered gates count as machinery under the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations 2008, so the installer should risk-assess the gate, fit safety devices, force-test it against BS EN 12453 and give you a declaration of conformity. Safety edges or photocells make the gate stop and reverse if it touches or detects something in its path. Ask for the force test results in writing; we record them on every automated gate we fit.
How long do electric driveway gates last?
A well-built aluminium gate on sound foundations should last for decades, while motors and control boards typically last around 10 to 15 years with regular servicing. A service at least once a year, checking the safety edges, photocells and force settings and greasing the hinges or clearing the track, keeps the gate safe and catches worn parts before they fail. Gates dragging on sagging posts wear motors out much faster.
Are aluminium, composite or wooden driveway gates better?
For automated gates, aluminium is usually the best choice: it is light, does not rust or rot and never needs painting, which is easy on the motor and hinges. Composite gives a timber look with little upkeep. Solid timber gates are heavy, swell and shrink with the weather and need treating, so they are harder to automate reliably. We fit aluminium and composite gates, matched to your fencing.
How far back from the road should driveway gates be set?
Far enough that a car can pull completely off the road and footway while the gates open, which highway guidance often puts at about 5m from the carriageway. Gates must also open inwards, never out over the footway or road, under the Highways Act 1980. On a short drive that cannot achieve that, gates may leave the car waiting on the pavement, and we will tell you so on the visit.
Do electric gates need a power supply from the house?
Yes, most electric gates run on a mains supply cabled underground from the house or garage to the motor, usually on its own protected circuit. A new circuit is notifiable work under Part P, so it should be installed and certified by a registered electrician, which is how ours are done. Solar kits exist for remote entrances, but they suit light gates and low use.
How long does it take to install electric driveway gates?
Fitting electric driveway gates usually takes a few days on site, though made-to-measure gates have a lead time before that. The foundations or track beam go in first and the concrete needs time to set, then the gates are hung, the motors and safety devices fitted, and the force test done last. The programme in your written proposal sets out the lead time and the days on site.
Should I choose underground or arm motors for swing gates?
Arm or ram motors fixed to the posts are cheaper, easier to service and suit most domestic swing gates. Underground motors sit in a box beneath each hinge, so they are almost invisible and suit period houses, but the box needs good drainage because standing water can damage the motor. On low-lying or poorly drained ground, above-ground motors are usually the safer choice.
