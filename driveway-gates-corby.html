Driveway gates in Corby

Driveway gates in Corby

Corby grew on the Northampton Sand ironstone that fed the steelworks, and large areas around the town were opencast quarried and later restored. For a driveway gate, that matters more than most people expect. A heavy automated gate puts real load on its posts, and a footing that sits half in natural ironstone and half in fill can settle unevenly. So we dig on the visit, find out what is there, and design the foundations around it.

Housing here runs from the New Town estates built for steelworkers, such as Kingswood, Lodge Park, Beanfield and Danesholme, to the older villages of Corby Village, Great Oakley and Weldon, and the growing Priors Hall Park in the north east. We fit sliding and swing gates in aluminium and composite to suit each, with keypads, intercoms or fobs, and every automated gate is force-tested to the standard. Older village frontages often already have walls we can work alongside.

Corby specifically

What makes Corby different

Restored ground is the main thing we design around in Corby. Fill and compacted subsoil can give a post footing less support than natural ironstone, so we go deeper or wider where the dig shows it is needed. Near the Willow Brook, which runs through Corby and Weldon, wetter ground means electrics placed clear of low spots. Planning is usually straightforward: Corby's only Article 4 direction, at Kingswood, covers houses in multiple occupation, not gates, though its nine conservation areas, including the Old Village, still need road-facing gates checked.

A typical Corby job is a house on Priors Hall Park, where the front was handed over as a bare, compacted plot, or an older estate home where the owners now want their cars behind a gate. It usually means new posts on footings designed after a test dig, automated swing gates opening inwards, a cable from the house, a keypad or fobs, and photocells.

Questions

Driveway gates in Corby: questions

How much do automated gates cost in Corby?
In Corby, expect automated swing gates to typically cost £4,000–£8,000 supplied and fitted, and a new automated sliding gate £5,000–£10,000. If you only need motors on existing gates, that is usually £1,500–£3,000. The power supply is extra. Where restored ground needs a deeper footing, it is included in the fixed written price we send after the visit.
Do gates need planning permission in Corby?
Rarely. Permitted development normally covers gates up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles, provided they do not open outwards over the footway or road. Corby's Kingswood Article 4 direction does not cover gates, but in its conservation areas or on a listed house, road-facing gates can need permission from North Northamptonshire Council.
My house is on old quarry land. Can it take a heavy gate?
Yes, with the right foundation. Restored ground varies, so we dig where the posts will go and look at what comes out. If it is fill or soft clay rather than ironstone, we design a deeper or wider footing so the posts stay plumb under the gate and motor.
Can you fit gates on a new-build Priors Hall Park plot?
We can. Many plots there are handed over as bare, compacted ground, so there are usually no existing posts or piers to use. We build the posts and foundations from scratch, run the power from the house, and position the gates so they open inwards clear of the car.
