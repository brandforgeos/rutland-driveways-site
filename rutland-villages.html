Village work

Where we work, and what we know about each

Where we work, and what we know about each

A few notes from the van — every village below is somewhere we have worked or quoted, and each has its own character to respect.

Stamford-side villages

  • Ketton: home of the famous Ketton stone — drives and walls here sit beside some of the finest limestone in England, and material choice shows immediately
  • Empingham: large village by the Rutland Water dam; mix of period stone and modern homes, plenty of full garden projects
  • Ryhall & Belmesthorpe: Gwash-side villages minutes from Stamford
  • Great & Little Casterton: on the old Ermine Street; period frontages onto the lane needing careful levels
  • Tinwell: estate village character — sympathetic finishes matter
  • Barnack, Collyweston & Easton on the Hill: just over the borders, the slate-and-stone villages; Collyweston gave its name to the roofs on half the county, and new work has to live up to that company

Oakham-side villages

  • Langham: a large village north of Oakham, where generous plots suit sweeping driveways
  • Whissendine: windmill village on the county edge; bigger gardens, longer drives
  • Greetham & Cottesmore: active, growing villages — family gardens, artificial lawns, drive widenings
  • Edith Weston: south shore of Rutland Water; premium garden and terrace projects with a view to live up to
  • Exton, Burley, Ashwell, Braunston, Egleton: the Vale villages we cross daily — stone settings where buff and natural palettes belong

If your village is not named, it is still covered — nowhere in Rutland is more than about twenty minutes from our base, and we also work the border villages of Lincolnshire, Leicestershire and Northamptonshire.

Why village work is its own skill

  • Materials that defer to the stone: buff resin, bound gravel, natural block and real stone edgings — never anything that fights a limestone frontage
  • Conservation awareness: many villages have conservation areas; permeable, sympathetic builds keep projects simple
  • Rural practicalities: long drives, unmade lanes, shared accesses, soakaway drainage where there are no mains gullies
  • Local reputation: in a village, our last job is our advert — we are happy to point you to work you can walk past

Conservation areas and Article 4 villages

Rutland has 34 conservation areas. In these 17 places an Article 4 direction can remove permitted development for front walls, gates and fences, parking spaces, porches and roofs: Ashwell, Ayston, Belton-in-Rutland, Braunston, Clipsham, Egleton, Langham, Little Casterton, Lyddington, Lyndon Top, Preston, Ridlington, Seaton, Stretton, Teigh, Tinwell, Whitwell. We check the direction for your village before designing anything that faces the road. Rutland list of conservation areas and Article 4 directions

Trees in a conservation area need six weeks' notice before work; protected trees need consent. Both are free. Rutland County Council: tree works
