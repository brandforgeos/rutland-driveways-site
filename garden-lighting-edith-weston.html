Garden lighting in Edith Weston

Garden lighting in Edith Weston

Edith Weston sits on the south shore of Rutland Water, a stone village with a green and a number of substantial houses. Terraces, outdoor rooms and lighting designed around the views are the typical projects here, and lighting is what makes an outdoor room work after dark. We design low-voltage schemes in layers: soft light around seating and dining, step and riser lights where levels change, uplighters on trees and walls to frame the garden, and nothing glaring. All prices on this page are guides until fixed in writing.

An outdoor room needs light for different moods: brighter for cooking or clearing up, lower for sitting late, and just the garden picked out when you are looking from indoors. That means zones on separate switches, with timers, dimming or app control. Where a terrace is being built or rebuilt, the ducts go in with the paving, so lights can sit in walls, steps and planters without any cable ever lying under a slab.

Edith Weston specifically

What makes Edith Weston different

Edith Weston is on the limestone plateau above the reservoir, and most plots drain reasonably well, though clay pockets turn up. That matters for buried uplighters: in a clay pocket they need a bed of free-draining stone, while elsewhere they can go in more simply, so we test before deciding. Rutland Water itself carries negligible flood risk according to Rutland's flood strategy, and the village is not named for river flooding. There is a conservation area and no Article 4 direction.

A typical Edith Weston scheme is for a substantial stone house with a terrace facing the garden or the water. It has three or four zones: light built into terrace walls and steps, soft light over a dining or seating area, uplit trees at the edge of the lawn, and path lights to the gate. Ducts are laid as the terrace is built.

Questions

Garden lighting in Edith Weston: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Edith Weston?
Terrace and outdoor-room schemes usually fall into the full multi-zone range of £2,000–£6,000 or more. A path, step and border scheme is typically up to about £1,500 installed, and a new outdoor circuit is priced separately. These are guides; the fixed price comes in writing after we visit.
Is planning permission needed for garden lighting in Edith Weston?
No. Garden lighting does not need planning permission, and Edith Weston has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable and is done and certified by a registered electrician. Keeping lights pointed down also keeps them from shining towards neighbours or the shore.
Can the terrace and garden lighting work separately?
Yes, and they should. We put the terrace, the paths and the wider garden on separate zones, so you can have bright light for cooking, low light for sitting and just the trees lit when you are indoors. Timers, dimming and app control are all options.
Do the clay pockets here affect where lights can go?
Only for fittings set into the ground. Where a test hole finds clay, we dig out beneath the fitting and bed it on free-draining stone so water does not sit around it. Wall, step and planter lights are not affected by what is in the ground.
