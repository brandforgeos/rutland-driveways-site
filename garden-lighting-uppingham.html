Garden lighting in Uppingham

Garden lighting in Uppingham

Uppingham is built on a hill, and plenty of its gardens climb or drop away from the house. That makes lighting a safety question as much as a pleasure: a flight of garden steps you cannot see on a dark evening is a fall waiting to happen. We design low-voltage schemes that light the treads and risers first, then add uplighters on trees and walls and gentle light around the places you sit. Prices on this page are guides, and the fixed price comes in writing once we have seen the garden.

Many houses in the centre, near the school buildings, the High Street and the Market Place, have rear courtyards rather than gardens. A courtyard enclosed by ironstone walls can look superb with two or three wall lights grazing the stone and a single fitting under a planter or bench. On the Victorian and interwar roads out of town, and the later estates to the north and east, gardens are bigger and usually take a fuller scheme with paths, steps and borders lit together.

Uppingham specifically

What makes Uppingham different

The Northampton Sand ironstone under Uppingham drains better than the Vale clay around Oakham, but it changes within a single street, and the slopes send water downhill towards the lowest point. That shapes where we put things. Transformers and controls go at the top of a garden, not at the bottom where water collects, and ducts are laid to fall so they do not channel water into a fitting. The conservation area covers the historic centre, and the town itself has no Article 4 direction.

A typical Uppingham scheme is for an interwar or later house with a garden that steps down in two or three levels. Lights go into the risers of each flight, a couple of wall lights mark a retaining wall, and a tree or shrub on the lowest level is uplit so the garden has depth from the house. Ducts follow the paths between levels.

Garden lighting in Uppingham: questions

What does garden lighting cost in Uppingham?
A path, step and border scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more, with a new outdoor circuit priced separately. A small courtyard usually sits towards the lower end. All of these are guides until we have visited and put a fixed price in writing.
Do I need planning permission for lighting in Uppingham or nearby villages?
Garden lighting itself does not need planning permission. Uppingham has no Article 4 direction, though Lyddington, Seaton, Ayston, Preston, Ridlington and Belton-in-Rutland do, covering things like front walls, gates and parking spaces, so we check if lighting is part of wider front work. A new mains circuit is done and certified by a registered electrician.
Can you add lights to existing stone steps on a sloping garden?
Usually, yes. Riser lights can often be fitted into existing steps, and where cutting into old stone is not sensible, low path lights beside the flight do the same job. The cable then runs alongside the steps rather than under them, so nothing has to be lifted.
Is lighting worth it in a small town-centre courtyard?
Often more than in a big garden, because you see the whole space from the house. Two or three wall lights washing the stone, plus one soft light near seating, can make a courtyard feel like an extra room. We keep the light pointing down so it does not spill into neighbouring windows.
