Accessible paths & ramps in Oakham

Accessible paths & ramps in Oakham

Getting to your own front door should not depend on someone lending an arm. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, level landings and widened paths across Oakham, so that a wheelchair, a walking frame or a pushchair can reach the door without a step in the way. That includes adaptations funded by a Disabled Facilities Grant, which we build to the council's schedule and to the occupational therapist's recommendations rather than to our own idea of what looks right.

Oakham's streets throw up very different starting points. A limestone or ironstone house near the Market Place may have its door almost on the pavement, a semi off Brooke Road or Burley Road usually has a short front garden and a step or two, and a newer home at Barleythorpe or Oakham Heights often has a builder-grade drive that is due for replacement anyway. We set out each ramp around the person using it, with gentle gradients and level landings top and bottom.

Oakham specifically

What makes Oakham different

Most of the ground around Oakham is Vale of Catmose clay, and it stays wet for months. A ramp is a long, sloping hard surface that ends at your door, so if it is laid flat or falls the wrong way, rainwater runs straight to the threshold and sits there. Rutland County Council's flood strategy lists Oakham as a surface water flood risk area of national significance, with the Barleythorpe Brook culverted under parts of town. We dig a test hole on the visit and design a channel, soakaway or permeable edge so the landing stays dry and safe underfoot.

For a typical Oakham semi or terrace, the job is a concrete ramp from the drive or pavement up to the front door, with a level landing big enough to open the door from a wheelchair, handrails where the rise needs them and a textured non-slip finish. On the clay, that usually means a deeper base than people expect and a drainage channel across the door end.

Questions

Accessible paths & ramps in Oakham: questions

How much does an access ramp cost in Oakham?
For an Oakham home, a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface typically costs up to about £2,500. Extra length, landings or a widened path are priced when we visit and dig the test hole, and grant-funded work follows the council's schedule. Treat these as guide figures; the fixed price follows in writing.
Do I need planning permission for a ramp in Oakham?
Usually not. A ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. Oakham's conservation area covers the historic centre, yet the town has no Article 4 direction, so outside listed buildings the normal rules apply with the usual conservation area limits. Rutland County Council deals with planning.
Will a ramp make water collect at my front door?
It can if it is built badly, and on Oakham's clay that is a real risk. We lay the landing to fall away from the door, and where the ground will not take the water we add a channel or soakaway designed from the test hole. Water that has nowhere to go is the commonest problem we fix in Oakham.
Can you replace a new-build drive and add a ramp at the same time?
It can. On estates such as Barleythorpe and Oakham Heights it often makes sense to replace a tired builder-grade drive and build the ramp and landing as one job, so the levels, falls and drainage are worked out together rather than patched onto each other.
