Garden lighting in Whissendine: what is involved

We design and install low-voltage garden lighting: step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around seating, with the ducts laid as the patio is built and the electrics certified.

Whissendine, on the north western edge of Rutland near the Leicestershire border, is known for its working windmill, and has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes. Bigger gardens and longer drives are common.

We cover Whissendine (LE15) and the villages around it, including Langham, Teigh, Ashwell, Pickwell and Stapleford.

Whissendine specifically

Ground and conservation in Whissendine

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. Ground drains slowly, so we design the base and the water route before anything else. Cables run in ducts laid below the paving and planting, so we bring the lighting in as the patio or garden is built in Whissendine wherever we can.

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings.

Garden lighting in Whissendine: questions

How much does a garden lighting scheme cost in Whissendine?
A path, step and border lighting scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone garden scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. Your fixed price comes in writing after the site visit.
Do I need planning permission for a garden lighting scheme in Whissendine?
Garden lighting does not need planning permission, but a new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, so it is done and certified by a registered electrician. Keep lights pointing down and away from neighbours' windows.
Does the ground in Whissendine make a difference?
Cables run in ducts laid below the paving and planting, so we bring the lighting in as the patio or garden is built in Whissendine wherever we can.
Can you light an existing garden?
Yes. Walls, beds and steps can be lit without lifting paving; lights set into the paving itself usually mean lifting slabs.
Is low voltage bright enough?
Yes. Modern LED fittings give plenty of light, and a few well-placed lights look better than floodlighting.
