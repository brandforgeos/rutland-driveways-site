Fencing in Kettering

Fencing in Kettering

Kettering's boundaries vary with the age of the house. Victorian and Edwardian terraces near the centre often have narrow gardens with tall walls on one side and an old fence on the other. Villas along the Headlands have longer, wider plots. Interwar and post-war estates, Barton Seagrave and the growing Hanwood Park development each have their own pattern, with Hanwood Park plots often finished with basic panels the owners want to upgrade.

We build closeboard timber fencing on concrete posts with gravel boards, and composite or aluminium slatted fencing on aluminium posts. For a terrace garden, a slatted panel can make a small space feel tidier and more private. For a long villa garden, closeboard on concrete posts is usually the sensible choice for the main run, with a slatted screen closer to the house. A matching gate across the side passage finishes the job neatly.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

Ground around Kettering ranges from ironstone to clay, and on new-build plots it is often compacted subsoil under a thin layer of topsoil. That compacted layer is hard to dig and does not drain, so we break through it and set each post in concrete at the right depth. Kettering has 26 conservation areas and several site-specific Article 4 directions covering individual streets or buildings, so for any boundary facing the road we check your address first.

A typical Kettering job is a Victorian terrace or 1930s semi with a rear garden fence that has blown or rotted in places. We take out the run, set concrete posts, fit gravel boards and closeboard, and add a gate where the garden meets a rear alley or side path.

Questions

Fencing in Kettering: questions

What is the price of a fence in Kettering?
Typical guide prices are £80–£100 per metre supplied and fitted on concrete posts for closeboard timber and from about £240 per metre fitted at 1.8m high for composite and aluminium slatted fencing. Taking out the old fence is priced on the visit, and the fixed figure is given in writing afterwards.
Is my Kettering street covered by an Article 4 direction?
Possibly. Kettering's Article 4 directions are site-specific, mostly older ones covering individual streets or buildings, and there are 26 conservation areas. Otherwise fences are normally permitted development up to 2m, or 1m next to a road or footpath used by vehicles. We check your address before designing anything facing the road.
Our Hanwood Park garden is rock hard. Is that a problem?
It is common on new-build plots, where compacted subsoil sits under thin topsoil. It makes digging harder and water sits on it, so we dig properly for each post and keep timber up on gravel boards.
Can you fence a terrace garden with no side access?
Yes. Narrow terrace gardens with access only through the house or a rear alley take more planning for getting materials in and old panels out. We look at the access on the visit and allow for it in the price.
