Driveway gates in Ryhall

Driveway gates in Ryhall

Ryhall, with Belmesthorpe, sits on the River Gwash just north of Stamford, and many of its homes were built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders as Stamford's. On those estates, front drives tend to follow a familiar pattern, and the question is often whether the opening is wide and deep enough for swing gates or better suited to a sliding gate along the boundary. We measure both options on the visit.

Around the green and the church, stone cottages bring tighter entrances and older walls that may or may not carry a gate and motor. We fit sliding and swing gates in aluminium and composite, with keypads, intercoms or remote fobs, and force-test every automated gate to the standard. Where you already have sound gates, automating them is often the most cost-effective route. If the existing posts are sound, adding motors and safety devices may be all that is needed.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Ryhall is on the limestone, which generally drains well and gives posts firm ground. The Gwash runs through the village, though, and Rutland's flood strategy names Ryhall among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. Near the river, the ground is wetter, so we check each site and set footings and electrics accordingly. Unlike neighbouring Stamford, Ryhall has no Article 4 direction: normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings, though road-facing gates in the conservation area can need permission.

A typical Ryhall job is a modern estate house with an open-plan front drive, where the owners want to close it off with automated swing gates. It usually involves building new posts on concrete footings, a cable run from the house, the gates and motors, a keypad or fobs, and photocells, with the leaves sized to open inwards clear of the parked car.

Driveway gates in Ryhall: questions

How much do gates cost in Ryhall?
On a Ryhall estate drive, automated swing gates typically cost £4,000–£8,000 supplied and fitted, and a new automated sliding gate £5,000–£10,000. Where gates are already in place, automating them is usually £1,500–£3,000. Power supply is on top. We give a fixed price in writing once we have measured the opening and checked the ground.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction apply in Ryhall?
No. Ryhall is in Rutland, and its conservation area has no Article 4 direction. That leaves gates as normally permitted development up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles, with the rule that they must not open outwards over the footway or road. On a listed house, or facing the road within the conservation area, gates can need permission from Rutland County Council.
We live close to the Gwash. Does that change anything?
It can. Ground near the river is wetter, so we dig to check it before designing post footings, and we keep the control box and connections clear of the lowest part of the entrance. On drier limestone further from the river, a standard footing is often enough.
Is our estate drive deep enough for swing gates?
Swing leaves need to open inwards without hitting a parked car or the slope of the drive. On shorter estate drives, that space can run out, and a sliding gate along the boundary may suit better. We measure the opening, the depth and the swing on the visit.
