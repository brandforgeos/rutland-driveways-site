A dropped kerb needs approval from the highway authority before any work touches the footway, and the fees depend on your council: £194 for Rutland's permit, £300 or £470 in Stamford (Lincolnshire County Council), £300 to apply plus £240 if a contractor builds it in Market Harborough (Leicestershire), and £333 in Corby, Kettering and Weldon (North Northamptonshire). We hold the NRSWA street works qualification and can build the crossing in every area we cover.

Fees and rules by council

Highway authorityTownsFee and who builds itRules and timings
Rutland County CouncilOakham, Uppingham, Ketton, Empingham, Cottesmore, Greetham, Langham, Whissendine, Ryhall, Edith Weston£194 for a Section 184 vehicle access permit (one or two homes)You need the highways team's consent before anything touches the verge or footway. A frontage onto a classified (A, B or C) road, a listed building or anything other than a single house also needs planning permission first.
Lincolnshire County CouncilStamford£300 if Lincolnshire County Council builds it (the council puts a typical crossing at £2,500–£3,500, tarmac only, 2.75m wide), or £470 if an accredited contractor builds itLincolnshire County Council replies within 10 working days. When a contractor builds it, the start date has to be 21–90 days ahead. Trees, lamp posts or utilities within 1m can restrict the works, and A, B and C roads need planning permission first.
Leicestershire County CouncilMarket Harborough, Great Bowden£300 to apply, then either the county council builds it at a quoted price, or an accredited contractor builds it under a £240 permitThe parking space should be at least 5.5m deep (4.8m absolute minimum) and 2.4m wide, and steeper than 1:20 over the first 5m is a likely refusal. The county assesses the site within 30 days and decides within two weeks; A, B and C roads need planning permission from the district first.
North Northamptonshire CouncilCorby, Kettering, Weldon£333 licence and inspection feeThe parking space must be at least 5m deep and 3m wide, the crossing at least 10m from a junction and 1m from a tree, and A, B and C roads need planning permission first. A decision usually takes 28 days, and work starts 2–3 weeks after the licence is issued.

Questions

Things people ask us

Do I need planning permission for a dropped kerb?
Only on a classified (A, B or C) road, for a listed building, or where an Article 4 direction applies, such as road-facing frontages in the Stamford conservation area. Otherwise the highway authority's consent is what you need.
Can you build the dropped kerb?
Yes, in every area we cover. We hold the NRSWA street works qualification, apply under the right permit or licence for your council, and build the driveway behind it so the levels line up.
