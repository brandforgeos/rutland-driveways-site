Glass verandas in Corby

Glass verandas in Corby

Corby's housing is mostly post-war and modern: the New Town estates built for steelworkers at Kingswood, Lodge Park, Beanfield and Danesholme, and now the large Priors Hall Park development in the north east. These houses have straightforward brick back walls, which suit a veranda well. The aluminium frame fixes along the wall on a proper plate with flashing above it, the posts stand at the front, and the glass roof falls gently to a gutter built into the beam.

The usual reason people ask is simple: the back garden gets used for a few weeks a year and they want more from it. A veranda gives a dry place for a table outside the back doors without making the kitchen dark, and side screens, LED lighting or heaters can be added to suit. We work across Corby and its older villages too, including Corby Village, Great Oakley, Gretton, Cottingham, Rockingham and East Carlton.

Corby specifically

What makes Corby different

Corby sits on the Northampton Sand ironstone that fed the steelworks, and large areas around the town were opencast quarried and restored. Under one garden you can find natural ironstone at one end and clay or fill at the other. A veranda's posts carry the outer edge of the roof, so each footing has to reach ground that will not settle. We dig at the post positions, look at what comes out, and design the footing depth and the downpipe route from that.

A typical Corby job is a 4m × 3m veranda across the back of a New Town semi or a newer Priors Hall Park house, over an existing patio or a new one laid at the same time. It involves footings at each post, a wall plate and flashing into the brickwork, a glass roof and a downpipe. Near the Willow Brook, the water route gets extra attention.

Questions

Glass verandas in Corby: questions

How much is a veranda in Corby?
Typically £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda, and £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m, with side screens, lighting and heaters on top. If we find fill at the post positions and the footings need to go deeper, that is in the written fixed price, not added afterwards.
Do I need planning permission for a veranda in Corby?
Usually not for a rear veranda within the rear extension limits, away from a road and on a house that is not listed. Corby's nine conservation areas, including the Old Village, Great Oakley and Gretton, have tighter rules. The Kingswood Article 4 direction controls houses in multiple occupation and does not affect extensions.
My Priors Hall Park garden was handed over bare and compacted. Is that a problem?
Not for the veranda itself, because the posts go on footings dug below the surface. It does matter for the rainwater: compacted ground sheds water rather than soaking it up, so a downpipe left to run onto the lawn will make a wet patch. We plan a soakaway or drain connection to suit.
Can a veranda be fixed to an older New Town house?
Yes. We check the back wall on the visit for sound brickwork, weep holes, air bricks and the damp proof course, then set the wall plate height so the roof clears the doors and windows and the flashing sits correctly. Any render or cladding may need cutting back locally where the plate fixes.
