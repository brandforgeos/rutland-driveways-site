Fire pits & sunken seating in Uppingham

On a hilltop town like Uppingham, a sloping garden is often the obvious place for sunken seating. Cut a level terrace into the bank, retain it with a wall that doubles as a bench back, and you have a sheltered spot with the fire at its centre. We build fire pits set into patios, sunken seating with retaining walls and steps, and the drainage at the bottom that stops the area turning into a pond. Prices here are guides; your fixed price follows in writing after we visit.

Housing in Uppingham shapes the design. Around the High Street and the Market Place, many properties have rear courtyards rather than gardens, which suit a compact built-in pit or a gas pit more than a large sunken area. The Victorian and interwar houses on the roads out tend to have longer plots with a fall, and the later estates to the north and east usually have more regular gardens with room for a full seating area.

Uppingham specifically

What makes Uppingham different

The Northampton Sand ironstone under Uppingham usually drains better than the Vale clay around Oakham, but it varies within a single street, so we never assume. The bigger issue is the slope. A seating area cut into a bank collects water running down the garden as well as the rain falling into it, and a retaining wall holding back a slope has soil and water pushing against it. That is why our walls go on proper footings with drainage behind, and the low point gets a drain or soakaway sized for the ground.

A typical Uppingham job is a Victorian or interwar house on one of the roads out of town, with a garden that rises or falls away from the back door. We cut a level area into the slope, build a retaining wall faced in stone or brick that forms the back of a bench, add steps down from the patio and set a lined fire pit in the middle, with water from higher up the garden routed around it.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Uppingham: questions

What does a sunken fire pit area cost in Uppingham?
In Uppingham, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500. Cut into a slope, a full sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job. On a sloping Uppingham garden, the size and height of the retaining walls is what moves the price most, so we price it after measuring the fall.
Do the Article 4 directions near Uppingham affect a fire pit?
Uppingham itself has no Article 4 direction, though the historic centre is a conservation area. Nearby villages such as Lyddington, Seaton and Ayston do have directions, where front walls, gates and parking spaces can need planning permission. A fire pit or sunken area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission.
I only have a courtyard in the town centre. Can I still have a fire pit?
Usually, yes, though a compact pit suits a courtyard better than sunken seating. In a tight space close to neighbours and buildings, smoke is the main concern, and smoke can be a statutory nuisance. A gas pit is cleaner and turns on at a switch, and it needs a Gas Safe engineer to connect it.
Will water from the top of my garden flood the seating area?
It will if nothing stops it. On Uppingham's slopes we look at where surface water comes from as well as where it goes, and plan a route around or through the sunken area. Then a drain or soakaway at the bottom deals with the rain that falls straight into it.
