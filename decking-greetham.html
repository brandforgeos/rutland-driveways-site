Composite & timber decking in Greetham

Composite & timber decking in Greetham

Greetham sits just west of the A1, a limestone village with a historic core of stone cottages and farm buildings, later infill and estate housing, and Greetham Valley on its edge. Many owners here are already updating the front of the house, widening drives that have been gravel for decades, and the back garden is often next on the list. Gardens in the village vary from compact cottage plots to wider estate gardens, and the right size of deck follows from that rather than from a standard pattern.

A deck is a practical way to add a level outdoor room. We build ours on a braced frame: posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the joist spacing the boards call for, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath. Beside stone cottages, softwood or stone-coloured composite tends to suit best, while on the estate houses grey and charcoal boards are popular. Hidden fixings and a picture-frame edge give a clean finish whichever board you choose, and LED step lights can be added where a step down to the lawn needs to be seen after dark.

Greetham specifically

What makes Greetham different

Because most of Greetham is on free-draining limestone, the ground under a deck usually lets rain soak away rather than ponding, and firm material is often close to the surface. That keeps the base work straightforward on many plots. Low-lying gardens are the exception, since a few properties are at river flood risk, and there we test the ground, raise the frame clear of it, and keep the space underneath open. Greetham's conservation area has no Article 4 direction to work around.

A typical Greetham deck is a low platform off the back of an estate or infill house, or a smaller deck tucked behind a stone cottage, often finished in stone-coloured boards. The job involves clearing the area, setting pads on firm limestone, building the frame, laying membrane and fitting the boards and edging.

Composite & timber decking in Greetham: questions

How much is a deck in Greetham?
Composite comes in at £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted, softwood timber at £110–£140 per m². Most Greetham rear decks of 15–20m² in composite fall within £3,000–£5,000, and raised designs with steps and balustrades sit above that. We confirm a fixed price in writing once we have seen the garden.
Does Greetham's conservation area restrict decking?
Greetham has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction. Garden decking at the back of the house is normally permitted development if no part is more than 30cm high and it covers no more than half the garden with other outbuildings. Anything taller needs an application, and a listed building needs consent.
Does good drainage mean a deck is cheaper to build here?
It can make the groundwork simpler, as firm limestone often sits close to the surface. The price still depends mainly on board choice and area, so we confirm it after seeing the garden.
My house is on lower ground in the village. What changes?
We test the ground more carefully, set the supports on firm material, and keep the frame raised and ventilated so it stays dry if the ground gets wet. We also leave the space under the deck open rather than enclosed.
