Fire pits & sunken seating in Kettering

Kettering covers a wide range of gardens, and a fire pit that suits one would be wrong for another. We build pits set into the patio, lined and faced in stone or brick, and sunken seating areas with retaining walls, built-in benches, steps and a drain at the bottom so the area never floods. Every price on this page is a guide, and you receive a fixed price in writing once we have seen your garden and checked the ground.

Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas close to the centre and along the Headlands tend to have narrow gardens with neighbours close on both sides. Interwar and post-war estates have more room, Barton Seagrave to the south east has established family gardens, and Hanwood Park on the east side, planned for up to 5,500 homes, gives new owners a blank plot to plan from scratch. We also cover Burton Latimer, Geddington, Rothwell, Desborough and Mawsley.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

Ground around Kettering ranges from ironstone to clay, and that alone decides whether a soakaway will work. New-build plots add another layer: compacted subsoil under a thin covering of topsoil, which sheds water rather than absorbing it. Dig a sunken area into that and water from the lawn heads straight for it. We check what is under each garden, design the drain or soakaway to suit, and take retaining wall footings down to firm ground. Near the River Ise on the east of town, drainage is planned with the river in mind.

A typical Kettering job is a newer house at Hanwood Park or Barton Seagrave with a plain lawn and a small patio. We extend the patio, set a lined fire pit into it, and build an L-shaped bench along one side, faced in brick to match the house. Where there is room, the seating area is sunk slightly below the lawn with a drain at the low point.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Kettering: questions

What does a fire pit with seating cost in Kettering?
From about £3,500 for a fire pit with built-in seating, and typically £1,200–£2,000 for a built-in stone or brick fire pit on its own. In Kettering, a full sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job. We give you a fixed price in writing after the visit, once we know what the ground is like.
Do Kettering's conservation areas affect a fire pit?
Planning first: a fire pit or sunken seating area in a Kettering rear garden does not normally need planning permission. Kettering has 26 conservation areas, and its Article 4 directions are site-specific, mostly older ones covering individual streets or buildings, so we check your address before designing anything. Whatever the street, keep fires a safe distance from fences, trees and buildings.
Will a fire pit bother the neighbours in a terraced street?
It can if smoke drifts into gardens and open windows, and smoke can be a statutory nuisance. In close terrace gardens near the centre, a gas pit is worth considering: it is cleaner and turns on at a switch, and needs a Gas Safe engineer to connect it. Wood gives the real fire, but needs more thought about who is downwind.
Why does the ground on a new Hanwood Park plot matter?
Because a sunken area is only as good as the ground around it. New-build plots are often compacted subsoil under thin topsoil, which drains poorly. We break through it where needed, design the drain or soakaway for what is underneath, and build retaining walls on footings that reach firm ground.
