Glass verandas in Ketton

Glass verandas in Ketton

Ketton gave its name to Ketton stone, the cream oolitic limestone quarried beside the village, and its older streets are built of it, many under Collyweston slate. A veranda against one of these houses has to respect the stone. We fix the wall plate into sound masonry and dress the flashing so rain cannot get behind it, and we keep the frame slim so the wall stays the main thing you see from the garden.

Around the historic core are later 20th-century houses and bungalows, which are often the simplest homes to fit a veranda to. A bungalow has a lower eaves line, so we measure carefully to keep a sensible roof fall and comfortable head height under the front beam. Glass gives the clearest roof; polycarbonate is the lighter, cheaper option. We cover Ketton and the villages nearby, including Tinwell, Collyweston, Barrowden and Tixover, along with Stamford, Ryhall and Empingham.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Ketton is on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which usually drains well, so a soakaway for the veranda downpipe is often a simple job. The exception is the River Chater, which runs through the village to meet the Welland. Ketton has the only Environment Agency flood warning area in Rutland, and ground near the river behaves differently from the limestone higher up. We test before designing a soakaway and set post footings to suit what we find. Planning is with Rutland County Council, and the conservation area has no Article 4 direction.

A typical Ketton veranda is about 4m × 3m, fixed to the back of a stone cottage or a later bungalow over an existing patio. It means a wall plate fixed into stone or brick, flashing, posts on footings, a glass roof with a built-in gutter, and a downpipe to a soakaway. On stone houses we usually suggest a frame colour that sits with the cream stone.

Questions

Glass verandas in Ketton: questions

What will a veranda cost me in Ketton?
Most Ketton homes fall within the guide of £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda, or £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m. Lighting, heaters and side screens are priced in addition. Fixing into older stone is allowed for in the fixed price we send in writing after the visit.
Do I need planning permission for a veranda in Ketton?
Usually not at the back of the house within the rear extension depth and height limits, away from a road, on a house that is not listed. Ketton's conservation area has tighter rules but no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings. We check your address before designing.
Will fixing a veranda damage my stone wall?
It should not, if it is done properly. We find sound stone for the wall plate fixings, avoid soft or crumbling faces, and cut the flashing into a joint rather than relying on sealant alone. If the wall needs repointing where the plate goes, we say so on the visit.
My house is near the Chater. Can I still have a soakaway?
Possibly, but we will not assume it. Ground close to the river is wetter than the limestone further up, and a soakaway only works where the water can drain away. We test on the visit, and if a soakaway will not work, we look at other routes for the roof water.
