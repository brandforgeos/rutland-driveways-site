Accessible paths & ramps in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses set around them and along its lanes. A ramp at the front of one of those houses is seen by everyone who walks past, and the village's neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, has policies on design and character. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, level landings and widened paths that do the job properly and sit comfortably beside the houses around them.

On the later housing at the village edges the brief is simpler, and a straight ramp beside the drive is often the answer. Wherever the house is, the gradient and landings suit the person using the ramp, with handrails where needed. If the work is funded by a Disabled Facilities Grant, it follows the occupational therapist's recommendations and the council's schedule. We cover Welham, Slawston, Foxton and Little Bowden as well as the village itself.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped and affected part of Great Bowden Lane. A ramp turns a step into a slope that collects rain, so we design for the water first: the landing falls away from the door, and the run-off goes to a channel or permeable edge suited to the clay.

A typical Great Bowden job is an older brick or ironstone house facing a green or lane, with a step at the door and a short frontage. The ramp is finished in materials that suit the house, with a level landing, handrails and a drainage route, while edge-of-village homes usually get a ramp beside the drive.

Permissions in Great Bowden

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Great Bowden conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by Harborough District Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Accessible paths & ramps in Great Bowden: questions

How much is an access ramp in Great Bowden?
A permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface in Great Bowden typically costs up to about £2,500 as a guide. Finishes chosen to suit the greens, longer ramps, landings and widened paths are priced on the visit, and grant-funded work follows the council's schedule. You get a fixed written price afterwards.
Will a ramp need planning permission in Great Bowden?
Not usually for a ramp or path in the garden, though a raised ramp or platform over 30cm high can need it. Great Bowden has a conservation area and a neighbourhood plan with design policies, so road-facing work should respect the materials around it. Planning for the village sits with Harborough District Council.
Can a ramp look right outside a thatched or ironstone cottage?
Absolutely. We choose finishes and edging that sit with the house, keep the ramp as compact as the gradient allows, and use simple handrails, so it reads as part of the frontage rather than an add-on.
Our lane has flooded before. Does that affect the ramp?
It affects the drainage. Great Bowden's clay drains slowly, so we lay the ramp to shed water to the side, keep the landing falling away from the door and give the run-off a proper route.
