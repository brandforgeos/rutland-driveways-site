Fire pits & sunken seating in Corby

Corby's gardens were shaped by the steel industry as much as by the housebuilders, and that shows when you dig. We build fire pits set into the patio and sunken seating areas with retaining walls, built-in benches, steps and drainage at the bottom, and in Corby the first job is always finding out what the walls will stand on. Everything on this page is a guide price. Once we have dug on your plot, you get a fixed price in writing.

The New Town estates such as Kingswood, Lodge Park, Beanfield and Danesholme were built for steelworkers and usually have practical, regular rear gardens that suit a pit with built-in seating. Priors Hall Park, planned for more than 5,000 homes, hands many gardens over as bare, compacted plots, which is a blank canvas but needs work before anything is built. The older villages, Corby Village, Great Oakley and Weldon, have stone houses and more established gardens.

Corby specifically

What makes Corby different

Corby sits on the Northampton Sand ironstone that fed the steelworks, and much of the land around the town was opencast quarried and then restored. In a single garden we can find natural ironstone at one end and clay or fill at the other. For a sunken area that matters twice: retaining walls need footings on ground that will not move, and the drain or soakaway at the bottom has to suit what is actually there. Near the Willow Brook, drainage needs planning with extra care.

A typical Corby job is a semi on one of the New Town estates or a newer house at Priors Hall Park, with a lawn that is either worn out or never properly made. We break up compacted ground, dig a sunken square or circle, build retaining walls on footings, face benches in brick to match the house and set a lined fire pit in the centre, drained at the low point.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Corby: questions

How much is a sunken fire pit area in Corby?
Across Corby, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500. Corby sunken seating areas with retaining walls and drainage are priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build. On restored ground the footing depth can change the price, which is why we dig before fixing it.
Do I need planning permission for a fire pit in Corby?
For a Corby home, a fire pit or sunken seating area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission. Corby has nine conservation areas, including the Old Village, Great Oakley and Weldon, and its only Article 4 direction, at Kingswood, controls houses in multiple occupation rather than garden work. Wherever you are in Corby, keep fires clear of fences, trees and buildings.
What if my garden is on restored quarry ground?
Then we find out exactly what it is before designing anything. Restored ground can be firm in one spot and soft in the next, and a retaining wall built on soft fill can lean or crack. We dig trial holes, take the footings down to suitable ground, and size the drainage for what we find.
Our Priors Hall Park garden is bare and compacted. Where do we start?
With the ground. Many gardens there are handed over as bare, compacted plots, which drain poorly until they are broken up. We plan the fire pit area, its drainage and any lawn or planting around it together, so the seating area is not the only part of the garden that stays dry.
