Glass verandas in Stamford

Glass verandas in Stamford

Stamford asks more questions of a veranda than most towns we cover. The centre is Georgian and older, with more than 600 listed buildings, and the whole conservation area has carried an Article 4 direction since December 2018. Outside the conservation area, on the Victorian streets along Empingham Road, Tinwell Road or Casterton Road and on the new estates to the north, a rear veranda is usually as simple as anywhere else. Inside it, we check first and design second.

The veranda itself is the same well-made thing wherever it goes: a powder-coated aluminium frame that will not rot or need painting, a toughened glass or polycarbonate roof, a gutter built into the front beam and a wall plate flashed into the house. What changes in Stamford is the paperwork and the look. A slim frame in a muted colour against limestone is a different proposition from a bright white one, and we talk that through on the visit.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

On the ground, Stamford is kinder than many towns. The Lincolnshire Limestone and its loamy soils usually drain freely, so a soakaway for the downpipe is often straightforward. The exceptions are local: old cellars, rubble fill behind older houses, and the Welland flood plain, where the Environment Agency runs a flood warning area. Any of those can change where a post footing goes or where the rainwater can be sent, so we dig and look at each site rather than assume the limestone is there.

A typical Stamford veranda sits behind a Victorian or 1920s house on one of the roads out of the centre, or a newer home on the north side of town, covering a patio outside the back doors at roughly 4m by 3m. It involves post footings, a wall plate and flashing, a glass roof and a downpipe to a soakaway. In the conservation area, a planning application may need to come first.

Glass verandas in Stamford: questions

How much does a glass veranda cost in Stamford?
A 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda is typically £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted, and a 6m × 3m £15,000–£22,000. Extras such as side screens, lighting and heaters are costed separately. If planning permission is needed in the conservation area, we tell you before you commit, and the fixed price comes in writing after the visit.
Does the Stamford Article 4 direction affect a veranda?
It can. Since December 2018 the direction across the whole conservation area removes permitted development for extensions and porches facing a road, footpath or open space. A veranda fixed to the house is treated like a rear extension, so if your garden faces a footpath or open space it may need permission from South Kesteven District Council. On a listed house, permitted development does not apply.
Is a veranda suitable for a house near the River Welland?
It can be, but the drainage needs more thought. Near the river, in the Environment Agency's flood warning area, a soakaway may not be the right home for the roof water, and the ground under the post footings may be softer. We check both on the visit and design the footings and downpipe route to suit.
Will a modern aluminium veranda look wrong on a limestone house?
Not if it is kept simple. A slim frame, a clear glass roof and a colour chosen to sit back against the stone keep the house as the main thing you see. The conservation area appraisal favours natural materials such as York stone for hard surfaces, so we keep any new paving underneath in keeping.
