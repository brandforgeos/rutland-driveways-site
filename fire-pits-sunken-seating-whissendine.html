Fire pits & sunken seating in Whissendine

Whissendine's long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes comes with bigger gardens and longer drives than many villages. A large garden is a good setting for a fire pit with sunken seating, set away from the house where the evening is quieter. We build pits lined and set into the patio, and sunken areas with retaining walls on footings, built-in benches, steps and drainage. Prices here are guides, fixed in writing once we have visited.

Farm conversions often come with yards, old walls and outbuildings that give a seating area natural shelter, while newer homes usually have open lawns to plan from scratch. Either way, the ground underneath is clay, and the drainage is the part of the job most people never see but the part that decides whether the seating stays dry and usable winter after winter. We also cover Langham, Teigh, Ashwell, Pickwell and Stapleford.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the ground drains slowly. Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable, and names the village among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. A sunken seating area here is effectively a hole in clay, so the drain or soakaway at the bottom is designed from a test hole, and where the clay will not take water we find another route for it or recommend a level design.

A typical Whissendine job is a farm conversion or a stone house with a large rear garden. We set a sunken seating area into the lawn or alongside an old wall, build retaining walls on footings with drainage behind, face the benches in brick or stone to match the buildings, and centre a lined wood-burning pit, with the drain at the low point laid first.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Whissendine: questions

How much does sunken seating cost in Whissendine?
In Whissendine, a full sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build. As a Whissendine guide, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500. On clay, the drainage is a real part of the job and is included in your written price.
Do I need planning permission for a fire pit in Whissendine?
Whissendine is straightforward on planning: a fire pit or sunken seating area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission. Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development rules apply outside listed buildings. Keep any fire a safe distance from fences, trees, barns and other buildings.
Main Street floods. Should I worry about a sunken area?
It is worth being cautious. The flooding on Main Street shows how slowly water clears in the village. A sunken area needs a drain or soakaway sized for clay, and on some plots that is not achievable, in which case we will recommend a level seating area with a built-in pit instead.
Can we build the seating beside an old barn or yard wall?
Sometimes, if the wall is sound and we are not asking it to hold back soil. Where the seating sits lower than the yard, we build a new retaining wall on proper footings with drainage behind, and keep the fire a safe distance from the building itself.
