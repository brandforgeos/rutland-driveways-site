What a new deck costs

What is included in the price

The per-square-metre rate covers the whole build, not just the boards: posts or pads set on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame with joists spaced to suit the boards, a weed membrane, drainage underneath so the frame stays dry, the boards themselves and the fixings.

Finishing touches such as picture-frame edging, hidden fixings and LED step lights can be included where you want them, and we list them on the quote so you can see what each adds.

What a new deck costs

What makes it cost more or less

  • Board choice: composite sits at the top of the range, pressure-treated softwood at the bottom.
  • Height: a deck close to the ground is the simplest; a raised deck needs a heavier frame, steps and balustrades.
  • Balustrade style, from timber spindles to glass panels.
  • Shape: curves, angles and cut-outs round trees mean more cutting and more waste.
  • Ground conditions under the deck and how much levelling the frame needs.
  • Access for carrying boards and framing into the back garden.
  • Removing an old deck or patio first.

Decking costs in Rutland, Stamford and the nearby towns

What sits under the deck matters as much as the boards. On the Vale of Catmose clay around Oakham, and the slow-draining mudstones of Market Harborough, we make sure water can get away from the frame so posts and joists are not standing wet through winter. Stamford's limestone usually drains freely. Corby and Kettering new-build gardens are often compacted subsoil under thin topsoil, and parts of Corby are restored quarry ground, so we dig to firm ground for the pads. Sloping Uppingham gardens often mean a raised deck, which brings planning and balustrade costs into play.

What to check in any quote

  • That the frame, joist spacing and ground fixings are described, not just the boards.
  • Whether a weed membrane and under-deck drainage are included.
  • The finished height, and whether it goes over 30cm and so needs planning permission.
  • Which edging, fixings and lighting are in the price.
  • Who takes away waste and any old decking.

Questions

Things people ask us

What is the cheapest type of decking?
Pressure-treated softwood is the cheapest, at £110–£140 per m² fitted against £200–£250 for composite. A simple low-level rectangle on firm ground keeps the cost lowest. Bear in mind softwood needs regular treatment to keep looking good, so the saving at the start is partly spent later.
Is composite decking worth the extra cost?
For most people who want a deck that looks the same year after year, yes. Composite does not rot, splinter or need staining, so the higher fitted price buys back the time and materials you would spend treating timber. If budget is tight or the deck is temporary, softwood is a sensible choice.
What does a deck cost to maintain?
Composite needs little more than sweeping and an occasional wash to shift dirt and leaves. Softwood needs cleaning and re-treating every year or two, plus checks for loose boards and fixings. Keeping the gap under the deck clear of debris helps either type last.
Do I pay anything before the deck is built?
The payment terms, including any deposit and when the balance falls due, are written into your quote. You pay nothing until you have accepted it in writing, and bank details are sent with the first invoice rather than shown on the quote.
How long does a decking quote stay valid?
The validity period is printed on each quote. Board and timber prices change during the year, so a quote accepted after that date gets its figures rechecked before we book the work in.
How much does composite decking cost per square metre in 2026?
Composite decking typically costs £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted, and pressure-treated softwood £110–£140 per m². That rate covers the whole build: posts or pads, sub-frame, weed membrane, drainage gravel, boards and fixings. A 10m² composite deck comes to about £2,000–£2,500 and a 15–20m² deck usually £3,000–£5,000. Raised decks with steps and balustrades cost more and are priced on the visit.
Is decking cheaper than a patio?
Softwood decking usually is, at £110–£140 per m² fitted against £130–£180 for an Indian sandstone patio and £150–£200 for porcelain. Composite decking, at £200–£250 per m², generally costs more than either. On a sloping garden decking can work out cheaper overall, because a raised frame avoids the retaining walls and fill a level patio would need, though a deck more than 30cm high needs planning permission.
