Fire pits & sunken seating in Stamford

Stamford is a town where people expect things to be built properly and to look as if they belong. A fire pit or sunken seating area behind a limestone house should feel like part of the garden rather than something bought in a box. We build pits set into the patio, lined and faced in stone or brick, and sunken seating with retaining walls, built-in benches, steps and drainage. The figures on this page are guide prices, and a fixed price follows in writing after our visit.

The centre is Georgian and older, built of local limestone under Collyweston slate, and its rear gardens are often walled and compact. Out along Rutland Terrace, Empingham Road, Tinwell Road and Casterton Road, Victorian houses tend to have longer gardens, the 1920s streets around Melbourne Road and New Cross Road have regular plots, and the new estates on the north side of town usually start with a bare lawn and a basic patio.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

Stamford's Lincolnshire Limestone and loamy soils usually drain freely, which makes a sunken seating area easier here than on clay: a soakaway at the low point often does the job. But old cellars, rubble fill and the Welland flood plain all change the picture locally, and the Environment Agency runs a flood warning area for properties near the river. We dig before we design. Materials matter too, and natural stone walls and benches suit the town's character, especially in and around the conservation area.

A typical Stamford job is a walled rear garden behind a Victorian or older stone house, where the owners want somewhere to sit out in the evening. We lower a section of the garden, retain it with walls faced in limestone or a matching material, build benches along two or three sides and set a lined fire pit at the centre, with a soakaway beneath the floor.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Stamford: questions

How much does a fire pit with seating cost in Stamford?
A fire pit with built-in seating starts from about £3,500, and a built-in stone or brick fire pit on its own typically costs £1,200–£2,000. Sunken seating with retaining walls and drainage is priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build in a Stamford garden. If you want natural stone facing to match the house, we price exactly that after the visit.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction affect a fire pit?
Since December 2018, an Article 4 direction across Stamford's conservation area removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates and fences where they face a road, footpath or open space. A fire pit in a private rear garden does not normally need planning permission, but if the work would be visible from those places, check with South Kesteven District Council first.
My garden is near the Welland. Can I still have sunken seating?
Possibly, but it needs care. The Environment Agency runs a flood warning area for properties near the River Welland, and flood plain ground does not behave like the free-draining limestone elsewhere in town. We test the ground on the visit, and if a sunken area would sit in wet ground we will say so and suggest a level design.
Is a wood fire pit a problem in the town centre?
It can be in a tight walled garden with neighbours close by. Smoke can be a statutory nuisance, so be considerate about when you light it. Wood gives the real fire and the smell; gas is cleaner and turns on at a switch, which suits many central Stamford gardens. Any gas pit in Stamford needs a Gas Safe engineer to connect it.
