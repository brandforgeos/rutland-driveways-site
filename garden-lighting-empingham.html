Garden lighting in Empingham

Garden lighting in Empingham

Empingham sits at the eastern end of Rutland Water, below the dam, and many gardens here look out over the water or open country. That view is the reason not to over-light. A garden lit with bright floodlights turns the windows into mirrors and blanks out everything beyond the fence. We design low-voltage schemes the other way round: step and riser lights at ground level, soft light around seating, and a few uplighters on chosen trees, so your eye can still travel past the garden. Prices here are guides.

Plots in the village are often generous, and a bigger garden usually suits being lit in zones rather than all at once. A terrace by the house can be on one switch, a path to the far end on another, and a lit tree or wall that you see from the kitchen on a third. Timers, sensors or app control mean each part comes on when it is wanted. The stone-built centre around the church and main street suits the quietest schemes of all.

Empingham specifically

What makes Empingham different

Empingham is on the limestone side of Rutland and much of the village drains reasonably well, which suits buried uplighters. The exception is ground near the North Brook, a tributary of the Gwash that runs through the village and which Rutland's flood strategy traces through Cottesmore, Empingham and Ryhall. There the ground stays wet, so we keep connections and controls high and use wall or spike fittings. Empingham has a conservation area and no Article 4 direction.

A typical Empingham scheme is for a stone house or later home on a wide plot with open country or water beyond. It usually splits into three zones: soft light around the terrace, low lights on a path down the garden, and one or two uplit trees towards the boundary. Everything points down or into the planting, so the view stays dark.

Questions

Garden lighting in Empingham: questions

How much does garden lighting cost in Empingham?
A full multi-zone garden scheme typically costs £2,000–£6,000 or more, which suits many of the bigger plots here. A path, step and border scheme is typically up to about £1,500 installed. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately, and all prices are guides until we confirm them in writing.
Do I need planning permission for lighting in Empingham?
No. Garden lighting does not need planning permission, and Empingham has no Article 4 direction, though it does have a conservation area. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, done and certified by a registered electrician. Keep lights pointing down and away from neighbours' windows.
Will garden lights spoil our view at night?
Not if they are designed low. Bright, high lights reflect in your windows and hide the view; low step and path lights and a few uplit trees let your eyes adjust so you can still see beyond the garden. We avoid floodlighting entirely for that reason.
Can lighting run down to the bottom of a long Empingham garden?
Yes. Long cable runs are planned so the fittings at the far end are as bright as those near the house, and the far zone can be on its own switch or timer. Near the North Brook, where the ground stays wet, we use wall or spike fittings rather than buried lights.
