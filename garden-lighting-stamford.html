Garden lighting in Stamford

Garden lighting in Stamford

Stamford's limestone takes light beautifully. A wall lit softly from below, or a single tree picked out against Collyweston slate roofs, does more for a garden than any number of bright fittings. We design low-voltage schemes with step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around seating, and we choose small, dark fittings that disappear in daylight. All prices on this page are guides, and the fixed figure comes in writing once we have seen the garden.

The town has more than 600 listed buildings, and many centre gardens are walled plots behind Georgian and older houses. Out along Rutland Terrace, Empingham Road, Tinwell Road, Ryhall Road and Casterton Road the Victorian houses have longer gardens with room for paths and borders, while the 1920s houses around Melbourne Road and New Cross Road tend to have tidy, square rear gardens. The new estates on the north side are best lit when the patio goes in, with ducts laid in the same job.

Stamford specifically

What makes Stamford different

Two things set Stamford apart. First, the Article 4 direction across the whole conservation area, in place since December 2018, removes permitted development for hard surfaces, walls, gates and fences facing a road, footpath or open space. Lighting is not on that list, but if a scheme comes with new front paving or a wall, that part may need permission. Second, the old fabric: we fix into mortar joints rather than stone faces wherever we can. Near the Welland, inside the Environment Agency's flood warning area, controls go high.

A typical Stamford scheme is for a Victorian house off Empingham Road or Tinwell Road with a walled rear garden. Two or three uplighters graze the limestone wall, the path to the back gate has low lights, the terrace gets soft light around the table, and one specimen tree is lit from beneath. Cables run in ducts along the borders so no paving is lifted.

Garden lighting in Stamford: questions

How much is garden lighting in Stamford?
Path, step and border lighting typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, and a full multi-zone scheme £2,000–£6,000 or more. A new outdoor circuit is priced separately. Those are guide figures; a fixed written price follows our visit, when we know the walls, cable routes and power supply.
Does the Stamford Article 4 direction affect garden lighting?
Garden lighting does not need planning permission. The Article 4 direction covers hard surfaces, walls, gates, fences, porches and extensions facing a road, footpath or open space, so new front paving or walls built alongside lighting may need permission from South Kesteven. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable and is done and certified by a registered electrician.
Can lights be fixed to an old limestone wall without damaging it?
Yes, with care. We fix into the mortar joints rather than drilling the face of the stone, so a fitting can be removed later without leaving a scar. Where a wall or house is listed, we talk the fixings through with you before anything is attached, and ground-mounted uplighters avoid fixings altogether.
Our garden is near the River Welland. Does that change the design?
It changes where the important parts go. The Environment Agency runs a flood warning area near the Welland, so the transformer and controls sit high and close to the house, and buried fittings are kept to drier ground. Low-voltage fittings themselves are well suited to wet gardens.
