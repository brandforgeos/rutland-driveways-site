Accessible paths & ramps in Weldon

Accessible paths & ramps in Weldon

Weldon stone has been quarried here for centuries, and the ground under the village shows it. A garden can be natural rock at one end and clay or fill at the other, and a ramp is a rigid slab that will not forgive uneven support. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, level landings and widened paths across Weldon, Great Oakley, Little Oakley, Deene, Brigstock and Stanion, digging first so the base suits what is actually there.

The historic centre of stone houses sits in a conservation area, appraised in 2009, while the later estates that now join Weldon to Corby have more standard frontages and drives. North Northamptonshire has no Article 4 direction in Weldon affecting driveways. Every ramp is set out to the gradient and landings its user needs, and where a grant is paying, we work to the occupational therapist's specification and the council's schedule of prices.

Weldon specifically

What makes Weldon different

Weldon sits over the limestone and ironstone that were quarried here, and ground can change from natural rock to clay or fill within a garden. If a ramp settles more at one end than the other, the slab cracks or a lip opens at the door, which is a real hazard for wheels. We dig to find firm ground and size the base to suit. The Willow Brook runs past the village, with the Weldon flood storage reservoir nearby, so drainage is designed rather than assumed.

A typical Weldon job is an estate house with a step at the front door and a path beside the drive, or a stone house in the old village with a small frontage. Either way it means a trial dig, a properly sized base, a ramp with a level landing and handrails where they are needed.

Permissions in Weldon

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Weldon conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by North Northamptonshire Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Accessible paths & ramps in Weldon: questions

How much does a ramp cost in Weldon?
As a guide, up to about £2,500 typically covers a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface. Where the trial dig finds fill or clay, the base is priced to suit, along with any longer ramps, landings or widened paths. Grant work follows the council's schedule, and every price is fixed in writing.
Do I need planning permission for a ramp in Weldon?
A garden ramp or path should not normally need permission, but raising a ramp or platform beyond 30cm can trigger it. Weldon's historic village is a conservation area, and North Northamptonshire has no Article 4 direction there affecting driveways. North Northamptonshire Council deals with any planning question.
Could old quarrying affect a ramp in my garden?
It can. Ground here may switch from rock to clay or fill within a few metres, and a ramp spanning both can settle unevenly. A trial dig on the visit tells us how deep the base needs to go.
Can a ramp in the old village look in keeping?
In most cases, yes. Beside Weldon stone houses we use natural-toned finishes and edging so the ramp sits comfortably in the conservation area, while keeping the surface non-slip and the gradient right for the user.
