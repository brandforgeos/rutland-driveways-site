Composite & timber decking in Kettering

Composite & timber decking in Kettering

Kettering's housing gives decks two quite different jobs. Close to the centre and along the Headlands, Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas often have long, narrow gardens where a deck by the back door creates a sitting area without losing the rest of the plot. On the interwar and post-war estates, at Barton Seagrave and at Hanwood Park, planned for up to 5,500 homes, the gardens are squarer and the brief is usually a family deck big enough for a table.

Whatever the house, we build the same way: posts or pads on firm ground, a treated or aluminium sub-frame at the joist spacing the boards need, and a weed membrane and drainage underneath so the frame stays dry. Picture-frame edges, hidden fixings and LED step lights are added where you want them, and narrow terrace gardens often suit boards laid lengthways to draw the eye down the plot. Where a terrace garden is shared with a neighbour's view, we keep the deck low.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

On Kettering's new estates the topsoil is often only a thin layer over compacted subsoil, which looks firm but can hold water at the surface and does not drain. In the older streets the ground may be ironstone or clay. We check under each garden before setting the supports, and make sure water can get away from beneath the deck. Kettering has 26 conservation areas and site-specific Article 4 directions, mostly on individual streets or buildings, so we check your address first.

A typical Kettering deck is either a slim platform across the back of a Victorian terrace, finishing with a step onto a long garden path, or a squarer composite deck on a Hanwood Park or Barton Seagrave estate house. Both involve checking the ground, setting supports, building the frame and fitting membrane, boards and edging.

Questions

Composite & timber decking in Kettering: questions

How much does decking cost in Kettering?
A slim deck behind a terrace costs less in total than a family-sized one, since composite is priced at £200–£250 per m² supplied and fitted and softwood at £110–£140 per m². For a 15–20m² composite deck, budget £3,000–£5,000. Steps and balustrades on a raised deck cost more. You get a fixed written price after our visit.
Will I need planning permission for a deck in Kettering?
Under the normal rules, a rear deck of no more than 30cm in height, covering at most half the garden with other outbuildings and platforms, does not need permission. Taller decks need it from North Northamptonshire Council, and so does any work to a listed house. Because Article 4 directions here are site-specific, we check your address.
My terrace garden is long and narrow. What size deck works?
Usually a deck the width of the garden and only as deep as you need for seating, so the rest of the plot stays open. We set the boards to suit the shape and keep a clear step down to the path or lawn.
Why does water sit on my Hanwood Park lawn, and will it affect a deck?
New-build gardens are often thin topsoil over compacted subsoil, so rain cannot soak away. Under a deck we lay membrane and drainage so water moves on, and take the supports through the compacted layer to firm ground.
