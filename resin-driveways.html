Resin driveways

What you are actually buying

A resin bound driveway done well is smooth, permeable and effectively weed-free, and it suits stone properties better than almost any other surface. Done badly, it fades, cracks and lifts. The difference is the product and the base, which is why we install as a Vuba approved contractor across Rutland, Stamford, Oakham and Uppingham.

What you are actually buying

Resin bound surfacing is natural aggregate mixed with clear resin and hand-trowelled onto a prepared base, usually at 18mm over a 6mm stone. The finish is seamless and porous: rain drains through the surface rather than off it.

  • UV-stable resin. Cheap aliphatic-free resins yellow and go brittle. We use Vuba's UV-stable systems so the colour you choose is the colour you keep.
  • The base. We excavate and build an open-grade permeable base to suit your ground, not just overlay whatever is there. Overlays are only quoted where the existing slab genuinely warrants it.
  • The detail. Granite setts, block or steel edging, recessed manhole covers, drainage channels where they are needed. The edges are where bad jobs announce themselves.

Permeable means planning-friendly

Since 2008, paving more than five square metres of a front garden with an impermeable surface needs planning permission unless the water drains within the property. Resin bound surfacing on a permeable base is SuDS-compliant, so in almost every case no application is needed — useful everywhere, and especially in conservation settings like central Stamford.

Our standard resin build-up

  • Excavation to formation level, ground assessed on site
  • Sub-base: compacted open-grade stone for permeability
  • Binder course: open-textured AC20 or grid system to suit
  • Wearing course: 18mm Vuba UV-stable resin bound aggregate
  • Edging: granite, block, sleeper or steel, agreed at design stage
  • Guarantee: 5 year workmanship, in writing

Planning and dropped kerbs by council

A front driveway needs no planning permission if it is permeable or drains to your own lawn or border; more than 5m² of impermeable surface draining to the road does (Planning Portal guidance). In the Stamford conservation area the Article 4 direction brings any new road-facing hard surface under planning control. A new or wider entrance also needs a dropped kerb, and the rules depend on the highway authority.

Questions

Things people ask us

How long does a resin driveway take to install?
Most domestic driveways take three to five days: excavation and base first, then the resin is laid in a single continuous pour and is ready to walk on within a day and drive on within three.
Will the colour fade?
Not meaningfully, if UV-stable resin is used. That is the main reason we install Vuba systems rather than unbranded resin. Ask any installer to name the product going on your drive; if they hesitate, be careful.
Is resin suitable next to a stone cottage or in a conservation area?
Usually it is one of the best choices. Buff and golden blends sit naturally against Clipsham and Ketton stone, and because the surface is permeable it normally avoids the planning issues that impermeable paving can raise.
Can you overlay my existing concrete?
Sometimes. If the slab is sound, drains adequately and has movement joints we can work with, an overlay can be sensible. If it is cracked or pooling, overlaying just transfers the faults upward, and we will tell you so.
How much does a resin driveway cost?
A resin bound driveway typically costs £150–£220 per m² all in, so a two-car drive of 40–50m² is usually £7,500–£11,000. Larger drives of 80m² and up come down to about £130–£180 per m². A resin bound drive on a permeable base needs no planning permission outside the Stamford conservation area.
