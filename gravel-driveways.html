Gravel driveways

Gravel that stays where it is put

Loose gravel on soil ruts, migrates onto the lawn and wears through to the membrane within a couple of winters. A stabilised drive holds the stone in honeycomb grid cells over a compacted, permeable sub-base, so it stays level and drains straight through.

Bound gravel, a resin-bonded gravel surface, gives the look of loose stone with a fixed surface, and it suits Stamford and village frontages where the council favours natural-looking finishes.

Long country drives are often best as a tarmac or bound gravel wheel track with loose gravel either side, which we price per job after the visit.

Planning and dropped kerbs by council

A front driveway needs no planning permission if it is permeable or drains to your own lawn or border; more than 5m² of impermeable surface draining to the road does (Planning Portal guidance). In the Stamford conservation area the Article 4 direction brings any new road-facing hard surface under planning control. A new or wider entrance also needs a dropped kerb, and the rules depend on the highway authority.
