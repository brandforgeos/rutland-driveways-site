Accessible paths & ramps in Greetham

Accessible paths & ramps in Greetham

Many drives in Greetham have been gravel for decades, and gravel is one of the hardest surfaces there is for a wheelchair, a walking frame or a pushchair. Wheels sink, frames snag, and a ramp at the door is not much help if the route to it is loose stone. We build permanent concrete and paved ramps, level landings and widened paths, and here that often means a firm strip through the drive to the door as well as the ramp itself.

The village's historic core of stone cottages and farm buildings sits in a conservation area, where stone-coloured materials look right, while the later infill and estate housing has more flexibility over finish. We set gradients and landings for the person using the ramp and fit handrails where the rise needs them. Adaptations paid for through a Disabled Facilities Grant are built to the occupational therapist's recommendations, with the price worked to the council's schedule.

Greetham specifically

What makes Greetham different

Greetham sits on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which generally drains well, so on most plots a ramp's base and run-off are straightforward. Rutland's flood strategy does name Greetham among the places with a few properties at river flood risk, and on low-lying plots we give the drainage more thought so water is not directed to the door. In the conservation area, a stone-coloured finish keeps a new ramp from standing out against the cottages.

A typical Greetham job is a house with a gravel drive and a step at the front door. We lay a firm, non-slip route through or beside the gravel from the parking to the door, with a short ramp and a level landing at the threshold, and handrails where they are needed.

Accessible paths & ramps in Greetham: questions

How much does a ramp cost in Greetham?
A permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface in Greetham typically costs up to about £2,500 as a guide. A firm path through an old gravel drive, longer ramps and landings are priced on the visit. Grant work follows the council's schedule, and we set the fixed price out in writing.
Will a ramp need planning permission in Greetham?
Rarely. A ramp or path laid in the garden does not normally need planning permission; a raised one, or a platform, more than 30cm high can. Greetham has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction, so outside listed buildings the usual permitted development rules apply. Rutland County Council is responsible for planning.
Do we have to replace the whole gravel drive?
Not necessarily. A firm, edged path through or along the gravel can give a wheelchair a solid route to the door while keeping the rest of the drive as it is. If the drive is due for replacement anyway, doing both together is worth considering.
Our house is low-lying. Will a ramp cause water problems?
Greetham's limestone usually drains well, but low-lying plots are where the village's flood risk sits. We lay the landing to fall away from the door and plan where the run-off goes, so the ramp does not lead water to the threshold.
