Driveway gates in Kettering

Driveway gates in Kettering

Kettering has 26 conservation areas, including the town centre, Barton Seagrave, Burton Latimer, Geddington and Rothwell, and a scattering of site-specific Article 4 directions, mostly older ones covering individual streets or buildings. That patchwork means two houses a few roads apart can face different rules for a front gate. Before we design anything that faces the road, we check your address so you know where you stand. It takes a few minutes and saves an application you did not need, or a gate you should not have fitted.

The housing itself varies just as much. Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas near the centre and along the Headlands often have short frontages where every centimetre of swing matters. Interwar and post-war estates have more room. At Barton Seagrave and Hanwood Park, newer homes often have open fronts with no posts at all. We fit sliding and swing gates in aluminium and composite, automate them, and force-test every automated gate to the standard. Hanwood Park plots usually need posts built from new.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

Ground around Kettering ranges from ironstone to clay, and new-build plots at places like Hanwood Park are often compacted subsoil under a thin topsoil, which can give a post footing less support than it looks. We check what is under each garden before setting foundations. For the villas along the Headlands, a short drive can make sliding gates the better choice, since they need no swing space. Near the River Ise on the east of town, we keep motors and controls clear of the wettest ground.

A typical Kettering job is a Victorian or Edwardian villa along the Headlands with a short frontage, where the owners want to secure the drive without losing parking space to a gate's swing. That usually points to a single automated sliding gate running along the boundary, on a concrete foundation, with a keypad, a cable run from the house, and photocells and safety edges.

Questions

Driveway gates in Kettering: questions

What do electric gates cost in Kettering?
A Kettering sliding gate, new and automated, typically costs £5,000–£10,000 supplied and fitted; automated swing gates are typically £4,000–£8,000. Adding automation to your current gates is usually £1,500–£3,000. The power supply is priced on top, and we send a fixed written quote after checking the site and your address.
Will I need planning permission for gates in Kettering?
It depends on the address. Gates are normally permitted development up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles. But Kettering has 26 conservation areas and several site-specific Article 4 directions, and listed houses need care too, so we check your property before designing anything facing the road.
My Headlands drive is short. Will gates eat into parking?
Swing gates can, because the leaves need space to open inwards. Sliding gates suit short drives because they run along the boundary instead, leaving the drive clear. You need a clear stretch of wall or fence for the gate to park against, which we measure on the visit.
Is new-build ground at Hanwood Park strong enough for gate posts?
It can be, but we check rather than assume. New-build plots are often compacted subsoil under a thin topsoil, and the quality varies. We dig where the posts will go and size the footings for what we find and for the weight of the gate and motor.
