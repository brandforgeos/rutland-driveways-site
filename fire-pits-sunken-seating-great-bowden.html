Fire pits & sunken seating in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes and later housing on the edges. A fire pit or sunken seating area here has to suit that setting and be sited with real thought, because a stray spark matters more near a thatched roof. We build lined pits set into the patio and sunken seating with retaining walls, benches, steps and drainage. The prices on this page are guides; your fixed price follows in writing.

Older houses around the greens tend to have established gardens bounded by walls and hedges, while the later housing on the edges usually has open, regular plots. The village has its own neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, with policies on design and character, so materials that suit the houses around them are the sensible choice. We also cover Market Harborough, Little Bowden, Welham, Slawston and Foxton. Each design is drawn for its own plot.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped and part of Great Bowden Lane was affected. A sunken seating area dug into this ground will fill unless the water has a route out, so we design the drain or soakaway from a test hole before the retaining walls are built.

A typical Great Bowden job is a house on the edge of the village with a regular rear lawn. We set a shallow sunken area away from the house and any trees, build retaining walls on footings with drainage behind, face the benches in brick or an ironstone-toned material, and centre a lined fire pit, with the drain at the low point designed first.

You can check any address on the Environment Agency flood map.

Fire pits & sunken seating in Great Bowden: questions

How much does a fire pit cost in Great Bowden?
For Great Bowden, a built-in stone or brick fire pit typically costs £1,200–£2,000, and a fire pit with built-in seating from about £3,500, as a guide. A sunken seating area with retaining walls and drainage in Great Bowden is priced per job, usually as part of a wider garden build. On clay, the drainage is part of that price, which we confirm in writing.
Do I need planning permission in Great Bowden's conservation area?
In Great Bowden, a fire pit or sunken seating area in a rear garden does not normally need planning permission. Great Bowden has a conservation area covering the historic village and its greens, and a neighbourhood plan with design policies, so anything facing the road should respect the materials around it. Harborough District Council handles planning.
We have a thatched roof. Can we have a wood fire pit?
Fires should always be kept a safe distance from fences, trees and buildings, and with thatch that distance matters more than ever. On a smaller plot we may advise a gas pit instead, which is cleaner and turns on at a switch, with no burning wood. Any gas pit needs a Gas Safe engineer to install it.
Will a sunken area flood on Welland valley clay?
It will if it is not drained, because clay holds water and the sunken floor becomes the lowest point in the garden. We dig a test hole, then size a drain or soakaway for the ground, and if your plot cannot shed the water reliably we will recommend a level design instead.
