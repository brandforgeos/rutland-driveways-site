Driveway gates in Great Bowden

Driveway gates in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens just north east of Market Harborough, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes. Its conservation area covers the historic village and its greens, and its neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, has policies on design and character. A new front gate here should look as though it belongs, which usually means a simple design in a finish that sits with the walls behind it.

Later housing on the village edges has more conventional drives, often with room for swing leaves to open inwards. Our gates come in aluminium and composite, sliding or swing, with keypads, intercoms or fobs, and each automated gate is force-tested to the standard before handover. If you already have sound timber or metal gates, automating them is usually £1,500–£3,000 plus the power supply. Swing gates are usually the more natural look on a village frontage.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly and move with the seasons, so gate posts need footings designed for wet clay rather than a standard hole. The River Welland and the Langton Brook run to the south, and Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood affecting part of Great Bowden Lane, so properties on lower ground get controls mounted high. In the conservation area or on a listed house, road-facing gates can need permission from Harborough District Council.

A typical Great Bowden job is a brick or ironstone house facing one of the greens, where the owners want automated swing gates that look right in the street. It usually involves new posts on deep footings in the clay, gates in a simple style and finish to suit the house, a cable run from the house, a keypad or fobs, and photocells across the opening.

Driveway gates in Great Bowden: questions

What does an automated gate cost in Great Bowden?
Great Bowden guide prices: automated swing gates typically £4,000–£8,000 supplied and fitted, a new automated sliding gate £5,000–£10,000, and automating gates you already have usually £1,500–£3,000. The power supply goes on top. Footings for the clay and your chosen finish are included in the fixed price we confirm in writing after visiting.
Do I need planning permission for gates in Great Bowden?
Often not: permitted development normally allows gates up to 2m high, or 1m beside a road used by vehicles, so long as they never open outwards over the footway or road. In the conservation area or on a listed house, road-facing gates can need permission from Harborough District Council, and the neighbourhood plan's design policies are taken into account.
What gate style suits a house on the green?
Something simple that respects the ironstone, brick or thatch behind it. The neighbourhood plan asks road-facing changes to respect local character, so we suggest plain designs and finishes that sit with your walls, and bring samples to judge on site.
Does the slow-draining clay affect gates?
It does. Welland valley clay stays wet and moves between seasons, which can shift a shallow post and make leaves drop or catch. We dig on the visit and design footings deep and wide enough for the clay and the weight of the gate and motor.
