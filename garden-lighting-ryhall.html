Garden lighting in Ryhall

Garden lighting in Ryhall

Ryhall, with Belmesthorpe, sits on the River Gwash just north of Stamford. Much of the housing was built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders as Stamford's estates, so many gardens share a familiar shape: a patio off the back of the house, a lawn and borders, and a fence on three sides. That makes a modest, well-planned scheme very effective. We design low-voltage lighting with step and riser lights, uplighters on trees and walls, and soft light around seating. All prices on this page are guides.

Around the green and the church the older stone cottages have smaller, more enclosed gardens where a couple of wall lights and a lit shrub go a long way. On the estates, the most useful upgrade is often lighting built into a replacement patio, with ducts laid as it is built so cables never sit under slabs. Existing gardens can be lit too, using walls and beds, without lifting any paving at all.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Ryhall is on the limestone, which generally drains well, but the Gwash runs through the village and ground near the river is wetter. Rutland's flood strategy names Ryhall among the places with a few properties at river flood risk, so gardens near the river get wall and spike fittings, sealed ducts and controls mounted high. Ryhall is in Rutland, not South Kesteven, so Stamford's Article 4 direction does not apply: Ryhall has a conservation area over the historic village and no Article 4 direction.

A typical Ryhall job is for an estate semi or detached house with a standard rear garden. The scheme lights the patio step, puts two or three spike lights in the borders, adds a wall light over a bench or table and uplights one tree or shrub at the back fence. Everything runs from a single transformer on a dusk timer.

Questions

Garden lighting in Ryhall: questions

What does garden lighting cost in Ryhall?
A path, step and border scheme typically costs up to about £1,500 installed, which covers most estate gardens here. A full multi-zone garden scheme is £2,000–£6,000 or more, and a new outdoor circuit is priced separately. These are guide prices; we confirm a fixed price in writing after visiting.
Does Stamford's Article 4 direction cover Ryhall?
No. Ryhall is in Rutland, and has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction. Garden lighting does not need planning permission in any case. A new outdoor mains circuit is notifiable electrical work, done and certified by a registered electrician, and lights should point down and away from neighbours' windows.
What would a modest scheme include for a standard estate garden?
Usually a light on the step from the patio doors, a few spike lights in the borders, a soft wall light over seating and one uplit shrub or tree at the back. That gives the garden depth from the house without bright light spilling over the fences.
We are close to the Gwash. Does that rule out buried lights?
Not always, but on wetter ground near the river we usually prefer wall and spike fittings, which sit above the soil. Where a buried light is wanted, it is bedded on free-draining stone with a sealed duct, and the transformer stays high near the house.
