Glass verandas in Great Bowden

Glass verandas in Great Bowden

Great Bowden is a village of greens just north east of Market Harborough, with ironstone, brick and thatched houses around its greens and lanes and later housing on its edges. A veranda behind one of the older houses has to sit comfortably with walls and roofs that have been there a long time. That means a slim frame, a clear roof, a quiet colour and careful thought about where the wall plate can fix, especially below a low eaves line.

The village has its own neighbourhood plan, first made in 2018, with policies on design and character, and road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. A rear veranda is usually out of sight from the road, which helps, but we still design it as if a neighbour across the lane might see it. On the later houses at the edges of the village, the brief is simpler, and the choice is mostly about size and extras.

Great Bowden specifically

What makes Great Bowden different

Great Bowden sits on the clays and mudstones of the Welland valley, which drain slowly, with the River Welland and the Langton Brook to the south. Harborough's flood records note the April 1998 flood, when both overtopped and part of Great Bowden Lane was affected. For a veranda, that means we design for the water first: the downpipe needs a route the clay can cope with, whether a tested soakaway or a drain, and the post footings go down to firm ground.

A typical Great Bowden veranda is a 4m × 3m frame behind a brick or ironstone house in the village, or a later house on its edge, over a patio outside the back doors. It involves footings dug through the clay, a wall plate and flashing, a glass roof, a gutter and downpipe to a tested soakaway or drain, and a frame colour chosen to suit the house.

Glass verandas in Great Bowden: questions

How much is a veranda in Great Bowden?
Our guide is £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda, and £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m. Screens, lighting and heaters sit on top of those figures. Drainage for the valley clay and any fixing work on an older wall are included in the fixed price we send in writing.
Do I need planning permission for a veranda in Great Bowden?
Usually not for a veranda at the back of the house within the rear extension limits, provided it does not face a road and the house is not listed. Great Bowden's conservation area makes the rules tighter, and planning is handled by Harborough District Council, so we check your address before designing anything.
Can a veranda be fitted to a thatched cottage?
It needs careful thought. The wall plate fixes to the wall below the eaves, so the question is whether there is enough height and sound wall to fix to while keeping the roof well clear of the thatch. We look at that on the visit. If the cottage is listed, permitted development does not apply, so permission comes first.
Does the neighbourhood plan affect a rear veranda?
The plan has policies on design and character, and road-facing changes should respect the materials around them. A rear veranda is usually far less visible, but in the conservation area it still makes sense to keep the frame slim and the colours quiet, which is how we design them here anyway.
