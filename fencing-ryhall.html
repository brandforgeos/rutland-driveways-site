Fencing in Ryhall

Fencing in Ryhall

Ryhall and Belmesthorpe sit on the River Gwash just north of Stamford, with stone cottages around the green and the church, and later estate housing built by the same post-war and modern housebuilders that worked in Stamford. Those estate fences were often put up as cheaply as possible, and many owners now want something sturdier, more private and lower in maintenance. We cover Belmesthorpe on the same basis, from cottages to newer houses.

For an estate garden, composite or aluminium slatted fencing on aluminium posts is a popular upgrade, as it never needs painting and gives a modern finish. Closeboard timber on concrete posts with gravel boards remains the best value. Around the older stone cottages, natural timber usually sits better, and we can match gates and screens to whichever system you choose. Near the Gwash, gravel boards and well-set posts matter more, because the ground there stays wetter.

Ryhall specifically

What makes Ryhall different

Ryhall is on the limestone, which generally drains well and gives posts a firm footing. The Gwash runs through the village, though, and Rutland's flood strategy names Ryhall among the places with a few properties at river flood risk. On plots near the river we set posts in concrete at the right depth for wetter ground and keep all timber on gravel boards. The historic village is a conservation area with no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings.

A typical Ryhall job is an estate house with original panel fencing around the rear garden. We remove it, set aluminium or concrete posts, and fit composite slatted or closeboard panels, often with a matching side gate and a screen beside the patio. Plots near the Gwash get their gravel boards and posts set with particular care.

Fencing in Ryhall: questions

What does an estate fence upgrade cost in Ryhall?
Composite or aluminium slats cost from about £240 per metre fitted at 1.8m high, and closeboard £80–£100 per metre supplied and fitted on concrete posts. Taking away the original estate fence is priced on the visit, and we then send you a fixed written price.
Will my Ryhall fence need planning consent?
In most cases, no. Up to 2m high, or 1m beside a road or footpath used by vehicles, a fence is normally permitted development. The historic village is a conservation area without an Article 4 direction, so the usual limits apply there. Listed buildings need consent, and on newer estates we check for front boundary planning conditions.
Is composite fencing worth it over timber on an estate garden?
Closeboard on concrete posts is better value and lasts well. Composite and aluminium slatted fencing costs more but never needs painting and stays looking new, which many owners prefer if they plan to stay.
We are close to the Gwash. Will a fence cope?
Yes, if it is built for it. Ground near the river is wetter, so we keep timber off the soil with gravel boards and set posts in concrete at the depth the ground needs.
