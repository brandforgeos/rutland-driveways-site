Accessible paths & ramps in Ketton

Accessible paths & ramps in Ketton

Ketton's later 20th-century houses and bungalows are often where level access is needed first, and a bungalow with a step or two at the front and back door is a straightforward place to start. We build permanent concrete and paved access ramps, landings and widened paths so wheelchairs, walking frames and pushchairs can get to the door, and we work across Tinwell, Collyweston, Barrowden and Tixover as well as the village itself.

In the older streets, built of the cream oolitic Ketton stone and often roofed in Collyweston slate, the brief changes. A grey concrete ramp against a cream stone frontage looks out of place, so in the conservation area we use buff and natural tones that sit with the stone. Grant-funded work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations and is priced to the council's schedule, so the finish never comes at the expense of the function.

Ketton specifically

What makes Ketton different

Ketton is on the Lincolnshire Limestone, which usually drains well and gives a ramp a firm base. Close to the River Chater, which runs through the village to meet the Welland, the ground is a different matter, and the Welland flood warning area at Ketton is the only one in Rutland. We test before designing a soakaway, and on lower plots we make sure the ramp's run-off is not just moved to a new low spot by the door.

A typical Ketton job is a bungalow or later 20th-century house with a step at the front door, where we build a ramp from the drive with a level landing and a handrail. In the older core, it is more often a shorter ramp or graded path to a stone cottage door, finished in buff tones.

Permissions in Ketton

  1. PlanningA ramp or path in the garden does not normally need planning permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. We build to the gradients and landings in Approved Document M, and Disabled Facilities Grant work follows the occupational therapist's recommendations. Listed buildings and the Ketton conservation area have tighter rules, so we check your address before anything is designed.
  2. Disabled Facilities GrantGrants are run by Rutland County Council. You apply to the council, an occupational therapist assesses what is needed, and the grant can cover up to £30,000 in England. We price to the council's schedule and work to the OT's report.
  3. Design and priceWe measure up, check the ground and the planning position, and give you one fixed written price.

Questions

Accessible paths & ramps in Ketton: questions

How much does a ramp cost in Ketton?
The guide price for a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface is typically up to about £2,500. A buff or natural finish to suit Ketton stone, longer ramps and landings are priced on the visit. Grant work is costed to the council's schedule, and your fixed price comes in writing afterwards.
Do I need planning permission for a ramp in Ketton?
In most cases no. Planning permission is not normally needed for a garden ramp or path, though a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can need it. Ketton has a conservation area but no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings, within the usual conservation area limits. Rutland County Council is the planning authority.
Can a ramp match a Ketton stone house?
It can look right beside one. Ketton stone frontages call for buff and natural tones rather than grey, so we choose the finish and edging to suit, while keeping the surface non-slip and the gradient set for the person using it.
We live near the Chater. Is that a problem for a ramp?
Not if it is planned. Ground near the river does not drain like the limestone higher up, so we test it and design where the water goes, rather than relying on a soakaway that may sit in wet ground.
