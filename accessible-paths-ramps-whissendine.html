Accessible paths & ramps in Whissendine

Accessible paths & ramps in Whissendine

Whissendine has a long main street of stone and brick houses, farm conversions and newer homes, and bigger gardens and longer drives are common. For someone who needs level access, that length cuts both ways: there is room for a gentle ramp, but there is also a long route from the car to the door that has to be firm and safe. We build permanent concrete and paved ramps, level landings and widened paths to cover both.

Whissendine is not one of Rutland's conservation areas and has no Article 4 direction, so outside listed buildings the normal permitted development rules apply. That leaves more freedom over finish and layout than in many nearby villages, including Langham next door. The gradient and the landings are set out around whoever will use the ramp day to day, and Disabled Facilities Grant adaptations are built to the occupational therapist's brief and the council's price schedule.

Whissendine specifically

What makes Whissendine different

Whissendine is on the clay of the Vale, and the Whissendine Brook runs through the village. The ground drains slowly, and Rutland's flood strategy records that Main Street floods and becomes impassable. A ramp is a long, hard, sloping surface, so on this clay it can easily feed water to a threshold that used to be protected by a step. We design the base and the water route first, laying the landing away from the door and adding channels where needed.

A typical Whissendine job is a house or farm conversion with a long drive and a step at the door. It usually means a firm, widened path from the parking, a ramp with a level landing at the door, handrails where the rise needs them, and a drainage channel to take water away.

Sources: Rutland flood risk strategy.

Accessible paths & ramps in Whissendine: questions

What does a ramp cost in Whissendine?
Typically up to about £2,500 covers a permanent concrete ramp with handrails and a non-slip surface, as a guide. Whissendine's long drives often mean a widened path as well, and that, along with extra landings and drainage channels, is priced on the visit. Any grant-funded adaptation is priced to the council's schedule.
Do I need planning permission for a ramp in Whissendine?
Probably not. Whissendine is not a conservation area and has no Article 4 direction, so normal permitted development applies outside listed buildings. A garden ramp or path does not normally need permission, but a raised ramp or platform more than 30cm high can. Rutland County Council would handle any application.
We are on Main Street, which floods. Is a ramp a good idea?
It can be, but the step at your door may be keeping water out. We lay the landing to fall away from the threshold and plan a channel or route for the water, so the ramp improves access without inviting the street's water to the door.
Can a long drive be made wheelchair friendly?
That is possible. On Whissendine's longer plots we often lay a firm, widened path from the parking to the ramp, with a non-slip finish and falls that shed water, so the whole route is usable, not only the last step.
