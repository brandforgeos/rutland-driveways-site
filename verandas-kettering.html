Glass verandas in Kettering

Glass verandas in Kettering

Kettering covers a wide range of houses in a small area. Close to the centre and along the Headlands are Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas; beyond them, interwar and post-war estates; then Barton Seagrave to the south east and Hanwood Park on the east side, where thousands of new homes are planned. A veranda works on all of them, but the fixing, the size and the paperwork differ from one to the next.

The build itself is consistent. A powder-coated aluminium frame carries a toughened glass or polycarbonate roof. A wall plate fixed and flashed into the house takes the back edge, posts on footings take the front, and a gutter in the front beam feeds a downpipe. Glass looks better and is quieter in the rain; polycarbonate is lighter and cheaper. Both keep you dry, so the choice comes down to budget and taste.

Kettering specifically

What makes Kettering different

Ground around Kettering ranges from ironstone to clay, and on new-build plots at Hanwood Park and elsewhere it is often compacted subsoil under a thin layer of topsoil. That layer can look firm but does not drain, so a downpipe left to discharge onto the lawn creates a wet patch. We check what is under each garden, set the post footings into ground that will carry them, and give the roof water a soakaway or drain connection. Near the River Ise on the east of town, we look at the water route especially closely.

A typical Kettering veranda is a 4m × 3m frame over the patio of an interwar semi or a new Hanwood Park house, or a wider 6m frame along the back of a Victorian villa. The work includes post footings, the wall plate and flashing, a glass or polycarbonate roof, the downpipe and its soakaway, with LED lighting in the frame if wanted.

Questions

Glass verandas in Kettering: questions

How much does a veranda cost in Kettering?
A guide figure for a 4m × 3m aluminium and glass veranda is £9,000–£14,000 supplied and fitted, rising to £15,000–£22,000 for a 6m × 3m. Side screens, lighting and heaters are added on top. Once we have seen the house and the ground, we send a fixed price in writing.
Do Kettering's Article 4 directions affect a veranda?
Most do not, because they are site-specific, mostly older ones covering individual streets or buildings. A rear veranda is usually permitted development within the rear extension limits if it does not face a road and the house is not listed. Inside one of Kettering's 26 conservation areas the rules are tighter, so we check your address before designing.
Why do new-build gardens at Hanwood Park need extra drainage work?
Because the ground under the turf is often compacted subsoil with only a thin layer of topsoil on top. Rain from a veranda roof arrives in one place, at the downpipe, and compacted ground cannot take it away. We dig to see what is there and design a soakaway or drain connection that suits it.
Can a veranda go on the back of a Victorian villa?
Yes. Older brick walls take a wall plate well once we have found sound brickwork and the right height above doors and windows. A taller back wall can allow a better roof fall, and a 6m frame can suit a wide villa. If the house is in the town centre conservation area, we check the rules first.
