Driveways in Oakham

Driveways for every kind of Oakham house

Oakham is our home patch. From ironstone cottages near the castle to the new family homes at Barleythorpe and Oakham Heights, we have built driveways on most streets in town — resin, block paving, tarmac and gravel, each chosen to suit the house in front of it.

Driveways for every era of Oakham

The old town. Around the castle, All Saints and the market place, Oakham's oldest streets mix limestone and warm ironstone under Collyweston and slate roofs. Frontages are small and the setting is sensitive: bound gravel, buff resin and natural-toned blocks keep new work in keeping. Access down the old yards and lanes takes planning, which we factor in from the first visit.

Victorian and interwar Oakham. The terraces and semis off Brooke Road, Braunston Road and Burley Road are classic front-garden-to-parking conversions: dropped kerb applications through the highways authority, levels worked out around bay windows and steps, and permeable surfacing so drainage rules never become a problem.

New Oakham. Barleythorpe and the growing developments north of town come with builder-grade drives that owners often replace within a few years — usually widening to two cars and upgrading tarmac or thin block to a properly based resin or block surface that matches the spec of the house.

The ground around Oakham

Much of the Vale of Catmose sits on heavy clay that holds winter water. That makes base depth and drainage design — soakaways, channels, permeable construction — the difference between a drive that stays flat and one that heaves. We build for the ground we actually dig, and our quotes say exactly what is going underneath.

Villages we serve around Oakham

We cover Oakham and the surrounding Vale of Catmose, where clay ground makes proper drainage essential. Villages we regularly work in include Langham, Braunston, Egleton, Burley, Ashwell, Whissendine, Cottesmore and Greetham.

Oakham specifically

Ground, drainage and conservation in Oakham

Oakham sits in the Vale of Catmose, and the ground around much of the town is heavy clay that holds water through the winter. Base depth and drainage are where Oakham drives succeed or fail, so we dig a test hole and design the soakaway, channel or permeable build-up on the visit.

The Oakham conservation area covers the historic centre, but Oakham has no Article 4 direction, so outside listed buildings the normal permitted development rules apply, with the usual conservation area limits.

Oakham is listed in Rutland County Council's flood strategy as a surface water flood risk area of national significance, and the Barleythorpe Brook runs culverted under parts of the town. Water that has nowhere to go is the commonest problem we fix here.

Questions

Driveways in Oakham: questions

How quickly can you visit?
We are based in Rutland, so Oakham visits are usually within a few days — often sooner.
Which surface do you recommend for the new estates?
For most modern homes it is between resin and block. Resin gives a seamless upgrade over builder-grade finishes; block suits houses with strong brick detailing. We will quote either or both.
How much does a new driveway cost in Oakham?
For a typical Oakham drive of 35–60m², resin bound costs roughly £5,250–£13,250, block paving £4,250–£10,250 and tarmac £3,250–£7,750, all in.
How do I get a dropped kerb in Oakham?
You apply to Rutland County Council: £194 for a Section 184 vehicle access permit (one or two homes). It is built by an approved contractor with street works accreditation and the right insurance, working under the council's permit. See dropped kerbs in Oakham.
Do I need planning permission for a driveway in Oakham?
A permeable drive, or one that drains to your own lawn or border, needs no planning permission; more than 5m² of impermeable surface draining to the road does.

Planning a driveway in Oakham?
