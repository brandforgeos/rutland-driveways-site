Driveway gates in Market Harborough

Driveway gates in Market Harborough

Market Harborough sits where the River Welland meets the River Jordan, on Jurassic mudstones and clays that drain slowly. The town centre flooded from surface water in September 2024, and more than 50 properties flooded in July 2013. For a driveway gate, that matters in two places: the post foundations, which have to stay put in wet clay, and the motors and control box, which should never be where water pools at the entrance.

Housing ranges from the historic core around the Square and the Old Grammar School, through Victorian and Edwardian streets out to Little Bowden and Great Bowden, to later estates and the large Airfield Farm development near Lubenham. We fit sliding and swing gates in aluminium and composite to suit each of these, with keypads, intercoms or remote fobs, and we force-test every automated gate to the standard after installation. On newer Airfield Farm homes, the fronts are usually open, so posts are built from new.

Market Harborough specifically

What makes Market Harborough different

On Market Harborough's mudstone and clay, a gate post needs a deeper, wider footing than it would on free-draining ground, and a sliding gate's foundation must not trap water against the track. We test the ground before designing either. Planning also has local wrinkles: the town core has been a conservation area since 1969, and Lubenham and Great Bowden have their own. An Article 4 direction served in 2020 on land at Queen's Street, Little Bowden, specifically covers gates, fences and walls, so road-facing gates there need checking.

A typical Market Harborough job is an Edwardian or later house on the roads towards Little Bowden, or a newer home at Airfield Farm, where the owners want a secure, automated entrance. It usually means new posts on deep footings in clay, a pair of swing gates or a single sliding gate, a cable run from the house, and photocells across the opening.

Questions

Driveway gates in Market Harborough: questions

What does an electric gate cost in Market Harborough?
Automated swing gates in Market Harborough typically run £4,000–£8,000 supplied and fitted, and a new automated sliding gate £5,000–£10,000. Automating gates you already have is usually £1,500–£3,000. Add the power supply on top. Deeper footings for clay are designed into the fixed price we put in writing after visiting.
Do I need permission for gates in Market Harborough?
For most homes, gates are permitted development up to 2m high, or 1m next to a road used by vehicles, and must not open outwards over the footway or road. In the town's conservation areas or on a listed house, road-facing gates can need permission from Harborough District Council, and the Article 4 direction at Queen's Street, Little Bowden covers gates.
Our street flooded in 2024. Is an automated gate a bad idea?
Not necessarily, but it changes the design. We keep the control box and connections above the likely water level, avoid putting a motor at the lowest point, and look at whether a sliding gate's track would sit in water. Sorting drainage across the entrance at the same time is often worth considering.
Do clay soils affect gate posts?
They do. Slow-draining clay stays wet and can move with the seasons, and a gate post that shifts will make leaves drop or bind. We dig on the visit to see what is there, then size the footings for the ground and the weight of the gate and motor.
